\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=forestbg, colframe=forest, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students read a \textbf{piecewise-defined function} as \emph{one}
    function built from different rules on different pieces of its domain. They \textbf{evaluate}
    it by first choosing the piece the input belongs to --- and at a \textbf{boundary}, the piece
    whose inequality \emph{includes} the input. They write the absolute value function as a
    two-piece linear rule, so Lesson~1.3's V is the first piecewise function they already know.
    They read a pre-drawn piecewise graph using \textbf{open} and \textbf{closed} endpoints, decide
    whether the pieces \emph{meet} at a boundary or \emph{jump} (a \textbf{discontinuity}), and
    name increasing, decreasing, and \textbf{constant} intervals. They evaluate the
    \textbf{greatest-integer (step) function} $\lfloor x\rfloor$ --- rounding \emph{down}, including
    for negatives --- and read its staircase.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} \textbf{A2.F.2} --- investigate and analyze the
    characteristics of \emph{piecewise-defined functions} algebraically and graphically: domain,
    range, zeros, and intercepts, \textbf{including graphs with discontinuities}
    (\textbf{A2.F.2a}); compare and contrast characteristics of piecewise-defined functions with
    the other families (\textbf{A2.F.2b}); increasing, decreasing, or \textbf{constant} intervals
    (\textbf{A2.F.2c}); and, for any $x$ in the domain, determine $f(x)$ from a graph or equation
    and explain its meaning in context (\textbf{A2.F.2f}). Absolute value (1.3) and the
    greatest-integer function are the two named entry examples of the piecewise family.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Lesson model:} \emph{Gradual release} --- I do, we do, you do alone. A warm-up
    activates prior knowledge; \textbf{Guided Notes} build the vocabulary with the class on one
    worked context (a streaming service, free for three months) and close with a \emph{Guided
    Practice} box (the ``we do''); an \textbf{Individual Practice} block on the same page is the
    ``you do'' --- three problems, worked silently and alone; a \textbf{debrief} consolidates and
    surfaces the errors; \textbf{homework} extends the practice. \emph{There is no group activity
    in this lesson} --- today's independent work is done individually.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Ideas \& Skills} & \textbf{Key Understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Read brace notation: one function, several rules, each with the inputs it is for.
  \item \textbf{Evaluate} by choosing the piece first --- and at a boundary, the piece whose
        inequality includes the input.
  \item Write $|x|$ and $a|x-h|+k$ as a two-piece linear rule.
  \item Read a piecewise graph: open vs.\ closed dots; \emph{meets} or \emph{jumps}.
  \item Name increasing, decreasing, and \textbf{constant} intervals.
  \item Evaluate $\lfloor x\rfloor$ (round \emph{down}) and read the staircase.
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item A function can follow one rule here and another there and still be \emph{one}
        input-to-output machine --- the condition is a filter that sends each input to exactly
        one rule.
  \item \textbf{Target misconception:} that a boundary input belongs to ``both'' pieces, or to
        whichever piece the story makes sound right. Exactly one piece owns it --- the one whose
        inequality includes it --- and the closed dot shows which.
  \item Pieces \emph{meet} only when both rules agree at the boundary; a jump is allowed, and it
        is what A2.F.2a means by a discontinuity.
  \item A step function is \emph{constant} on each interval, then jumps; ``rounding down'' is a
        step function.
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Piecewise-defined function} & One function given by different rules on different parts of its domain, written with a brace. \\
\textbf{Boundary (breakpoint)} & An $x$-value where the rule changes; the piece whose inequality includes it ($\le$, $\ge$) owns it. \\
\textbf{Open / closed endpoint} & Open $\circ$ excludes the point ($<$, $>$); closed $\bullet$ includes it ($\le$, $\ge$). \\
\textbf{Continuous at a boundary} & Both rules give the \emph{same} value there, so the pieces meet with no gap. \\
\textbf{Discontinuity (jump)} & The two rules disagree at the boundary, so the graph jumps. \\
\textbf{Greatest-integer function} & $\lfloor x\rfloor=$ the greatest integer $\le x$ --- rounds \emph{down}: $\lfloor 3.7\rfloor=3$, $\lfloor-2.5\rfloor=-3$. \\
\textbf{Step function} & A piecewise function that is \emph{constant} on each interval, then jumps; its graph is a staircase. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{fixedskillbox}[Warm-Up --- Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review]{forestbg}
The Warm-Up rehearses exactly what today leans on. \textbf{(1)} Evaluating two linear rules at
given inputs is the mechanical skill every \emph{piece} needs --- a piecewise function is just
several of these, each with a condition. \textbf{(2)} The V as two lines, $y=x$ for $x\ge0$ and
$y=-x$ for $x<0$, with the check $|-6|=-(-6)$, is the whole bridge from Lesson~1.3 --- the first
piecewise function they already know. \textbf{(3)} Open vs.\ closed dots and $<$ vs.\ $\le$, ending
with the question that carries the lesson: \emph{if one rule is for $x<1$ and another for $x\ge1$,
which rule does $x=1$ use?} \textbf{Debrief item 3 aloud, briefly}, and land only this: \emph{$x=1$
fails $x<1$ and satisfies $x\le1$, so it belongs to exactly one rule --- the second. Hold on to
that.} \textbf{This page is set at 12pt} --- larger type than the rest of the packet, deliberately.
\end{fixedskillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{forestbg}
    ``A streaming service is free for the first 3 months, then \$12 a month for every month after
    that.'' Ask: \textbf{``How much have you paid in total after 2 months? After 3? After 5?''}
    ($\$0$, $\$0$, $\$24$.) \textbf{``Could one straight line describe the total for every
    month?''} (No --- it is flat, then rises.) \textbf{``So how many rules does the cost follow?''}
    (Two.) Write the brace on the board, $C(m)=0$ for $0\le m\le3$ and $12(m-3)$ for $m>3$, and
    say: \emph{one function, two rules --- a piecewise-defined function. Today: which rule does an
    input use, what does the graph look like where the rule changes, and what happens when a rule
    ``rounds''?}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Guided Notes --- the Lesson (20 min)]{forestbg}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{1. One function, several rules.} Read the brace for $C(m)$: each rule comes with the
inputs it is \emph{for}. Evaluate by \emph{choosing the piece first}: $C(2)=0$, $C(3)=0$
(the boundary --- $3$ satisfies $0\le m\le3$, so month $3$ is still free), and $C(5)=24$ in the
first \texttt{work} block. State the boundary rule, then the storyless $f(x)$ with $f(2)$ going
to the $\ge$ piece.

\textbf{2. Absolute value is piecewise.} $|x|=x$ for $x\ge0$ and $-x$ for $x<0$; check $|-3|$.
Then the trail from 1.3, $H(x)=2|x-3|+1$, rewritten as $2x-5$ for $x\ge3$ and $7-2x$ for $x<3$ in
two side-by-side \texttt{work} blocks. Both rules give $H(3)=1$, so the pieces \emph{meet} at the
vertex --- the first ``meets'' example, before the contrast.

\columnbreak
\textbf{3. Open or closed, meets or jumps.} The two-column table on the \emph{same} story: total
paid $C(m)$ meets at $m=3$ (both rules give $0$; constant on $[0,3]$, increasing after), while the
price-this-month $P(m)$ jumps (rules give $0$ and $12$; range $\{0,12\}$). Then the gold caution:
a classmate reads $P(3)=12$ ``because the price is \$12 after the free months'' --- but $3$
satisfies $0\le m\le3$, so $P(3)=0$, and the closed dot at $(3,0)$ says so. \emph{Exactly one piece
owns the boundary.} This is the target misconception, and this box is where it dies.

\textbf{4. Step functions.} $P(m)$ is constant on each piece, then jumps --- a step function. The
greatest-integer function rounds \emph{down}: $\lfloor3.7\rfloor=3$, $\lfloor-2.5\rfloor=-3$
(down means more negative). Read the staircase: constant $n$ on $[n,n+1)$, closed left, open
right. Age in whole years and full hours parked are the two real examples.

\textbf{Guided Practice (the ``we do'').} $p(x)=-2x$ for $x\le-1$, $x+3$ for $x>-1$: evaluate
four inputs including the boundary, decide meets-or-jumps (both give $2$: meets), then read
decreasing/increasing intervals and the minimum off the pre-drawn graph. Do this one \emph{with}
the class --- it is the last thing they see before working alone.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Practice --- \emph{On Your Own} (15 min, silent)]{redbox}
\textbf{Launch (1 min):} ``Same moves you just watched --- now nobody helps you. Three problems.
Find the piece first, then apply the rule; at a boundary, ask which inequality \emph{includes} the
input. Stuck? Look up the page at the notes, not at your neighbour.'' The block sits on the last
page of the Guided Notes, so nothing new is handed out.
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{What students do.} \textbf{Problem~1} evaluates $h(x)$ across a boundary that
\emph{jumps}: $h(0)=1$, $h(2)=-1$ (the $\le$ piece owns $2$), $h(5)=4$; then the meets-or-jumps
test --- rules give $-1$ and $-2$, so $h$ is \emph{not} continuous at $2$. \textbf{Problem~2}
runs backwards from a pre-drawn graph with a jump at $x=2$: domain $[-2,5]$, $f(1)=1$, $f(2)=-2$
read off the \emph{closed} dot, increasing on $[-2,2)$, constant on $[2,5]$, not continuous ---
and then they \emph{write} the rule, $x$ on $[-2,2)$ and $-2$ on $[2,5]$. \textbf{Problem~3} is
two pieces and the staircase: $|x-3|$ as $x-3$ for $x\ge3$ and $3-x$ for $x<3$ in \texttt{work}
blocks, then $\lfloor2.9\rfloor$, $\lfloor-1.2\rfloor$ (the trap: $-2$), $\lfloor4\rfloor$, and
the interval $[4,5)$ where $\lfloor x\rfloor=4$.

\columnbreak
\textbf{What the teacher does.} Circulate the whole 15 minutes; keep it silent. Give a
\emph{question, cue, or prompt}, never an answer:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep]
  \item 1: ``Does $2$ satisfy $x\le2$? Does it satisfy $x>2$? So which rule gets it?''
  \item 1: ``You found $h(2)$. Now what does the \emph{other} rule give at $2$? Same number?''
  \item 2: ``Two dots at $x=2$. Which one is filled in? That is the one $f(2)$ reads.''
  \item 2: ``What is the rule on the flat part? On the slanted part, what line is that?''
  \item 3: ``$-1.2$ --- which integer is just \emph{below} it on the number line?''
  \item Finished early: ``In problem~1, change $2x-6$ to $2x-5$. Does $h$ meet at $2$ now?''
\end{itemize}
Note names as you go: one student for each of the three problems, to put work on the board in the
debrief.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Debrief (10 min --- what goes on the board, in a second color)]{forestbg}
Students correct their own Individual Practice in a second colour as you go. Work all three, but
protect the time for items~2 and~3.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em, nosep, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Problem~1 --- the piece first, then the rule.} Put the three evaluations up
        with the \emph{reason} beside each: $0\le2$ so $-0+1=1$; $2\le2$ so $-2+1=-1$; $5>2$ so
        $10-6=4$. Ask who used $2x-6$ at $x=2$, and what inequality they would need for that.
  \item \textbf{The must-land moment: exactly one piece owns the boundary.} Draw problem~2's
        graph on the board and put a finger on the two dots at $x=2$. The filled one is
        $f(2)=-2$; the open one is where the slanted piece \emph{stops without} its endpoint. Point
        back at $P(3)=0$ from the notes' caution box. Say it out loud: \emph{the story cannot
        decide it; the inequality does.}
  \item \textbf{Meets or jumps --- one test.} Beside problem~1, write both rules' values at
        $x=2$ ($-1$ and $-2$) and beside problem~2 the two dots' heights ($2$ and $-2$). Same
        value means meet; different means jump. Contrast with the Guided Practice, where both
        rules gave $2$ and the graph is one connected piece.
  \item \textbf{Problem~3 --- two pieces, and rounding down.} $|x-3|$ is $x-3$ on the right and
        $3-x$ on the left --- the V's two rays. Then $\lfloor-1.2\rfloor=-2$: down means
        \emph{more negative}. Ask what $\lfloor x\rfloor$ does on $[4,5)$ (nothing --- constant).
\end{enumerate}
If time is short, cut item~4's floor discussion and protect items~2 and~3.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring --- Watch For]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{giving a boundary input to the strict-$<$ piece}, or to ``both'' (notes~1,
        Individual Practice~1, homework~1 and~6) --- the lesson's target misconception. Send them
        to the inequality, not to the story;
  \item reading the \emph{open} dot as the value at a breakpoint (Individual Practice~2,
        homework~3);
  \item calling every piecewise graph ``discontinuous'' --- pieces \emph{can} meet; check whether
        the two rules agree there (notes~2, Guided Practice, homework~2);
  \item greatest-integer sign error: rounding \emph{toward zero}, so $\lfloor-1.2\rfloor=-1$
        (Individual Practice~3, homework~4);
  \item saying a step function is ``increasing'' on a step --- it is \textbf{constant} there, then
        jumps (notes~4);
  \item dropping the condition when writing $|x-3|$ as pieces, or writing $x-3$ for \emph{both}
        pieces (Individual Practice~3);
  \item in homework~5(c), not seeing that a surcharge opens a gap: the pieces meet only if both
        rules give \$30 at $5$ GB.
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts: ``Which piece owns this input --- how do you know?'' ``Open or closed here, and
what inequality does that show?'' ``Do the two rules agree at the boundary?'' ``Round down ---
which integer is just below?''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Item~1 is the mechanical core: each piece of a piecewise function is a rule you evaluate, so plugging
in has to be automatic --- including into $5-x$ with a negative input. Item~2 is the whole bridge
from Lesson~1.3: the V is literally two linear rules joined at the vertex, and the check
$|-6|=-(-6)=6$ is the one that convinces students the ``$-x$'' piece is not negative. Item~3's last
bullet is the make-or-break idea for today: $x=1$ fails $x<1$ and satisfies $x\le1$, so it belongs
to \emph{exactly one} rule. Debrief that one sentence and leave it --- section~1's boundary rule
and section~3's caution box pay it off. \textbf{This page is set at 12pt} --- larger type than the
rest of the packet, deliberately. It is still one page; if you add to it, check that it stays one
page.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
Twenty minutes, paced by section: 3 for the vocabulary and hook, 4 for section~1, 4 for
section~2, 5 for section~3, 2 for section~4, and 2 for the Guided Practice box worked \emph{with}
the class. \textbf{Keep the whole notes block on one context} --- the streaming service --- so
``which piece owns month~3'' is doing the work in every section. Section~3 is the one to protect:
the two-column table puts \emph{meets} ($C$) beside \emph{jumps} ($P$) on the same story, and the
gold caution's $P(3)=0$ --- month~3 is still free, the closed dot says so --- is the concrete case
that makes ``exactly one piece owns the boundary'' undeniable. Three \texttt{work} blocks are
printed in the numbered sections ($C(5)$ and the two halves of the trail) plus two more in
Individual Practice~3; students write in the reserved space while the key prints the same steps
there, so the two files paginate alike. If you fall behind, compress section~4 to the four floor
values and the ``down means more negative'' sentence --- but do \emph{not} cut section~2's
``both rules give $H(3)=1$, so they meet'': it is the first meets example, and section~3's
contrast needs it.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Individual Practice]
Fifteen minutes, and \textbf{keep it silent} --- this is the only block all period where a student
finds out what they can do without help. Budget roughly 5 minutes each. The three problems are
deliberately the three \emph{directions} of the skill: rule $\to$ values (across a boundary that
jumps), graph $\to$ values and then $\to$ rule, and the two entry examples (the V as pieces, and
the staircase). A student who evaluates problem~1 correctly but writes $f(2)=2$ in problem~2 is
reading the open dot --- send them to the caution box's $P(3)$, not to a rule. Watch for
$h(2)=-2$ in problem~1 (the strict piece) and $\lfloor-1.2\rfloor=-1$ in problem~3 (rounding
toward zero). Fast finishers change problem~1's second rule to $2x-5$ and report whether $h$ now
meets at $2$ (yes: both give $-1$); that is the work you want on the board in the debrief.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
Item~1's last blank is the boundary check --- ``the first, because $-1\le-1$'' is the whole idea in
six words; expect $f(-1)=-2$ from anyone who used the strict piece. Item~2 is the meets-or-jumps
pair in the wild: $g$ jumps ($3$ vs.\ $4$) and $k$ meets ($3$ vs.\ $3$), and the one-sentence
explanation should say ``same value at the boundary.'' Item~4 has the two rounding traps
($\lfloor-3.1\rfloor=-4$, $\lfloor-0.5\rfloor=-1$) and the boundary question --- $\lfloor x\rfloor=x$
exactly at the integers, and never above $x$. Item~5(c) is the concept check: a \$10 surcharge
makes the second piece $40+10(g-5)$, which gives \$40 at $5$~GB against the first piece's \$30 ---
a jump. Item~6 is the formative check: sort into ``chose B (got it) / chose A (used the strict-$<$
piece --- the target misconception, still alive) / chose C (arithmetic) / chose D (thinks a boundary
belongs to no one)'' to decide whether Lesson~1.5 opens with a one-minute re-look at ``which
inequality includes it.'' One \texttt{work} block (item~5b). With the group activity cut, the
last 10 minutes are for \emph{starting} this page in class --- launch item~1 aloud, then
circulate; the rest is due next class.
\end{teachernote}

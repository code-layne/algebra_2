\begin{hookbox}
A streaming service is \emph{free for the first 3 months}, then costs \emph{\$12 a month} for
every month after that. Let $C(m)$ be the \emph{total} you have paid after $m$ months.
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item Total paid after $2$ months: \blank{1.2cm} \quad after $3$ months: \blank{1.2cm} \quad
        after $5$ months: \blank{1.2cm}
  \item Could one straight line describe the total for \emph{every} month? \blank{1.0cm} \;
        How many different rules does the cost follow? \blank{1.0cm}
\end{itemize}
One function, two rules --- one for the free months and another after them. That is a
\textbf{piecewise-defined function}, and today we learn to evaluate them, read their graphs, and
meet the step function that ``rounds.''
\end{hookbox}

\boxguard[24]
\begin{notesbox}{1. One Function, Several Rules --- Evaluate by Choosing the Piece}
A \textbf{piecewise-defined function} uses different rules on different parts of its domain,
written with a brace. Each rule comes with the inputs it is \emph{for}. For the service:
\[ C(m)=\begin{cases} 0, & 0\le m\le 3 \\ 12(m-3), & m>3 \end{cases} \]
\textbf{To evaluate: first pick the piece, then apply its rule.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item $C(2)$: is $2\le3$? \blank{0.8cm} \; so use the \blank{1.2cm} piece: $C(2)=$ \blank{0.9cm}
  \item $C(3)$: the boundary. $3$ satisfies $0\le m\le3$ (the $\le$ includes it), so $C(3)=$
        \blank{0.9cm} --- month $3$ is still free.
  \item $C(5)$: is $5>3$? \blank{0.8cm} \; so use the second piece:
        \begin{work}
          C(5) &= 12(5-3) \\
               &= 12(2) \\
               &= 24
        \end{work}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Boundary rule:} an input on the boundary belongs to the piece whose inequality
\emph{includes} it --- the one with $\le$ or $\ge$, never the strict one. \\[2pt]
\textbf{Try one with no story.} $f(x)=\begin{cases} 2x+1, & x<2 \\ 5-x, & x\ge2 \end{cases}$
\quad $f(0)=$ \blank{0.9cm} \quad $f(2)=$ \blank{0.9cm} (which piece? \blank{1.4cm}) \quad
$f(5)=$ \blank{0.9cm}
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[24]
\begin{notesbox}{2. Absolute Value Is Piecewise --- the V Is Two Pieces}
Lesson 1.3's V is two lines pasted at the vertex, and now we can \emph{write} it that way. When
$x\ge0$, the bars change nothing: $|x|=x$. When $x<0$, the bars flip the sign: $|x|=-x$.
\[ |x|=\begin{cases} \text{\blank{1.4cm}}, & x\ge0 \\[2pt] \text{\blank{1.4cm}}, & x<0 \end{cases} \]
\vspace{-4pt}
\begin{center}Check: $|-3| = -(-3) =$ \blank{0.8cm}\end{center}
\textbf{Worked: the trail from Lesson 1.3,} $H(x)=2|x-3|+1$. The vertex $x=3$ is the boundary.
\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.47\linewidth}
For $x\ge3$, $|x-3|=x-3$:
\begin{work}
  H(x) &= 2(x-3) + 1 \\
       &= 2x - 6 + 1 \\
       &= 2x - 5
\end{work}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.47\linewidth}
For $x<3$, $|x-3|=-(x-3)=3-x$:
\begin{work}
  H(x) &= 2(3-x) + 1 \\
       &= 6 - 2x + 1 \\
       &= 7 - 2x
\end{work}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}
So $H(x)=\begin{cases} 2x-5, & x\ge3 \\ 7-2x, & x<3 \end{cases}$ --- the right ray has slope
\blank{0.8cm} and the left ray has slope \blank{0.8cm}, and at the boundary both rules give
$H(3)=$ \blank{0.8cm}, so the two pieces \blank{1.2cm} (meet / jump) at the vertex.
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[28]
\begin{notesbox}{3. Reading a Graph: Open or Closed, Meets or Jumps}
On a graph, a \textbf{closed} dot $\bullet$ \emph{includes} its endpoint ($\le,\ge$) and an
\textbf{open} dot $\circ$ \emph{excludes} it ($<,>$). Same story, two different functions --- the
\emph{total} paid so far, and the \emph{price this month}:
\[ C(m)=\begin{cases} 0, & 0\le m\le3 \\ 12(m-3), & m>3 \end{cases} \qquad\qquad
   P(m)=\begin{cases} 0, & 0\le m\le3 \\ 12, & m>3 \end{cases} \]

\small \textbf{Careful --- at a boundary, exactly one piece owns the input.} A classmate reads
$P(3)=12$ ``because the price is \$12 after the free months.'' But $3$ satisfies $0\le m\le3$, so

$(3,0)$ says so. The open dot at $(3,12)$ is \emph{not} a point of the graph --- it marks where
the second piece \emph{starts without} its endpoint.
\end{tcolorbox}

\boxguard[26]
\begin{notesbox}{4. Step Functions: the Greatest-Integer Function $\lfloor x\rfloor$}
The monthly price $P(m)$ is \emph{constant} on each piece, then jumps --- a \textbf{step
function}. The most famous one is the \textbf{greatest-integer function} $\lfloor x\rfloor$: the
greatest integer that is \emph{less than or equal to} $x$. It \textbf{rounds down}.
\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.56\linewidth}
$\lfloor 3.7\rfloor=$ \blank{0.8cm} \quad $\lfloor 5\rfloor=$ \blank{0.8cm} \quad
$\lfloor -0.4\rfloor=$ \blank{0.8cm} \quad $\lfloor -2.5\rfloor=$ \blank{0.8cm} \\[4pt]
\emph{Careful:} rounding \emph{down} sends $-2.5$ to \blank{0.8cm}, not $-2$ --- down means
\emph{more negative}, not ``toward zero.'' \\[4pt]
The graph is a \textbf{staircase}: on each interval $[n,n+1)$ the output is the constant $n$
--- closed on the left, open on the right --- then it jumps up by $1$. \\[4pt]
From the staircase: $\lfloor 1.5\rfloor=$ \blank{0.8cm}; \; $\lfloor x\rfloor=2$ for every $x$ in
\blank{1.2cm}; \; the function is \blank{1.4cm} (increasing / constant) on that interval.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.40\linewidth}\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.40]
  \lgrid{-1.5}{4.5}{-1.5}{4}
  \draw[very thick, forest] (-1,-1)--(0,-1); \fdot{forest}{-1}{-1} \hdot{forest}{0}{-1}
  \draw[very thick, forest] (0,0)--(1,0);    \fdot{forest}{0}{0}   \hdot{forest}{1}{0}
  \draw[very thick, forest] (1,1)--(2,1);    \fdot{forest}{1}{1}   \hdot{forest}{2}{1}
  \draw[very thick, forest] (2,2)--(3,2);    \fdot{forest}{2}{2}   \hdot{forest}{3}{2}
  \draw[very thick, forest] (3,3)--(4,3);    \fdot{forest}{3}{3}   \hdot{forest}{4}{3}
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}
Real quantities that ``round down'' are step functions: your age in whole years is
$\lfloor\text{exact age}\rfloor$, and the number of full hours a car has been parked is
$\lfloor t\rfloor$.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{objectivebox}
\textbf{By the end of this lesson, I will be able to\dots}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item decide whether a relation --- given as ordered pairs, a table, a mapping, or a graph --- is a \emph{function}, and state its \emph{domain} and \emph{range};
  \item use function notation: find $f(x)$ from $x$, and find $x$ from $f(x)$, algebraically and graphically;
  \item determine a linear function's domain, range, \emph{zeros}, slope, and intercepts, and interpret each one in context;
  \item write a linear equation in slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard form --- from a graph, from two points, from a slope and a point, and parallel or perpendicular to a given line;
  \item explain how transforming the parent $y=x$ by $kf(x)$ and $f(x)+k$ changes the slope and the $y$-intercept;
  \item read the key characteristics of \emph{quadratic} and \emph{exponential} functions off their graphs;
  \item compare and contrast the three families using tables and graphs.
\end{itemize}
\end{objectivebox}

\begin{vocabbox}
\small
Fill in each term as we build it together.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\vocabans{Relation vs.\ function}{A relation is any pairing of inputs with outputs. It is a function when every input has \emph{exactly one} output. Repeated \emph{outputs} are fine; repeated \emph{inputs} with different outputs are not.}
\vocabans{Domain and range}{The domain is the set of all inputs ($x$); the range is the set of all outputs ($y$). Context can restrict either one.}
\vocabans{Function notation $f(x)$}{Read ``$f$ of $x$'' --- the output of the function $f$ at the input $x$. It is not multiplication. ``$f(3)=0$'' and ``$(3,0)$ is on the graph'' say the same thing.}
\vocabans{Zero of a function}{An input that makes the output $0$ --- a solution of $f(x)=0$. On the graph it is an $x$-intercept.}
\vocabans{Slope (rate of change)}{$m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$: how much $y$ changes per unit change in $x$. A function is linear exactly when this rate is constant.}
\vocabans{Forms of a linear function}{Slope-intercept $y=mx+b$ (shows slope and $y$-intercept), point-slope $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$ (shows a slope and a point), standard $Ax+By=C$ (shows both intercepts easily). One line, three costumes.}
\vocabans{Parent function and transformation}{The simplest member of a family --- $y=x$, $y=x^2$, $y=b^x$. Transformations change it: $k\,f(x)$ stretches/reflects (here: changes the slope), $f(x)+k$ shifts vertically (here: changes the $y$-intercept).}
\vocabans{Constant difference vs.\ constant ratio}{Linear data have a constant first difference; quadratic data have a constant \emph{second} difference; exponential data have a constant ratio (you multiply, not add).}
\end{vocabbox}

\textbf{The idea the whole lesson hangs on:} an equation was a question about \emph{numbers}. A

\begin{notesbox}{1. What Makes a Rule a Function --- and How We Talk to It}
A \textbf{relation} is any pairing of inputs with outputs. It is a \textbf{function} when every
input has \ans{exactly one} output.
\\[2pt]
Note carefully what is \emph{not} forbidden: two different inputs \emph{may} share the same output.
The promise is about \ans{inputs} only. (Hook item (c) is a function.)

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{The same relation, four ways.} A function can arrive as ordered pairs, a table, a mapping,
or a graph. The test looks different each time, but it is always the same test.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Given as\dots} & \textbf{How you check} \\
\hline
ordered pairs / a table & look for a repeated \ans{input ($x$-value)} with different outputs \\
a mapping diagram & look for an input arrow that \ans{splits, pointing to two outputs} \\
a graph & the \textbf{vertical line test}: \ans{no vertical line may cross the graph twice} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
\emph{Why the vertical line test works.} A vertical line is the set of all points with one fixed
$x$. If it hits the graph twice, that single input has \ans{two} outputs --- the promise is
broken. The test is not a trick; it is the definition, \emph{drawn}.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}c c c@{}}
\minigrid{\draw[forest, very thick] (-4,-3) -- (4,5);
          \draw[keyred, dashed] (1,-6) -- (1,6);
          \fill[keyred] (1,2) circle (2.0pt);} &
\minigrid{\draw[forest, very thick] (2,-6) -- (2,6);
          \draw[keyred, dashed] (2,-6) -- (2,6);} &
\minigrid{\draw[forest, very thick, domain=-2.3:2.3, smooth, variable=\x] plot ({\x},{\x*\x-2});
          \draw[keyred, dashed] (1,-6) -- (1,6);
          \fill[keyred] (1,-1) circle (2.0pt);} \\
function? \ans{yes} & function? \ans{no} & function? \ans{yes} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The middle one is the line $x=2$. It \emph{is} a perfectly good line --- it is just not a
\ans{function}, because the single input $2$ is paired with every $y$ there is.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Domain and range.} The \textbf{domain} is the set of all \ans{inputs}; the \textbf{range}
is the set of all \ans{outputs}.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Function} & \textbf{Domain} & \textbf{Range} \\
\hline
$\{(-3,4),(0,1),(2,4),(5,9)\}$ & \ans{$\{-3,0,2,5\}$} & \ans{$\{1,4,9\}$} \\
the line in the Warm-Up, $2x-6$ & \ans{all real numbers} & \ans{all real numbers} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
For a set of pairs the domain is a \emph{list}. For an unbroken line it is \emph{all real numbers}
--- because you may put any number in.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Function notation.} $f(x)$ is read ``$f$ of $x$.'' It is \emph{not} multiplication.
\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\centering
$f$ is the \ans{name} of the machine \quad $\cdot$ \quad $x$ is the \ans{input} \quad
$\cdot$ \quad $f(x)$ is the \ans{output} \\[3pt]
So ``$f(3)=0$'' and ``the point $(3,0)$ is on the graph'' are the \ans{same} sentence.}}
\end{center}
Let $f(x)=2x-6$. Fill both directions --- going \emph{forward} is arithmetic; going
\emph{backward} is solving an equation, which is all of Lesson 1.2.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Forward: given $x$, find $f(x)$} & \textbf{Backward: given $f(x)$, find $x$} \\
\hline
$f(0)=$ \ans{$-6$} & $f(x)=4$ when $x=$ \ans{$5$} \\
$f(5)=$ \ans{$4$} & $f(x)=-2$ when $x=$ \ans{$2$} \\
$f(-1)=$ \ans{$-8$} & $f(x)=0$ when $x=$ \ans{$3$} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
That last box has a name. The value of $x$ that makes $f(x)=0$ is called a \textbf{zero} of the
function, and on the graph it is the \ans{$x$-intercept}.
\\[2pt]
\emph{So Lesson 1.2 was never really about equations.} Every time you solved $ax+b=0$ or
$ax^2+bx+c=0$, you were finding \ans{the zeros of a function}.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{2. Linear Functions --- Every Characteristic, Read Off One Graph}
Below is $f(x)=2x-6$ again, this time as an object to interrogate. Answer each question from the
picture, then check it against the rule.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}c l@{}}
\gridplot{\draw[forest, very thick, domain=-0.6:5, smooth, variable=\x] plot ({\x},{2*\x-6});
          \fill[keyred] (3,0) circle (2.6pt);
          \fill[keyred] (0,-6) circle (2.6pt);}
&
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.56\linewidth}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Domain} & \ans{all real numbers} \\
\textbf{Range} & \ans{all real numbers} \\
\textbf{Zero} & $x=$ \ans{$3$} \\
\textbf{$x$-intercept} & \ans{$(3,0)$} \\
\textbf{$y$-intercept} & \ans{$(0,-6)$} \\
\textbf{Slope} & \ans{$2$} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{minipage}
\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Two of those six rows are the same fact written two ways. Which two?
\ans{the zero and the $x$-intercept}
\\[2pt]
\textbf{Slope is a rate.} \; \TallMath{m=\frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}
=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}}
\\[2pt]
Use the points $(3,0)$ and $(5,4)$ from the graph: \; $m=$ \ans{$\dfrac{4-0}{5-3}=2$}
\\[2pt]
Say it in words: every time $x$ goes up by $1$, $y$ goes \ans{up by $2$}. That constant, unchanging
step is what makes the function \ans{linear}.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Now in context, where the numbers mean something.} A pool is being drained. The volume in
gallons after $t$ minutes is $V(t)=800-25t$.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Feature} & \textbf{What it means \emph{here} --- with units} \\
\hline
$y$-intercept $=800$ & \ans{the pool held $800$ gallons at the start ($t=0$)} \\
slope $=-25$ & \ans{it loses $25$ gallons every minute (negative = draining)} \\
zero: $V(t)=0$ at $t=$ \ans{$32$} & \ans{the pool is empty after $32$ minutes} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
What is the domain \emph{in this situation}? \ans{$0\le t\le 32$} \; Why is it not all real numbers?
\ansline{Negative time has no meaning here, and after $32$ minutes the pool is empty --- the model
stops describing anything. The algebra allows every real number; the \emph{situation} does not, and
the situation wins.}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{3. Writing the Line --- Three Forms, Four Situations}
Same line, three costumes. Each form is honest about a different thing.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l l X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Form} & \textbf{Looks like} & \textbf{Hands you, for free\dots} \\
\hline
slope-intercept & $y=mx+b$ & \ans{the slope $m$ and the $y$-intercept $b$} \\
point-slope & $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$ & \ans{a slope and one point already on the line} \\
standard & $Ax+By=C$ & \ans{both intercepts quickly (set $x=0$, then $y=0$)} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
Rewrite $y-4=2(x-5)$ in slope-intercept form: \ans{$y=2x-6$} \; then in standard form:
\ans{$2x-y=6$} \\
Did the line change? \ans{No} \; This is Lesson 1.2's \emph{literal equations} --- one
equation, solved for different things.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Write the equation.} Four situations, one strategy: get a slope, get a point.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{You are given\dots} & \textbf{Work} & \textbf{Equation} \\
\hline
(a) slope $-3$, point $(2,5)$ & \ans{$y-5=-3(x-2)$} & \ans{$y=-3x+11$} \\
(b) the points $(1,2)$ and $(4,11)$ & \ans{$m=\frac{11-2}{4-1}=3$} & \ans{$y=3x-1$} \\
(c) a vertical line through $(7,-2)$ & \emph{no slope exists} & \ans{$x=7$} \\
(d) a horizontal line through $(7,-2)$ & \emph{slope is} \ans{$0$} & \ans{$y=-2$} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
Only one of (c) and (d) is a function. Which, and why?
\ans{(d) --- $x=7$ fails the vertical line test; $y=-2$ gives every input the single output $-2$.}

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Parallel and perpendicular.} Parallel lines have \ans{equal} slopes. Perpendicular lines
have slopes that are \ans{opposite reciprocals} (multiply to $-1$).
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l@{}}
Through $(0,1)$, parallel to $y=\tfrac12x-4$: & \ans{$y=\tfrac12x+1$} \\
Through $(0,1)$, perpendicular to $y=\tfrac12x-4$: & \ans{$y=-2x+1$} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Transforming the parent.} The \textbf{parent} linear function is $y=x$: slope $1$,
$y$-intercept $0$. Two moves change it, and each one touches exactly one feature.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Change} & \textbf{New slope} & \textbf{New $y$-intercept} \\
\hline
$k\,f(x)$: \; $y=3x$ & \ans{$3$} & \ans{$0$ --- unchanged} \\
$f(x)+k$: \; $y=x+3$ & \ans{$1$ --- unchanged} & \ans{$3$} \\
$k\,f(x)$ with $k<0$: \; $y=-x$ & \ans{$-1$ (reflected)} & \ans{$0$ --- unchanged} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
So multiplying changes the \ans{slope} and adding changes the \ans{$y$-intercept}. Match each equation
to its graph (the dashed line is the parent $y=x$ in every panel).
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}c c c@{}}
\minigrid{\draw[linegray, thick, dashed] (-4,-4) -- (4,4);
          \draw[forest, very thick] (-2,-6) -- (2,6);} &
\minigrid{\draw[linegray, thick, dashed] (-4,-4) -- (4,4);
          \draw[forest, very thick] (-4,-1) -- (3,6);} &
\minigrid{\draw[linegray, thick, dashed] (-4,-4) -- (4,4);
          \draw[forest, very thick] (-4,4) -- (4,-4);} \\
\ans{$y=3x$} & \ans{$y=x+3$} & \ans{$y=-x$} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\emph{Remember this pair of moves.} In Unit 2 the same two transformations get applied to a V, in
Unit 3 to a parabola, and in Unit 7 to a growth curve. The parent changes; the moves do not.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{4. The Other Two Families --- Quadratic and Exponential}
Same questions. Different answers. Read everything off the pictures.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Quadratic: $g(x)=x^2-2x-3$.} You met this curve in Lesson 1.2, where you solved
$x^2-2x-3=0$. Now the solutions have a job.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}c l@{}}
\gridplot{\draw[forest, very thick, domain=-2.2:4.2, smooth, variable=\x] plot ({\x},{\x*\x-2*\x-3});
          \fill[keyred] (-1,0) circle (2.6pt);
          \fill[keyred] (3,0) circle (2.6pt);
          \fill[keyred] (1,-4) circle (2.6pt);}
&
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.56\linewidth}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Zeros ($x$-intercepts)} & \ans{$x=-1$ and $x=3$} \\
\textbf{$y$-intercept} & \ans{$(0,-3)$} \\
\textbf{Vertex} & \ans{$(1,-4)$} \\
\textbf{Maximum or minimum?} & \ans{minimum, value $-4$} \\
\textbf{Domain} & \ans{all real numbers} \\
\textbf{Range} & \ans{$y\ge -4$} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{minipage}
\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The factored form is $g(x)=(x-3)(x+1)$. Where do you \emph{see} the $3$ and the $-1$ on the graph?
\ans{they are exactly the two $x$-intercepts} \\
That is the connection between the algebra and the picture: the factored form displays the
\ans{zeros}, the standard form displays the \ans{$y$-intercept}.
\\[2pt]
\textbf{The range is the new one.} For the line, the range was every real number. Here it is not ---
because the graph has a \ans{minimum} it never goes below. Range: \ans{$y\ge-4$}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Exponential: $h(x)=2^x$.} Here $y=ab^x$ with $a=$ \ans{$1$} and $b=$ \ans{$2$}.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}c l@{}}
\expgraph{\fill[keyred] (0,1) circle (2.4pt);}
&
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.56\linewidth}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{$y$-intercept} & \ans{$(0,1)$} \\
\textbf{Any $x$-intercept?} & \ans{none --- it never reaches $0$} \\
\textbf{Domain} & \ans{all real numbers} \\
\textbf{Range} & \ans{$y>0$} \\
\textbf{Increasing or decreasing?} & \ans{increasing} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{minipage}
\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
This family has \textbf{no zero at all}. Explain why not, using the table you built in the Hook:
\ansline{Doubling a positive number never produces zero, and running backwards halves it forever
--- $2^{-1}=\frac12$, $2^{-3}=\frac18$, $2^{-10}=\frac{1}{1024}$. The outputs shrink toward zero
without ever arriving, so $2^x=0$ has no solution and the graph has no $x$-intercept.}
\\[2pt]
The $y$-intercept of $y=ab^x$ is always $a$, because $b^0=$ \ans{$1$} --- so the $a$ is the
\emph{starting amount}.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{In context.} A colony starts at $50$ bacteria and doubles every hour: $P(t)=50\cdot 2^{\,t}$.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|c|c|c|c@{}}
\hline
$t$ (hours) & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ \\
\hline
$P(t)$ & \ans{$50$} & \ans{$100$} & \ans{$200$} & \ans{$400$} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
What does the $50$ mean here? \ans{the starting population} \; The $2$? \ans{it doubles each hour}
\\[2pt]
Going \emph{backward}: after how many hours are there $800$ bacteria? \ans{$4$ hours} \;
(You can read this off the table --- no new algebra needed yet.)
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{5. Three Families, Side by Side}
Return to the Hook table and look at how each column \emph{grows}.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l|c|c|c|c|c|X@{}}
\hline
$x$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ & \textbf{How it changes} \\
\hline
$f(x)=x$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ & add \ans{$1$} each time \\
$g(x)=x^2$ & $0$ & $1$ & $4$ & $9$ & $16$ & differences $1,3,5,7$ --- \emph{those} go up by \ans{$2$} \\
$h(x)=2^x$ & $1$ & $2$ & $4$ & $8$ & $16$ & \ans{multiply} by $2$ each time \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\centering
\textbf{Linear:} constant \ans{difference}. \quad
\textbf{Quadratic:} constant \emph{second} \ans{difference}. \quad
\textbf{Exponential:} constant \ans{ratio}.}}
\\[3pt]
This is how you identify a family from a \emph{table} alone, with no equation and no graph.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l|c c c c|X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{At} $x=0,1,2,3$ & \multicolumn{4}{c|}{\textbf{the $y$ values are\dots}} & \textbf{Which family? How do you know?} \\
\hline
(a) & $5$ & $8$ & $11$ & $14$ & \ans{linear --- add $3$ each time} \\
(b) & $3$ & $6$ & $12$ & $24$ & \ans{exponential --- multiply by $2$} \\
(c) & $1$ & $4$ & $9$ & $16$ & \ans{quadratic --- second differences are $2$} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{The comparison that matters.} Fill in the last row of the summary.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X X X@{}}
\hline
 & \textbf{Linear $x$} & \textbf{Quadratic $x^2$} & \textbf{Exponential $2^x$} \\
\hline
Domain & all reals & all reals & all reals \\
Range & all reals & \ans{$y\ge0$} & \ans{$y>0$} \\
Number of zeros & \ans{one} & $0$, $1$, or $2$ & \ans{none} \\
Eventually largest? & \ans{no} & \ans{no} & \ans{yes} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
In the Hook the exponential was \emph{tied} at $x=2$ and again at $x=4$, then pulled ahead for good.
Write the moral in one sentence:
\ansline{A function that \emph{multiplies} eventually beats one that only adds, no matter how far
behind it starts --- so ``which is bigger'' is never a fair question without saying \emph{where}.}
\end{notesbox}

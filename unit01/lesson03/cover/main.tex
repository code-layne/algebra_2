\begin{remindbox}
\small The \textbf{absolute value parent} $f(x)=|x|$ graphs as a \textbf{V} opening up, with its
\emph{vertex} (turning point) at the origin and \emph{axis of symmetry} $x=0$. Every other absolute
value function is that same V after a move: in \textbf{vertex form} $g(x)=a|x-h|+k$ the \textbf{vertex}
is $(h,k)$ and the \textbf{axis} is $x=h$. \emph{Inside} the bars acts horizontally and
\emph{backwards} ($|x-h|$ moves the vertex to $x=h$); \emph{outside} the bars acts vertically as
expected ($+k$ up/down). The number $a$ opens the V \textbf{up} when $a>0$ (so $k$ is a
\textbf{minimum}) or \textbf{down} when $a<0$ (so $k$ is a \textbf{maximum}), and $|a|$ makes it
\textbf{narrower} ($|a|>1$) or \textbf{wider} ($|a|<1$).
\end{remindbox}

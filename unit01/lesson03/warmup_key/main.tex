\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Skills We'll Use Today}
Three things you already know. Today an absolute value becomes a \emph{graph}; these are the
tools you'll need --- work quickly, then check with a neighbour.

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{1. Build the table for $y=|x|$.} Evaluate each absolute value.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & $-2$ & $-1$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ \\ \hline
$y=|x|$ & \ans{$2$} & \ans{$1$} & \ans{$0$} & \ans{$1$} & \ans{$2$} \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The two halves of the table are \ans{the same} (the same / different), and the smallest
$y$-value is \ans{$0$}, at $x=$ \ans{$0$}.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{2. Moves you already know (Lesson 1.1).} Fill each blank with a direction or a word.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item Compared to $y=x$, the graph of $y=x+3$ shifts \ans{up} by $3$ (up / down).
  \item Compared to $y=x$, the graph of $y=2x$ is \ans{steeper} (steeper / flatter).
  \item Multiplying a rule by a \emph{negative} number \ans{reflects} its graph over the $x$-axis.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{3. Read the V.} The graph of $y=|x|$ is shown, with the line $y=3$ dashed.
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.48]
  \lgrid{-4}{4}{-1}{5}
  \draw[dashed, redacc] (-4,3)--(4,3) node[right, font=\scriptsize]{$y=3$};
  \draw[very thick, forest] (-4,4)--(0,0)--(4,4);
  \fill[charcoal] (0,0) circle (3.5pt);
  \fill[keyred] (-3,3) circle (3.5pt);
  \fill[keyred] (3,3) circle (3.5pt);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\vspace{-4pt}
The lowest point is at \ans{$(0,0)$}, and the graph is symmetric about the vertical line
$x=$ \ans{$0$}. The V crosses the dashed line $y=3$ at $x=$ \ans{$-3$} and $x=$
\ans{$3$} --- the two solutions of $|x|=3$ from Lesson~1.2.
\end{notesbox}

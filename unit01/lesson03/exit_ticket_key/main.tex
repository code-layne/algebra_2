\vspace{0.04in}

No notes. Show enough work that a wrong answer can still earn a right idea.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Function or not?} Circle your answer, then give the domain and range of the one
        that \emph{is} a function.
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
    \hline
    (a) \; $\{(-2,5),\,(0,1),\,(3,7),\,(0,4)\}$ & function \quad / \quad \ans{not a function} \\
    (b) \; \begin{tabular}{@{}c|c|c|c|c@{}}
             $x$ & $-1$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ \\ \hline
             $y$ & $4$ & $4$ & $4$ & $4$ \\
           \end{tabular}
      & \ans{function} \quad / \quad not a function \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{center}
    Domain: \ans{$\{-1,0,1,2\}$} \qquad Range: \ans{$\{4\}$}
    \\[2pt]
    One of those two breaks the rule. \emph{Which rule, in your own words?}
    \ansline{Each input gets exactly one output. In (a) the input $0$ is paired with both $1$ and
    $4$, so the rule cannot decide --- that is what disqualifies it. (b) repeats an \emph{output},
    which is allowed: nothing says two inputs cannot land on the same value.}

  \item \textbf{Work with the rule.} Let \; $f(x)=-3x+12$.
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}l l l@{}}
    (a) \; $f(2)=$ \ans{$6$} &
    (b) \; $y$-intercept: \ans{$(0,12)$} &
    (c) \; slope: \ans{$-3$} \\
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    (d) \; Find the \textbf{zero} of $f$. \; Equation you solved: \ans{$-3x+12=0$} \quad
    $x=$ \ans{$4$}
    \\[3pt]
    (e) \; Where would that zero show up on the graph of $f$?
    \ansline{As the $x$-intercept --- the graph crosses the $x$-axis at $(4,0)$.}

  \item \textbf{SOL-style multiple choice.} Which equation represents the line shown?
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}c l@{}}
    \gridplot{\draw[forest, very thick, domain=-1.4:5, smooth, variable=\x] plot ({\x},{-2*\x+4});
              \fill[keyred] (0,4) circle (2.4pt);
              \fill[keyred] (2,0) circle (2.4pt);}
    &
    \begin{minipage}[c]{0.44\linewidth}
    \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*., leftmargin=1.7em, itemsep=3pt]
      \item $y=2x+4$
      \item $y=-2x+4$ \quad \textcolor{keyred}{\textbf{$\leftarrow$}}
      \item $y=-2x-4$
      \item $y=-\tfrac12 x+4$
    \end{enumerate}
    \end{minipage}
    \\
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    In one sentence: what does the wrong answer you were most tempted by get wrong?
    \ansline{Answers vary --- A has the line going up instead of down, C reads the $y$-intercept as
    $-4$, and D swaps rise and run to get $-\frac12$ instead of $-2$.}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teachernote}
\footnotesize
\textbf{Answer 3: B.} The line falls $2$ for every $1$ right, so $m=-2$, and it crosses at $(0,4)$.
Each distractor breaks exactly one thing: \textbf{A} loses the sign of the slope, \textbf{C} the sign
of the intercept, \textbf{D} swaps rise and run. A student choosing D can read a graph but has the
ratio upside down --- a two-minute fix; a student choosing A is not attending to direction at all.

\textbf{Sort into four piles:} \emph{got it} / \emph{definition} (item 1 --- called (a) a function,
or called (b) \emph{not} one because outputs repeat) / \emph{zero} (item 2d/2e) / \emph{slope}
(item 3). The \emph{definition} pile costs the most later: a student who thinks repeated
\emph{outputs} disqualify a relation will call every parabola a non-function in Unit 3, and one
sentence fixes it --- the promise is about inputs only. The \emph{zero} pile is the Unit 1
through-line: 2(d) is Lesson 1.2's equation solving renamed, and 2(e) is the whole point of the
renaming --- the number you solved for is a \emph{place on the graph}.

\textbf{Unit 1 ends here,} so let the piles set the review day's emphasis rather than tomorrow's
reteach.
\end{teachernote}

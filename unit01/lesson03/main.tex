\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=forestbg, colframe=forest, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students read the absolute value parent $f(x)=|x|$ as the graph of
    a \emph{distance} --- a \textbf{V} with a single turning point --- and describe every other
    absolute value function as a \emph{transformation} of that parent. From vertex form
    $g(x)=a|x-h|+k$ they locate the \textbf{vertex} $(h,k)$ by setting the inside of the bars to
    zero, name the \textbf{axis of symmetry} $x=h$, decide from the sign of $a$ whether the V opens
    up (so $k$ is a \textbf{minimum}) or down (a \textbf{reflection}, so $k$ is a
    \textbf{maximum}), and from $|a|$ whether it is narrower or wider than the parent. They read
    domain, range, and increasing/decreasing intervals off a pre-drawn V, build a rule from a graph,
    and connect back to Lesson~1.2: the two solutions of $|ax+b|=c$ are the two places the V
    crosses the line $y=c$.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} This lesson applies the Algebra~2 \emph{transformation lens}
    \textbf{A2.F.1} --- write and graph a function using transformations of a parent function
    (\textbf{A2.F.1b}, \textbf{A2.F.1c}: $f(x)+k$, $f(x+k)$, $kf(x)$) --- to the absolute value
    parent as the entry example, and reads the resulting \emph{characteristics} with
    \textbf{A2.F.2}: domain, range, zeros, and intercepts (\textbf{A2.F.2a}), increasing/decreasing
    intervals (\textbf{A2.F.2c}), absolute maximum/minimum (\textbf{A2.F.2d}), and evaluating $f(x)$
    from a graph or a rule (\textbf{A2.F.2f}). Absolute value is itself a \emph{piecewise-defined}
    function, which motivates Lesson~1.4.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Lesson model:} \emph{Gradual release} --- I do, we do, you do alone. A warm-up
    activates prior knowledge; \textbf{Guided Notes} build the vertex-form vocabulary with the
    class on one worked context (a trail dropping to a creek and climbing out) and close with a
    \emph{Guided Practice} box (the ``we do''); an \textbf{Individual Practice} block on the same
    page is the ``you do'' --- three problems, worked silently and alone; a \textbf{debrief}
    consolidates and surfaces the errors; \textbf{homework} extends the practice. \emph{There is no
    group activity in this lesson} --- today's independent work is done individually.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Ideas \& Skills} & \textbf{Key Understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Read the parent V: vertex, axis, minimum, domain/range, where it falls and rises.
  \item Locate the vertex of $a|x-h|+k$ by setting the inside of the bars to zero.
  \item Decide \textbf{up or down} from the sign of $a$; name $k$ as a min or a max.
  \item Decide \textbf{narrower or wider} from $|a|$, the slope of the right-hand ray.
  \item Build a rule from a pre-drawn V: vertex first, then $a$ from a second point.
  \item Connect $|ax+b|=c$ (Lesson~1.2) to where the V crosses $y=c$.
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item $|x-h|$ is a \emph{distance}, so it bottoms out at zero exactly once --- that single fact
        makes the V, the vertex, and the mirror symmetry.
  \item Inside the bars acts \emph{horizontally and backwards}: $|x-3|$ is smallest where
        $x-3=0$, at $x=3$ --- a shift \emph{right}.
  \item \textbf{Target misconception:} that a negative $a$ ``just flips the picture.'' It flips the
        vertex's \emph{job}: $k$ stops being the lowest output and becomes the highest, and the
        range flips with it.
  \item A V is two rays with slopes $a$ and $-a$; that is why $a$ controls both direction and
        width, and why 1.4 can write the V as two pieces.
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Absolute value parent} & $f(x)=|x|$: a V opening up, vertex $(0,0)$, made of $y=x$ ($x\ge0$) and $y=-x$ ($x<0$). \\
\textbf{Vertex} & The turning point --- the lowest point if the V opens up, the highest if it opens down. \\
\textbf{Axis of symmetry} & The vertical line $x=h$ through the vertex; the two rays are mirror images across it. \\
\textbf{Vertex form} & $g(x)=a|x-h|+k$: vertex $(h,k)$, axis $x=h$. Find $h$ by setting the inside to zero. \\
\textbf{Reflection} & $a<0$ flips the V to open downward, so $k$ becomes a maximum and the range is $y\le k$. \\
\textbf{Vertical stretch / compression} & $|a|>1$ narrower (steeper rays); $0<|a|<1$ wider. $a$ is the slope of the right-hand ray. \\
\textbf{Minimum / maximum} & The output $k$ at the vertex: a minimum if $a>0$, a maximum if $a<0$. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{fixedskillbox}[Warm-Up --- Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review]{forestbg}
The Warm-Up rehearses exactly what today leans on. \textbf{(1)} A five-row table for $y=|x|$ makes
the symmetry visible before anyone names it --- the two halves match because $|x|$ is a distance,
and the single smallest value at $x=0$ is the vertex-to-be. \textbf{(2)} Three moves from
Lesson~1.1 --- $+3$ shifts up, $2x$ is steeper, a negative coefficient reflects --- are the exact
moves section~2 and section~3 aim at a new parent. \textbf{(3)} Reading the pre-drawn V: the lowest
point, the fold line, and where the V crosses the dashed line $y=3$. \textbf{Debrief item 3 aloud,
briefly}, and land only this: \emph{the V crosses $y=3$ at $-3$ and $3$ --- the two answers to
$|x|=3$ from last lesson. Hold on to that; section~4 comes back to it.} \textbf{This page is set
at 12pt} --- larger type than the rest of the packet, deliberately.
\end{fixedskillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{forestbg}
    ``A trail drops toward a creek, crosses it on a bridge, and climbs the far side. Its height
    above the creek, in hundreds of feet, $x$ miles from the trailhead, is $H(x)=2|x-3|+1$.'' Put
    the V on the board and ask: \textbf{``How high at the trailhead? At mile 3? At mile 6?''}
    ($7$, $1$, $7$.) \textbf{``Where is the trail lowest, and how low?''} (mile $3$, height $1$ ---
    the bridge.) Then the question that carries the lesson: \textbf{``Why can the height never
    drop below 1?''} ($|x-3|$ is a distance, so it is never below $0$, and $2\cdot0+1=1$.) Say:
    \emph{every absolute value graph is this same V --- the parent $y=|x|$ moved, stretched, or
    flipped --- and today we read the moves straight off the rule.}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Guided Notes --- the Lesson (20 min)]{forestbg}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{1. The parent $f(x)=|x|$.} Fill the table, read the V: vertex $(0,0)$, axis $x=0$,
minimum $0$, domain all reals, range $y\ge0$, decreasing on $(-\infty,0]$ and increasing on
$[0,\infty)$. Name the two rays, $y=x$ and $y=-x$ --- the seed of Lesson~1.4.

\textbf{2. Move it: inside vs.\ outside.} Outside the bars is vertical and behaves; inside is
horizontal \emph{and backwards}. Fill the two-row table, label the two pre-drawn shifts, then the
gold box: \emph{find the vertex by setting the inside to zero} --- $x-3=0$ at $x=3$, so $|x-3|$
moves \emph{right}. The trail's vertex $(3,1)$ falls out the same way.

\columnbreak
\textbf{3. The number $a$: up or down, narrow or wide.} The two-column table, valley
$H(x)=2|x-3|+1$ beside ridge $R(x)=-2|x-3|+7$: same axis, opposite opening, min $1$ versus max $7$,
range $y\ge1$ versus $y\le7$, and the increasing/decreasing intervals swap. Then the gold caution:
test $x=5$ on the ridge --- $R(5)=3$, \emph{below} $7$ --- so $7$ is the highest output. \emph{A
negative $a$ does not just flip the picture; it flips the vertex's job.} This is the target
misconception, and this box is where it dies. Close with width: $a$ is the right ray's slope,
so $|a|>1$ is narrower and $0<|a|<1$ wider.

\textbf{4. Vertex form, all at once --- and back to 1.2.} Read every feature of $g(x)=-2|x-1|+3$
from the rule, then the \texttt{work} block $g(0)=1$. Then $H(x)=5$: isolate, split, $x=1$ or $5$
--- \emph{last lesson's two solutions are the two places the V crosses $y=5$.}

\textbf{Guided Practice (the ``we do'').} $g(x)=\tfrac12|x+4|-2$: every feature, $g(0)$ in a
\texttt{work} block, then \emph{build} a rule from a pre-drawn V --- vertex $(-1,4)$, through
$(1,0)$, so $a=-2$ and $y=-2|x+1|+4$. Do this one \emph{with} the class --- it is the last thing
they see before working alone.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Practice --- \emph{On Your Own} (15 min, silent)]{redbox}
\textbf{Launch (1 min):} ``Same moves you just watched --- now nobody helps you. Three problems.
Set the inside to zero for the vertex, then let the sign of $a$ tell you up or down. Stuck? Look
up the page at the notes, not at your neighbour.'' The block sits on the last page of the Guided
Notes, so nothing new is handed out.
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{What students do.} \textbf{Problem~1} is features from a rule with $a<0$:
$p(x)=-3|x-2|+5$ --- vertex $(2,5)$, opens down, $5$ a \emph{maximum}, range $y\le5$, narrower ---
then $p(4)=-1$ in a \texttt{work} block. \textbf{Problem~2} runs the other direction: a pre-drawn
V with vertex $(1,-2)$ through $(3,2)$ gives $a=2$ and the rule $y=2|x-1|-2$; then they read the two
crossings with $y=2$ off the graph ($x=-1$ and $3$) and \emph{check} them by solving
$2|x-1|-2=2$ --- the 1.2 link, worked by hand. \textbf{Problem~3} is the crux: $y=|x+2|-3$ beside
$y=-|x+2|-3$ --- same vertex, same axis, opposite opening, min versus max, opposite range --- and a
classmate's claim that ``both have a minimum of $-3$'' to refute in two lines.

\columnbreak
\textbf{What the teacher does.} Circulate the whole 15 minutes; keep it silent. Give a
\emph{question, cue, or prompt}, never an answer:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep]
  \item 1: ``Where is the inside of the bars zero? That is your $h$.''
  \item 1: ``Your $a$ is negative --- so which way do the rays go from the vertex? Then is $5$ the
        top or the bottom?''
  \item 2: ``Start at the vertex and walk to $(3,2)$: how far up, how far over?''
  \item 2: ``You wrote $|x+1|$ --- but where is the vertex? Which sign puts it at $x=1$?''
  \item 3: ``Draw both, small. Which one has $-3$ at the top?''
  \item Finished early: ``In problem~2, change $a$ to $\tfrac12$. Does the vertex move? Do the
        crossings with $y=2$ move?''
\end{itemize}
Note names as you go: one student for each of the three problems, to put work on the board in the
debrief.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Debrief (10 min --- what goes on the board, in a second color)]{forestbg}
Students correct their own Individual Practice in a second colour as you go. Work all three, but
protect the time for items~2 and~4.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em, nosep, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Problem~1 --- vertex from the inside, direction from $a$.} $x-2=0$ gives
        $x=2$; $k=5$; vertex $(2,5)$. $a=-3<0$: opens down, so $5$ is a \emph{maximum} and the
        range is $y\le5$. Show $p(4)=-3(2)+5=-1$ --- \emph{below} $5$, as it must be.
  \item \textbf{The must-land moment: a negative $a$ flips the vertex's job.} Put problem~3's
        two V's side by side. Same vertex $(-2,-3)$, same axis $x=-2$; one opens up (min $-3$,
        range $y\ge-3$), one opens down (max $-3$, range $y\le-3$). Point back at the ridge's
        $R(5)=3<7$ from the notes. Say it out loud: \emph{the vertex is the same point, but it is
        the bottom of one graph and the top of the other.}
  \item \textbf{Problem~2 --- building a rule, then checking it.} Vertex $(1,-2)$; from there to
        $(3,2)$ is up $4$, over $2$, so $a=2$: $y=2|x-1|-2$. Ask who wrote $|x+1|$ and where that
        vertex would be. Then the check: $2|x-1|-2=2 \Rightarrow |x-1|=2 \Rightarrow x=3$ or
        $-1$ --- exactly the two crossings with $y=2$ on the picture.
  \item \textbf{Why $a$ does two jobs.} The V is two rays with slopes $a$ and $-a$. The sign of
        $a$ says which way they leave the vertex (up or down); the size says how steeply (narrow or
        wide). Preview: in 1.4 those two rays become two \emph{pieces} of one rule.
\end{enumerate}
If time is short, cut item~4 and protect items~2 and~3.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring --- Watch For]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{the horizontal sign flip} --- reading $|x-3|$ as a shift \emph{left} $3$
        (notes~2, Individual Practice~2, homework~6) --- send them to ``inside $=0$,'' not to a rule;
  \item \textbf{treating a negative $a$ as ``just a flip''} and keeping $k$ as a minimum
        (Individual Practice~1 and~3, homework~2) --- the lesson's target misconception. Send them
        to the ridge's $R(5)=3$, not to the rule;
  \item saying the range is ``all reals'' --- the V stops at $k$: $y\ge k$ up, $y\le k$ down
        (Individual Practice~1, homework~1);
  \item calling a wider graph ``$|a|>1$'' --- $|a|>1$ is \emph{narrower}, $0<|a|<1$ wider
        (notes~3, homework~4b);
  \item computing $a$ from a graph with the wrong run, or dropping its sign when the V opens down
        (Guided Practice~3, Individual Practice~2, extension);
  \item splitting the increasing/decreasing intervals at $0$ instead of at $x=h$ (notes~3);
  \item in homework~4(b), forgetting that $a=0$ is not a V at all --- the boundary is the trap.
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts: ``Where is the inside zero?'' ``Up or down --- and so is $k$ the top or the
bottom?'' ``Narrower or wider than the parent, and how do you know from $a$?'' ``Where does this V
cross $y=c$, and what equation is that?''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Item~1's table is the seed of the whole lesson: the two halves match because $|x|$ is a distance,
so $x$ and $-x$ give the same output --- that symmetry \emph{is} the axis of symmetry, and the one
smallest value is the vertex. Item~2 reactivates Lesson~1.1's language for the three moves we now
aim at a new parent; if the room hesitates on ``reflects,'' say it for them --- it is vocabulary,
not the lesson. Item~3's last question is deliberately a callback: the V crosses $y=3$ at $-3$ and
$3$, and those are the two solutions of $|x|=3$ from Lesson~1.2. Debrief that one sentence and
leave it --- section~4 pays it off with the trail. \textbf{This page is set at 12pt} --- larger
type than the rest of the packet, deliberately. It is still one page; if you add to it, check that
it stays one page.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
Twenty minutes, paced by section: 3 for the vocabulary and hook, 3 for section~1, 4 for
section~2, 5 for section~3, 3 for section~4, and 2 for the Guided Practice box worked \emph{with}
the class. \textbf{Keep the whole notes block on one context} --- the trail --- so ``the bridge is
the vertex'' is doing the work in every section. Two boxes to protect: section~2's gold box
(\emph{set the inside to zero}) kills the horizontal sign flip; section~3's two-column table and
its gold caution kill the ``negative $a$ just flips it'' error, and the ridge's $R(5)=3<7$ is the
concrete case that makes ``$k$ is now a maximum'' undeniable. Two \texttt{work} blocks are printed
in the numbered sections ($g(0)$ and the $H(x)=5$ solve) plus three more in the Guided Practice
and Individual Practice; students write in the reserved space while the key prints the same steps
there, so the two files paginate alike. If you fall behind, compress section~1's interval reading
to one sentence --- but do \emph{not} cut section~4's ``back to Lesson 1.2'' solve; Individual
Practice~2 checks its two crossings the same way.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Individual Practice]
Fifteen minutes, and \textbf{keep it silent} --- this is the only block all period where a student
finds out what they can do without help. Budget roughly 5 minutes each. The three problems are
deliberately the three \emph{directions} of the skill: rule $\to$ features (with $a<0$), graph
$\to$ rule (with a check by solving), and the same-vertex pair that isolates what the sign of $a$
does. A student who can do 1 and 2 but writes ``minimum'' in 3 has not accepted that the vertex
changes jobs --- send them back to the ridge picture in section~3, not to a rule. Watch for
$|x+1|$ in problem~2 (vertex at $x=1$ needs $|x-1|$) and for a run of $1$ instead of $2$ when
computing $a$. Fast finishers change problem~2's $a$ to $\tfrac12$ and report what moves and what
does not (vertex stays; the crossings with $y=2$ move out to $-7$ and $9$); that is the work you
want on the board in the debrief.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
Item~1(b) is the double check --- $a=-\tfrac12$ is both negative (opens down, $3$ a maximum) and
small (wider); expect ``narrower'' from anyone who reads the fraction as a stretch. Item~2 is the
target misconception in the wild: both rules share $(1,-2)$, and the two-line explanation should
say the rays \emph{rise} away from the vertex in one and \emph{fall} away in the other. Item~4 is
the boundary trap: for (b), $a=\pm1$ is the same width as the parent (so excluded) and $a=0$
flattens the V to the line $y=0$; students who write ``$a<1$'' have the idea but not the boundary.
Item~5(c) is the concept check --- doubling the speed doubles $a$ (narrower) and leaves the vertex
alone. Item~6 is the formative check: sort into ``chose B (got it) / chose A (the horizontal sign
flip, still alive) / chose C (both signs flipped) / chose D (read $+4$ as right and $-2$ as up)''
to decide whether Lesson~1.4 opens with a one-minute re-look at ``inside $=0$.'' Two
\texttt{work} blocks (items 5b and the extension). With the group activity cut, the last 10
minutes are for \emph{starting} this page in class --- launch item~1 aloud, then circulate; the
rest is due next class.
\end{teachernote}

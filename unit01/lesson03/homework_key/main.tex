\begin{notesbox}{Practice}
Every ``zero'' answer must also be given as a \textbf{point}. Every context answer must be a
\textbf{sentence with units}.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=8pt]
  \item \textbf{Function or not?} Circle one. Give the domain and range of each relation that
        \emph{is} a function.
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X X@{}}
    \hline
    \textbf{Relation} & \textbf{Function?} & \textbf{Domain / Range} \\
    \hline
    (a) \; $\{(-1,2),(0,4),(3,2)\}$ & \ans{yes} \; / \; no & \ans{$D=\{-1,0,3\}$, $R=\{2,4\}$} \\
    (b) \; $\{(4,1),(4,2),(6,3)\}$ & yes \; / \; \ans{no} & \ans{---} \\
    (c) \; the line $y=-7$ & \ans{yes} \; / \; no & \ans{$D=$ all reals, $R=\{-7\}$} \\
    (d) \; the line $x=-7$ & yes \; / \; \ans{no} & \ans{---} \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{center}
    Items (c) and (d) look almost identical and only one is a function. Explain the difference.
    \ansline{$y=-7$ gives \emph{every} input the single output $-7$ --- one in, one out, so it is a
    function (a horizontal line). $x=-7$ gives the single input $-7$ \emph{every} output there is,
    so that one input has infinitely many outputs and it fails the vertical line test. Repeating an
    output is allowed; repeating an input is not.}

  \item \textbf{Both directions.} Let $f(x)=5x-15$.
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X X@{}}
    \hline
    \textbf{Forward} & \textbf{Backward} \\
    \hline
    $f(1)=$ \ans{$-10$} & $f(x)=0$ when $x=$ \ans{$3$} \\
    $f(4)=$ \ans{$5$} & $f(x)=10$ when $x=$ \ans{$5$} \\
    $f(-2)=$ \ans{$-25$} & $f(x)=-15$ when $x=$ \ans{$0$} \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{center}
    Write the zero of $f$ as a point: \ans{$(3,0)$} \; and say what that point is on the graph:
    \ans{the $x$-intercept}

  \item \textbf{Read the line.} The graph below is $f$.
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}c l@{}}
    \gridplot{\draw[forest, very thick, domain=-6:6, smooth, variable=\x] plot ({\x},{0.5*\x-2});
              \fill[keyred] (4,0) circle (2.2pt);
              \fill[keyred] (0,-2) circle (2.2pt);}
    &
    \begin{minipage}[c]{0.56\linewidth}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
    \hline
    Domain & \ans{all real numbers} \\
    Range & \ans{all real numbers} \\
    $y$-intercept & \ans{$(0,-2)$} \\
    Zero & \ans{$x=4$, i.e.\ $(4,0)$} \\
    Slope & \ans{$\tfrac12$} \\
    $f(6)=$ \ans{$1$} & $f(x)=-3$ at $x=$ \ans{$-2$} \\
    Equation of $f$ & \ans{$y=\tfrac12x-2$} \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{minipage}
    \\
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}

  \item \textbf{One line, three costumes.} Start from $y-1=3(x-2)$.
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X X@{}}
    \hline
    \textbf{Form} & \textbf{The equation} & \textbf{What it shows at a glance} \\
    \hline
    point-slope & $y-1=3(x-2)$ & \ans{slope $3$ and the point $(2,1)$} \\
    slope-intercept & \ans{$y=3x-5$} & \ans{slope $3$, $y$-intercept $(0,-5)$} \\
    standard & \ans{$3x-y=5$} & \ans{both intercepts fast: $(\frac53,0)$ and $(0,-5)$} \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{center}
    Are these three different lines or one line? \ans{one line} \; What Lesson 1.2 skill did you just
    use? \ans{solving a literal equation --- rearranging for a chosen variable}

  \item \textbf{Write the equation.}
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.7}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
    \hline
    (a) \; slope $-4$, through $(1,9)$ & \ans{$y=-4x+13$} \\
    (b) \; through $(-3,2)$ and $(1,10)$ & \ans{$m=2$, so $y=2x+8$} \\
    (c) \; horizontal, through $(5,-8)$ & \ans{$y=-8$} \\
    (d) \; vertical, through $(5,-8)$ & \ans{$x=5$} \\
    (e) \; through $(0,-3)$, parallel to $y=\tfrac25x+1$ & \ans{$y=\tfrac25x-3$} \\
    (f) \; through $(0,-3)$, perpendicular to $y=\tfrac25x+1$ & \ans{$y=-\tfrac52x-3$} \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{center}
    Exactly one of (a)--(f) is not a function. Which? \ans{(d)}

  \item \textbf{Transforming the parent $y=x$.} Each graph below shows $y=x$ dashed and a
        transformed line solid. Write the equation of the solid line, and name the move.
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}c c c@{}}
    \minigrid{\draw[linegray, thick, dashed] (-4,-4) -- (4,4);
              \draw[forest, very thick] (-3,-6) -- (3,6);} &
    \minigrid{\draw[linegray, thick, dashed] (-4,-4) -- (4,4);
              \draw[forest, very thick] (-2,-6) -- (4,0);} &
    \minigrid{\draw[linegray, thick, dashed] (-4,-4) -- (4,4);
              \draw[forest, very thick] (-4,2) -- (4,-2);} \\
    (a) \ans{$y=2x$} & (b) \ans{$y=x-4$} & (c) \ans{$y=-\tfrac12x$} \\
    \ans{$k\,f(x)$, $k=2$} & \ans{$f(x)+k$, $k=-4$} & \ans{$k\,f(x)$, $k=-\tfrac12$} \\
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    Which of the three moves left the \emph{slope} alone? \ans{(b)} \; Which left the
    \emph{$y$-intercept} alone? \ans{(a) and (c)}

  \item \textbf{Read the parabola.} The graph below is $p(x)=-x^2+2x+3$.
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}c l@{}}
    \gridplot{\draw[forest, very thick, domain=-2.1:4.1, smooth, variable=\x]
                plot ({\x},{-\x*\x+2*\x+3});
              \fill[keyred] (-1,0) circle (2.2pt);
              \fill[keyred] (3,0) circle (2.2pt);
              \fill[keyred] (1,4) circle (2.2pt);}
    &
    \begin{minipage}[c]{0.56\linewidth}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
    \hline
    Zeros & \ans{$x=-1$ and $x=3$} \\
    $y$-intercept & \ans{$(0,3)$} \\
    Vertex & \ans{$(1,4)$} \\
    Maximum or minimum? & \ans{maximum, value $4$} \\
    Domain & \ans{all real numbers} \\
    Range & \ans{$y\le 4$} \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{minipage}
    \\
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    Factor $-x^2+2x+3$ and check that the factors predict the zeros you read.
    \ans{$-(x^2-2x-3)=-(x-3)(x+1)$, so the zeros are $3$ and $-1$ --- exactly what the graph shows.} \\
    The range is not ``all real numbers'' this time. Why not?
    \ans{the graph turns around at a maximum of $4$ and never goes above it}

  \item \textbf{An exponential in context.} A car bought for \$$24{,}000$ is worth
        $V(t)=24000\cdot(0.8)^{\,t}$ dollars after $t$ years.
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|c|c|c@{}}
    \hline
    $t$ (years) & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ \\
    \hline
    $V(t)$ & \ans{$\$24{,}000$} & \ans{$\$19{,}200$} & \ans{$\$15{,}360$} \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{center}
    In $V(t)=ab^{\,t}$: \; $a=$ \ans{$24000$} means \ans{the price when new ($t=0$)} \\
    $b=0.8$, so each year the car keeps \ans{$80$}\% of its value --- meaning it \emph{loses}
    \ans{$20$}\%.
    \\[2pt]
    Will the car ever be worth exactly \$$0$ according to this model? \ans{No} \; Explain, and
    say whether that is realistic.
    \ansline{Multiplying by $0.8$ shrinks the value every year but never reaches zero --- the model
    has no zero, so its graph has no $x$-intercept. That is unrealistic in the long run: a real car
    eventually becomes scrap with a fixed value, so the model is trustworthy for a handful of years
    and not for fifty.}

  \item \textbf{Name the family from the table alone.} No graphing, no guessing --- use differences
        and ratios, and say which you used.
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l|c c c c|X@{}}
    \hline
    \textbf{At} $x=0,1,2,3$ & \multicolumn{4}{c|}{\textbf{the $y$ values are\dots}} & \textbf{Family, and the evidence} \\
    \hline
    (a) & $-2$ & $1$ & $4$ & $7$ & \ans{linear --- constant difference $+3$} \\
    (b) & $5$ & $15$ & $45$ & $135$ & \ans{exponential --- constant ratio $3$} \\
    (c) & $2$ & $3$ & $6$ & $11$ & \ans{quadratic --- differences $1,3,5$; second difference $2$} \\
    (d) & $100$ & $50$ & $25$ & $12.5$ & \ans{exponential --- constant ratio $\tfrac12$} \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{center}
    Two of the four are exponential. What is different about them?
    \ans{(b) has ratio $>1$ and grows; (d) has ratio between $0$ and $1$ and decays.}

  \item \textbf{Explain.} A classmate says: ``$\{(1,3),(2,3),(5,3)\}$ can't be a function, and
        neither can $x=4$, because in both cases one value keeps repeating.'' Exactly one of those
        two claims is right. Say which, and explain the difference in terms of \textbf{inputs} and
        \textbf{outputs}.
        \ansline{Only the claim about $x=4$ is right, and the classmate's reason is wrong in both
        cases. In $\{(1,3),(2,3),(5,3)\}$ the repeated value $3$ is an \textbf{output}: three
        different inputs happen to land on the same result, which the definition never forbids. It
        \emph{is} a function --- it is the horizontal line $y=3$ sampled at three points.\\[3pt]
        The line $x=4$ fails for the opposite reason: the repeated value $4$ is an \textbf{input},
        and it is paired with every $y$ there is, so the rule cannot say what $f(4)$ means. One
        input, one output is the whole promise --- and it constrains inputs only. The vertical line
        test is that sentence drawn: a vertical line hits $x=4$ everywhere, and $y=3$ exactly once.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{extensionbox}
\textbf{Determined, undetermined, and overtaken.} Three questions about what it takes to pin a
function down --- and what happens when families race.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item $g$ is \textbf{linear}, $g(1)=-2$, and $g(4)=7$.
        \\[2pt]
        Slope: \ans{$3$} \quad Rule: $g(x)=$ \ans{$3x-5$} \quad $g(0)=$ \ans{$-5$} \quad
        Zero: \ans{$x=\tfrac53$}
        \\[2pt]
        Now delete the word ``linear.'' Is the rule still determined? \ans{No} \; Why?
        \ansline{Infinitely many curves pass through $(1,-2)$ and $(4,7)$. For example
        $q(x)=x^2-2x-1$ gives $q(1)=-2$ and $q(4)=7$ as well --- but $q(0)=-1$, not $-5$. Two points
        determine a \emph{line} and nothing more, so the word ``linear'' is doing real work.}
  \item A quadratic $q$ has zeros $-3$ and $5$.
        \\[2pt]
        Write one possible rule in factored form: \ans{$q(x)=(x+3)(x-5)$} \\
        Write a \emph{different} rule with the same two zeros: \ans{$q(x)=2(x+3)(x-5)$} \\
        What is the $x$-coordinate of the vertex, and how do you know it is the same for both?
        \ansline{$x=1$, the midpoint of $-3$ and $5$. A parabola is symmetric about the vertical
        line through its vertex, so that line must sit halfway between the two $x$-intercepts ---
        and stretching by a factor of $2$ changes how tall the curve is, not where it crosses.}
  \item \textbf{The race, one more time.} Compare $L(x)=100x$ and $E(x)=2^x$.
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|c|c|c|c|c@{}}
    \hline
    $x$ & $1$ & $5$ & $10$ & $15$ & $20$ \\
    \hline
    $L(x)=100x$ & \ans{$100$} & \ans{$500$} & \ans{$1000$} & \ans{$1500$} & \ans{$2000$} \\
    $E(x)=2^x$ & \ans{$2$} & \ans{$32$} & \ans{$1024$} & \ans{$32768$} & \ans{$1{,}048{,}576$} \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{center}
    $L$ starts enormously ahead. Between which two of those inputs does $E$ pass it?
    \ans{between $x=5$ and $x=10$}
    \\[2pt]
    A classmate says ``$100x$ is bigger because $100$ is bigger than $2$.'' In two sentences,
    explain what that argument confuses.
    \ansline{It compares the two numbers' \emph{sizes} instead of the two functions' \emph{kinds of
    growth}: $100$ is how much $L$ adds each step, while $2$ is what $E$ multiplies by. A head start
    is a fixed advantage and repeated multiplication is not, so the size of the coefficient decides
    only \emph{when} the exponential wins --- never \emph{whether} it does.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{extensionbox}

\textbf{That is all of Unit 1.} Three domain areas, in order: \emph{expressions} (1.1) were recipes
you could rewrite but never solve; \emph{equations} (1.2) were questions with one, no, or infinitely
many answers; and \emph{functions} (1.3) are machines whose graphs display every answer at once ---
which is why a ``zero'' and an ``$x$-intercept'' turned out to be the same thing said in two
languages. Lesson 1.0's properties were underneath all of it, licensing every step.

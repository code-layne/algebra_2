\begin{notesbox}{Practice}
Every ``zero'' answer must also be given as a \textbf{point}. Every context answer must be a
\textbf{sentence with units}.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=10pt]
  \item \textbf{Function or not?} Circle one. Give the domain and range of each relation that
        \emph{is} a function.

    (b) \; $\{(4,1),(4,2),(6,3)\}$ & yes \; / \; no & \blank{4.2cm} \\
    (c) \; the line $y=-7$ & yes \; / \; no & \blank{4.2cm} \\

    \end{tabularx}

\begin{extensionbox}
\textbf{Determined, undetermined, and overtaken.} Three questions about what it takes to pin a
function down --- and what happens when families race.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=6pt]
  \item $g$ is \textbf{linear}, $g(1)=-2$, and $g(4)=7$.
        \\[2pt]
        Slope: \blank{1.8cm} \quad Rule: $g(x)=$ \blank{3.2cm} \quad $g(0)=$ \blank{1.6cm} \quad
        Zero: \blank{2.0cm}
        \\[2pt]
        Now delete the word ``linear.'' Is the rule still determined? \blank{1.6cm} \; Why?
        \writeline
  \item A quadratic $q$ has zeros $-3$ and $5$.
        \\[2pt]
        Write one possible rule in factored form: \blank{4.0cm} \\
        Write a \emph{different} rule with the same two zeros: \blank{4.0cm} \\
        What is the $x$-coordinate of the vertex, and how do you know it is the same for both?
        \writeline
  \item \textbf{The race, one more time.} Compare $L(x)=100x$ and $E(x)=2^x$.
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
    \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|c|c|c|c|c@{}}
    \hline
    $x$ & $1$ & $5$ & $10$ & $15$ & $20$ \\
    \hline
    $L(x)=100x$ & \blank{1.2cm} & \blank{1.2cm} & \blank{1.2cm} & \blank{1.2cm} & \blank{1.2cm} \\
    $E(x)=2^x$ & \blank{1.2cm} & \blank{1.2cm} & \blank{1.2cm} & \blank{1.2cm} & \blank{1.2cm} \\
    \hline
    \end{tabularx}
    \end{center}
    $L$ starts enormously ahead. Between which two of those inputs does $E$ pass it?
    \blank{3.0cm}
    \\[2pt]
    A classmate says ``$100x$ is bigger because $100$ is bigger than $2$.'' In two sentences,
    explain what that argument confuses. \writelines{2}
\end{enumerate}
\end{extensionbox}

\begin{spiralbox}
\textbf{That is all of Unit 1.} Three domain areas, in order: \emph{expressions} (1.1) were recipes
you could rewrite but never solve; \emph{equations} (1.2) were questions with one, no, or infinitely
many answers; and \emph{functions} (1.3) are machines whose graphs display every answer at once ---
which is why a ``zero'' and an ``$x$-intercept'' turned out to be the same thing said in two
languages. Lesson 1.0's properties were underneath all of it, licensing every step.
\\[3pt]
\textbf{Next: the Unit 1 test,} then \textbf{Unit 2 --- Linear Functions}, which opens with Lesson
2.0, \emph{Characteristics of Linear Functions}. Every unit from here on begins the same way: meet
the new family, learn to read its graph, and only then learn to manipulate it. Today's six
questions --- domain, range, zeros, intercepts, rate of change, increasing or decreasing --- are the
questions you will ask of \emph{every} family for the rest of the year, and the list only grows.
\end{spiralbox}

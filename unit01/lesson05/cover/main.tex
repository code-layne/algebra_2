\begin{remindbox}
\small A \textbf{scatterplot} plots paired data $(x,y)$. Describe its \textbf{association} by
\emph{direction} (positive if $y$ rises with $x$, negative if it falls), \emph{form} (does a line fit?), and
\emph{strength}. The \textbf{correlation coefficient} $r$ lives in $[-1,1]$: its \emph{sign} is the
direction and its \emph{size} $|r|$ the strength of the \emph{linear} fit ($|r|\approx 1$ tight,
$r\approx 0$ none). The \textbf{line of best fit} $\hat y = ax+b$ (from technology) summarizes the trend:
its \textbf{slope} is a per-unit rate, its \textbf{$y$-intercept} a baseline, and substituting an $x$
\textbf{predicts} $\hat y$. Predicting \emph{inside} the data is \textbf{interpolation}; \emph{outside} it is
\textbf{extrapolation} (riskier). A strong $r$ does \emph{not} prove \textbf{causation}.
\end{remindbox}

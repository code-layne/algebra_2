\begin{notesbox}{1. Scatterplots \& Association: Direction, Form, Strength}
A \textbf{scatterplot} plots paired data $(x,y)$. Describe an association three ways: \textbf{direction}
(does $y$ rise or fall as $x$ rises?), \textbf{form} (do the points follow a \emph{line}?), and
\textbf{strength} (how tightly?). Classify each plot below.

  \dpt{1}{2}\dpt{2}{2}\dpt{3}{4}\dpt{4}{4}\dpt{5}{6}\dpt{6}{6}
  \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries] at (3,-1.6){(a)};
\end{tikzpicture}
\hspace{0.3cm}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.4]

  \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries] at (3,-1.6){(b)};
\end{tikzpicture}
\hspace{0.3cm}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.4]
  \lgrid{-0.5}{7}{-0.5}{7}
  \dpt{1}{3}\dpt{2}{6}\dpt{3}{2}\dpt{4}{5}\dpt{5}{3}\dpt{6}{5}
  \node[font=\scriptsize\bfseries] at (3,-1.6){(c)};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item Plot (a): direction is \blank{2.4cm}; the points \emph{do / do not} follow a line: \blank{1.6cm}.
  \item Plot (b): direction is \blank{2.4cm} --- as $x$ rises, $y$ \blank{2.0cm}.
  \item Plot (c): direction is \blank{2.4cm} --- there is \emph{no} clear linear trend.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{2. The Correlation Coefficient $r$}
Technology reports one number, the \textbf{correlation coefficient} $r$, that measures the \emph{linear}
association. It always lies between \blank{1.0cm} and \blank{1.0cm}. Its \textbf{sign} gives the
\emph{direction}; its \textbf{size} $|r|$ gives the \emph{strength}.
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1]
  \draw[semithick, charcoal, ->] (-3.4,0)--(3.4,0);
  \foreach \x/\lab in {-3/-1, 0/0, 3/1}{\draw (\x,0.12)--(\x,-0.12) node[below, font=\scriptsize]{$\lab$};}
  \node[font=\scriptsize, forest] at (-3,0.45){strong negative};
  \node[font=\scriptsize, forest] at (0,0.45){no linear trend};
  \node[font=\scriptsize, forest] at (3,0.45){strong positive};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\textbf{Match each $r$ to a plot from Section~1} (write a, b, or c):
\[ r\approx 0.90:\ \blank{1.2cm} \qquad r\approx -0.85:\ \blank{1.2cm} \qquad r\approx 0.05:\ \blank{1.2cm} \]
\textbf{Warning:} $r$ measures a \emph{linear} fit only, and a strong $|r|$ does \emph{not} prove one
variable \emph{causes} the other. A negative $r$ is not ``weak'' --- $r=-0.9$ is a \blank{2.0cm} (strong /
weak) association.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{3. The Line of Best Fit (Regression Line)}
Technology also returns the \textbf{line of best fit} $\hat y=ax+b$ --- the single line that best summarizes
the trend. The hat on $\hat y$ means it is a \emph{predicted} value. For the free-throw data, technology
gives
\[ \hat y = 1.5x + 2, \qquad r = 0.96. \]
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.42]
  \lgrid{-0.5}{9}{-0.5}{15}
  \draw[very thick, forest] (0,2)--(8.5,14.75);
  \dpt{1}{4}\dpt{2}{5}\dpt{3}{6}\dpt{4}{8}\dpt{5}{9}\dpt{6}{11}\dpt{7}{12}\dpt{8}{13}
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\textbf{Interpret in context (this is the point):}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item The \textbf{slope} $a=1.5$ means each additional hour of practice is associated with about
        \blank{1.6cm} more free throws made.
  \item The \textbf{$y$-intercept} $b=2$ is the predicted free throws for a player who practices
        \blank{1.4cm} hours --- a \emph{baseline}.
  \item Is $r=0.96$ a strong or weak fit? \blank{2.0cm}, because $|r|$ is close to \blank{1.0cm}.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{4. Predicting --- and Its Limits}
To \textbf{predict}, substitute an $x$ into $\hat y=ax+b$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item Predict for $x=6$ hours:
        \begin{work}
          \hat y &= 1.5(6) + 2 \\
                 &= 9 + 2      \\
                 &= 11
        \end{work}
        The data ran from $x=1$ to $x=8$, so $x=6$ is \emph{inside} the data --- this is
        \blank{2.4cm} (interpolation / extrapolation).
  \item Predict for $x=12$ hours:
        \begin{work}
          \hat y &= 1.5(12) + 2 \\
                 &= 18 + 2      \\
                 &= 20
        \end{work}
        But only $15$ shots were attempted, so $20$ is \blank{2.2cm} (reasonable / unreasonable).
        Predicting \emph{outside} the data is \blank{2.4cm}, and the trend may break down.
  \item \textbf{Correlation $\ne$ causation.} In summer, ice-cream sales and pool drownings both rise, so
        $r>0$ --- but ice cream does not \emph{cause} drownings. The real cause (\emph{hot weather}) is a
        \blank{2.4cm} variable. A strong $r$ shows the variables \blank{2.4cm} together, not that one
        \blank{2.0cm} the other.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{practicebox}
A study relates \textbf{daily minutes of exercise} ($x$) to \textbf{resting heart rate in bpm} ($y$).
Technology gives $\hat y = -0.4x + 78$ with $r=-0.88$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item Is the association positive or negative, strong or weak? \blank{3.0cm}
  \item Interpret the slope $-0.4$ in context. \writelines{2}
  \item Predict the resting heart rate for $x=30$ minutes.
        \begin{work}
          \hat y &= -0.4(30) + 78 \\
                 &= -12 + 78      \\
                 &= 66
        \end{work}
  \item The data ran from $x=0$ to $x=45$ minutes. Is the $x=30$ prediction interpolation or extrapolation?
        \blank{2.6cm} \; Does a strong $r$ prove exercise \emph{causes} a lower heart rate? \blank{1.4cm}
\end{enumerate}
\end{practicebox}

No notes. Mistakes happen --- blanks don't.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=10pt]
  \item \textbf{Classify.} List \emph{every} set each number belongs to (natural, whole, integer,
        rational, irrational, real).

        \vspace{3pt}
        $\sqrt{36}$ \; is \ans{$6$: natural, whole, integer, rational, real}

        \vspace{6pt}
        $\sqrt{6}$ \; is \ans{irrational, real (only)}

  \item \textbf{Name the property} that allows each step.
        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.7}
        \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
        \hline
        \textbf{Step} & \textbf{Property} \\
        \hline
        (a) \; $7(x+3)=7x+21$ & \ans{distributive property} \\
        (b) \; $2x-9=5 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x-9+9=5+9$ & \ans{addition property of equality} \\
        (c) \; $4+(-4)=0$ & \ans{additive inverse property} \\
        (d) \; $6x=24 \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{6x}{6}=\dfrac{24}{6}$ & \ans{division property of equality} \\
        \hline
        \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}
        Two of those four changed only \emph{one} side and two changed \emph{both}. In one sentence,
        say how a property of real numbers differs from a property of equality.
        \ansline{A property of real numbers rewrites a single expression into an equal one (here (a)
        and (c)); a property of equality acts on \emph{both} sides at once to make a new equation
        with the same solutions (here (b) and (d)).}

  \item \textbf{SOL-style multiple choice.} What is the solution to $-5x \ge 20$?
        \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*., leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=1pt]
          \item $x \ge -4$
          \item $x \le -4$ \quad \textcolor{keyred}{\textbf{$\leftarrow$ correct}}
          \item $x \ge 4$
          \item $x \le 4$
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teachernote}
\textbf{Answer 3:} B. Divide both sides by $-5$; because $-5$ is negative, the symbol reverses, so
$x\le -4$. Each distractor breaks exactly one idea: \textbf{A} divides correctly but forgets to
reverse; \textbf{C} reverses nothing \emph{and} drops the sign; \textbf{D} reverses the symbol but
also wrongly makes the answer positive --- the over-correction of a student who has learned ``flip
everything.''

\textbf{Sort the collected tickets into four piles} to decide how Lesson 1.1 opens:
\emph{got it} / \emph{radical-is-irrational} (item 1: $\sqrt{36}$ called irrational, or listed in
only one set) / \emph{property names shaky} (item 2 blank or described rather than named) /
\emph{reversal rule} (item 3 answered A or D). \textbf{Act on the fourth pile.} Lesson 1.2 solves
inequalities for a full period, so if that pile is large, open 1.2 by re-running the Ana-and-Ben
Hook with fresh numbers before starting anything new. The second pile is the cheapest to clear ---
thirty seconds on ``evaluate first, classify second'' fixes nearly all of it.

\textbf{Item 2's sentence is the one to read.} A student who can state the one-side/both-sides
distinction in their own words has the takeaway of the lesson, even if a property name or two is
imprecise.
\end{teachernote}

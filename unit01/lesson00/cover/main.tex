\begin{remindbox}
\small The \textbf{real numbers} are everything on the number line, split into the
\textbf{rationals} (writable as $\tfrac{a}{b}$; decimals terminate or repeat) and the
\textbf{irrationals} (decimals that never terminate or repeat). A number belongs to \emph{every} set
that contains it, and how a number is \emph{written} never decides which sets those are --- only its
\emph{value} does, so $\sqrt{9}$ is a natural number and $\tfrac{8}{2}$ is an integer. Each set was
invented because the one before it was not \textbf{closed} under some operation.
\\[3pt]
Three families of rules govern everything you do to an expression or an equation. \textbf{Properties
of real numbers} (commutative, associative, distributive, identity, inverse, zero) rewrite
\textbf{one side} --- they change how something looks, not what it is. \textbf{Properties of
equality} act on \textbf{both sides at once} to produce a new equation with the same solutions;
that is what makes solving legal. \textbf{Properties of inequality} do the same for $<,\le,>,\ge$
with a single exception: multiplying or dividing both sides by a \textbf{negative} number
\emph{reverses} the symbol, because a negative multiplier reflects the number line through zero and
reflection reverses order. It does \emph{not} reverse when you subtract, and not merely because the
answer is negative. For the rest of this course, ``I moved it over'' is not a justification --- the
name of the property is.
\end{remindbox}

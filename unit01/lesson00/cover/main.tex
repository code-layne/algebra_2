\begin{remindbox}
\small A function is \textbf{linear} exactly when its \emph{rate of change is constant} --- the same
\textbf{slope} everywhere --- which is why its graph is a straight line. The \textbf{$y$-intercept}
$(0,b)$ is the output when $x=0$, the ``starting value''; the \textbf{zero} $(a,0)$ is the input
that makes the output $0$, and it is the only place a non-horizontal line can change sign. Keep two
questions apart: \emph{increasing / decreasing} is which way the graph \textbf{moves};
\emph{positive / negative} is where it \textbf{sits} relative to the $x$-axis. A line can be positive
and falling at the same time.
\end{remindbox}

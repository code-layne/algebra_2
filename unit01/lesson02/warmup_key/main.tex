\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Skills We'll Use Today}
\small Three skills, one from each of the last two lessons. Work quickly.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{1. Name the move (Lesson 1.0).} The work is done for you. Write the \emph{property} that
makes each line legal.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Line} & \textbf{Why is this allowed?} \\
\hline
$5(x-3)=2x+9$ & \emph{given} \\
$5x-15=2x+9$ & \ans{distributive property} \\
$3x-15=9$ & \ans{subtraction property of equality (subtract $2x$ from both sides)} \\
$3x=24$ & \ans{addition property of equality (add $15$ to both sides)} \\
$x=8$ & \ans{division property of equality (divide both sides by $3$)} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
``I moved it to the other side'' is not one of the answers. Every line has a \emph{name}.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{2. Factor completely (Lesson 1.1).}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l@{}}
(a) \; $x^2-5x-14=$ \ans{$(x-7)(x+2)$} &
(b) \; $3x^2-27=$ \ans{$3(x+3)(x-3)$} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Now finish this sentence, which you already believe: \\
If two numbers multiply to give \textbf{zero}, then \ans{the first} or \ans{the second} must be zero.
\\[2pt]
Does the same reasoning work if the two numbers multiply to give \textbf{eight}? \ans{No}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{3. Read the picture.} Each number line below shows a \emph{solution set}. Write the
inequality it represents.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}c c@{}}
(a) & (b) \\[2pt]
\numline{\draw[ultra thick, forest] (-1,0) -- (5.55,0);
         \draw[thick, forest, fill=white] (-1,0) circle (2.7pt);}
&
\numline{\draw[ultra thick, forest] (3,0) -- (-5.55,0);
         \fill[forest] (3,0) circle (2.7pt);}
\\[3pt]
$x$ \ans{$>-1$} & $x$ \ans{$\le 3$} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Roughly how many numbers are in solution set (a)? \ans{infinitely many} \\
That answer is exactly why we \emph{draw} a solution set instead of listing it.

\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
\small
\textbf{Item 1 is Lesson 1.0 being cashed in.} Refuse ``I moved it over'' out loud --- the whole
point of 1.0 was that each move has a name and the name is what makes it legal. If the room cannot
produce ``subtraction property of equality,'' spend two extra minutes here; the notes ask for the
same column three more times today.

\textbf{Item 2 delivers the zero product property without announcing it.} Students state it
themselves in the fill-in sentence, and the ``does it work for eight?'' question is the guard that
matters --- from $(x-2)(x+5)=8$ nothing follows, and that error appears every year. Say the reason:
zero is the only number with no way to be built from two nonzero factors.

\textbf{Item 3 motivates graphing.} Take the ``how many numbers'' answer publicly. Because the set
is infinite, no list can express it, so the picture is not decoration --- it is the only available
notation. Also flag the two circle conventions now: open for $<$ and $>$, closed for $\le$ and
$\ge$, since \textbf{A.EI.1c} grades that detail.
\end{teachernote}

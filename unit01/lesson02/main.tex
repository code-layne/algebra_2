\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=forestbg, colframe=forest, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students read $|x-h|$ as the \emph{distance} between $x$ and $h$,
    and use that meaning to \emph{create}, \emph{solve}, and \emph{interpret} absolute value
    equations and inequalities in one variable. They \emph{isolate} the bars before splitting into
    two cases, handle the $c=0$ and $c<0$ special cases from the distance meaning rather than from a
    memorised rule, distinguish $|X|<c$ (one interval, \emph{between}) from $|X|>c$ (two rays,
    \emph{outside}), and represent a solution set in set notation, in interval notation, and on a
    number line. Lesson~1.1 wrote lines that cross zero exactly \emph{once}; today's rule reaches
    the same value \emph{twice}.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} This lesson delivers the whole of \textbf{A2.EI.1} ---
    represent, solve, and interpret absolute value equations and inequalities in one variable:
    \textbf{A2.EI.1a} (create an absolute value \emph{equation} to model a context),
    \textbf{A2.EI.1b} (solve an absolute value equation algebraically and verify),
    \textbf{A2.EI.1c} (create an absolute value \emph{inequality} to model a context),
    \textbf{A2.EI.1d} (solve an absolute value inequality and represent the solution set with set
    notation, interval notation, and a number line), and \textbf{A2.EI.1e} (verify solutions, and
    explain and interpret solutions in context).
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Lesson model:} \emph{Gradual release --- I do, we do, you do --- all of it inside the
    guided notes.} There is no group activity. The notes name the vocabulary as they teach it and
    deliver the instruction in \textbf{two} sections on one context (a radio tower on a parkway);
    the Guided Practice is worked together with students holding the pen; \textbf{the homework is
    the individual practice}, and students start it in class while you circulate. The whole-class
    debrief is \emph{spoken} --- there is no practice set and no reflection box on the notes page.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Ideas \& Skills} & \textbf{Key Understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Read $|x-h|$ as the \textbf{distance} between $x$ and $h$.
  \item \textbf{Isolate} the bars, then apply the two-case rule.
  \item Solve $|X|=c$ for $c>0$, $c=0$, and $c<0$.
  \item Solve $|X|<c$ (\emph{and}) and $|X|>c$ (\emph{or}).
  \item Write a set three ways: set, interval, number line.
  \item Model a ``within so much of'' context and interpret the answer.
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item A distance is never negative, and \emph{two} points sit the same distance from a
        centre --- one on each side. That single fact generates every rule today.
  \item You cannot split until the bars stand alone, because the two-case rule is a statement
        about $|X|$, not about the equation around it.
  \item \textbf{Target misconception:} that $\le$ and $\ge$ produce ``nearly the same'' answer.
        They produce \emph{opposite} sets --- one interval versus two rays. The mile-3 hiker
        (distance 9) is in exactly one of them.
  \item Set, interval, and number-line notation are three spellings of one set; the endpoint
        symbol ($[$ vs.\ $($, filled vs.\ open) is the only thing carrying inclusion.
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems --- taught directly in the guided notes}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Absolute value as distance} & $|x-h|$ is how far $x$ is from $h$ on the number line; never negative. \\
\textbf{Two-case rule} & $|X|=c$ with $c>0$ splits into $X=c$ or $X=-c$. $c=0$ gives one solution; $c<0$ gives none. Isolate first. \\
\textbf{``And'' vs.\ ``or'' inequality} & $|X|<c$ becomes $-c<X<c$ (one interval, \emph{between}); $|X|>c$ becomes $X<-c$ or $X>c$ (two rays, \emph{outside}). \\
\textbf{Interval notation} & $[7,17]$; brackets include an endpoint, parentheses exclude it. Next to $\pm\infty$, always a parenthesis; two rays need $\cup$. \\
\textbf{No solution / all reals} & $|X|=c$ or $|X|<c$ with $c<0$ has none; $|X|\ge c$ with $c<0$ holds for every $x$. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Warm-Up --- Activate Prior Knowledge (5 min)]{forestbg}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{The three items and what each seeds}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{1.} $|-7|$, $|4|$, $|-3|+|5|$, $-|-6|$, then \emph{how many numbers have
              absolute value 7?} \textbf{Seeds:} the bars measure size, and that question is the
              two-case rule in one line.
        \item \textbf{2.} Distances read off a number line and written with bars --- $|11-3|$,
              $|6-(-2)|$, and finally the open $|x-6|$. \textbf{Seeds:} the exact move notes
              section~1 formalises.
        \item \textbf{3.} Shade $x\ge-1$ and ``between $-3$ and $2$.'' \textbf{Seeds:} filled vs.\
              open dots, and the closing question --- \emph{could a shaded picture ever come in
              two separate pieces?} --- which section~2 answers.
    \end{itemize}
\end{minipage}

\begin{skillbox}[Guided Notes \& Practice --- I do, then we do (34 min)]{forestbg}
\textbf{This is the whole lesson.} There is no group activity, and there is no independent
practice set on this page: \textbf{the homework is the individual practice}, started in class.
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{Hook (2 min).} ``A parkway marked in miles, $0$ to $20$. A radio tower at \textbf{mile
12}; a handheld radio reaches it from \textbf{within 5 miles}. Two hikers stand exactly 5 miles
out --- where are they?'' (Miles $7$ and $17$.) Then the question that carries the lesson:
\textbf{``Why are there two of them, and not one?''} Get the picture and both answers on the
board, then say it: \emph{every question today is this picture --- how far is $x$ from the
tower, and is that close enough?}

\textbf{I do (about 18 min).} Two sections, each in two moves; students fill the blanks as each
idea is named, and the vocabulary box is filled \emph{as you go}, never in advance.

\emph{Section 1} names $|x-h|$ as a distance and works the first \texttt{work} block:
$|x-12|=5$ splits to $x=17$ or $x=7$ --- the hook's own markers, falling out of the algebra.
Verify both by substitution; that substitution \emph{is} what A2.EI.1b means by verifying. Name
the \textbf{two-case rule} against it. Five minutes. Its second half, \emph{Isolate the Bars
Before You Split}, fills the three-row table ($c>0$, $c=0$, $c<0$) off the distance meaning,
then works $3|x+1|-2=10$ and lands the sentence that matters: \emph{splitting at the first line
gives $3x+1-2=10$ --- one answer, and a wrong one.} Four minutes.

\textbf{Section 2 is the lesson.} Its first half is the two-column table on the \emph{same}
$|x-12|$ and the \emph{same} $5$: $\le$ shades $7\le x\le17$ as one piece, $\ge$ shades two rays
--- and the three notations are read straight off the two pictures, set, interval, and shaded
line, with the endpoint rule and the $\cup$. Five minutes. Its second half is the crux: the
gold box tests the hiker at \textbf{mile 3}, distance $9$. Is $9\le5$? No. Is $9\ge5$? Yes.
\emph{The wrong symbol does not shift the answer a little; it hands you the opposite set.} Say
it, write it, and point at the two number lines while you do. Close on the odd cases read off
the same picture --- a distance less than $-2$ (none) and at least $-2$ (all of them). Four
minutes.

\columnbreak
\textbf{We do (about 14 min).} The Guided Practice box: $2|x-5|-3=7$, isolated and split to
$x=10$ or $x=0$, then the \emph{same} problem with $\le$ and with $\ge$, each written as a
solution, an interval, and a shaded line. Students hold the pen; you hold the questions:
\emph{``What is stuck to the bars? Undo that first.''} \quad \emph{``Closer than or farther
than? So between, or outside?''} \quad \emph{``Does your picture have a gap in the middle? Then
it needs two intervals and a $\cup$.''} Part (c) is the crux transferred and the part to protect
when time is short.

\textbf{The pen must actually change hands.} Guided Practice is the only place today where you
can watch a student decide, so do not narrate it. Put part (a) on them cold, take a hand count
on \emph{one piece or two} in part (b) before anyone speaks, and make the room defend both
answers.

Circulate during Guided Practice and demand the reason, not the word:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{Part (a):} ``You have one answer. The bars measure a distance --- where is the
        other side?''
  \item \textbf{Part (b):} ``Why a bracket at $0$ and at $10$? Which symbol told you?''
  \item \textbf{Part (c), the crux transferred:} ``Shade it. Is there a gap? Then why is your
        answer written as one chain?''
\end{itemize}
While circulating, pick two papers to put up in the debrief --- one part (c) written correctly
as two rays with a $\cup$, and one written as a single chain like $10\le x\le0$.
\textbf{Your formative read comes twice}: here, and again in the last thirteen minutes as
students start the homework.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Debrief --- whole class (8 min)]{forestbg}
Pens down, papers up. \textbf{This debrief is spoken} --- there is no box at the end of the
notes to fill in, so it lives entirely on the board and in the room.
\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em, nosep, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Put parts (b) and (c) back up side by side} with their number lines, and make a
        student say which is \emph{closer than} and which is \emph{farther than} --- and point at
        the piece, or the gap, that earned it. Then state it plainly: \emph{closer than gives one
        connected piece; farther than gives two.}
  \item Put up the two part (c)s you picked. The single chain $10\le x\le0$ reads as
        \emph{empty}; make the room shade it and see the contradiction. That is the moment the
        union earns its place.
  \item Take the split-too-early error: ask who solved $2|x-5|-3=7$ by writing $2x-5-3=7$, and
        show what they got. One answer, and a wrong one --- section~1's sentence, back again.
  \item Take the bracket-on-$\infty$ error last: \emph{``Can you reach $\infty$? Then it never
        gets a bracket.''}
\end{enumerate}
\columnbreak
\textbf{Two things to demand aloud.} First, \textbf{make a student say the mile-3 hiker's
sentence without the notes} --- distance $9$, so not in range, so in exactly one of the two
answers. Second, \textbf{make them say what $|x-h|$ means in words} --- how far $x$ is from $h$
--- because tomorrow's V-shaped graph is that sentence drawn.

\textbf{If the debrief runs short}, cut item~4 and item~3 --- item~1 is the only one that cannot
be cut. \textbf{Do not let the debrief eat the last thirteen minutes} --- the homework start is
not optional padding, it is the individual practice.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Homework --- scored, due next class]{goldbox}
Six items spanning the whole of A2.EI.1: \textbf{1} two equations, the second needing isolation
first; \textbf{2} the deliberate contrast pair ($|x+3|<4$ against $|x+3|>4$) with the ``why one
piece, why two'' question; \textbf{3} \emph{reading an inequality off a shaded number line} ---
the only item that runs backwards, and the best single predictor of whether the distance meaning
stuck (find the centre $3$ and the reach $4$ first); \textbf{4} the special cases plus the
$k\le0$ boundary; \textbf{5} a thermostat tolerance model with an interpret-the-answer follow-up;
\textbf{6} an SOL-style multiple-choice item on $|x+2|>3$, the formative check.

\textbf{Start it in the last thirteen minutes of the period, in class and alone.} Launch item~1
aloud, then circulate: \textbf{item~1(b) is the isolation check} --- expect $5x+2-1=14$ from
anyone who did not internalise section~1 --- and \textbf{item~2 is where the misconception
shows}. Sort what you see into three piles as you walk --- two different-shaped answers with the
reason (ready); two different shapes with no reason (ready, needs the language); the same shape
for both, or a single chain for (b) (the misconception survived) --- and open Lesson~1.3 with a
one-minute re-look at ``less th\emph{AND}'' versus ``great\emph{OR}'' if that third pile ran past
a third of the room. \textbf{Item~6 carries the check when you score it:} (A) is the $<$ version
(the target misconception), (C) keeps one ray and forgets the other side, (D) confuses $>3$ with
$\ge-3$.

\textbf{Two pages is a ceiling at 12pt}, so the old extension items (building $|x-10|\le6$ from
its endpoints; why miles $7$ and $17$ belong to both answers) were cut for fit --- use them as
cold-call prompts in the debrief if time allows. \textbf{DeltaMath override:} well covered
there --- swap in a DeltaMath set on absolute value equations and inequalities if you prefer
auto-graded practice; that replaces this page and strikes its score cell on the cover. Never
assign both. \textbf{Preview:} Lesson~1.3, \emph{Absolute Value Functions \& Transformations} ---
today you \emph{solved} $|ax+b|=c$; next you \emph{graph} $y=|ax+b|$, and the two solutions you
found become the two places a V crosses a horizontal line.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Watch For (while circulating)]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{Splitting before the bars are alone} --- $3|x+1|-2=10$ becoming $3x+1-2=10$
        (notes~1, GP~(a), HW~1b) --- the single most common error today. Probe: \emph{``What is
        stuck to the bars? What is $|x+1|$ by itself?''}
  \item \textbf{Treating $\le$ and $\ge$ as nearly the same answer} (notes~2, GP~(b)--(c), HW~2,
        HW~6(A)) --- the target misconception. Send them to the mile-3 hiker, not to the rule.
        Probe: \emph{``Closer than, or farther than? So between, or outside?''}
  \item Giving only the positive case and stopping at one answer (Warm-Up~1, notes~1, HW~1a).
        Probe: \emph{``Where is the other side of the tower?''}
  \item \textbf{An ``or'' answer written as a single chain}, $10\le x\le0$ (GP~(c), HW~2b) ---
        make them shade it and see the gap.
  \item Solving $|x-4|=-3$ by dropping the bars to get $x=1$ (HW~4a). Probe: \emph{``How far is
        $-3$? Can a distance be that?''}
  \item Bracketing $\infty$, or dividing only part of a compound inequality (notes~2, GP~(b)).
        Probe: \emph{``Whatever you do to the middle, you do to all three parts.''}
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts: ``How far is that from the centre?'' ``One piece or two --- convince me from
the picture.'' ``Is that endpoint in or out? Which symbol says so?''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Close \& Start Homework (13 min)]{goldbox}
\textbf{Students start the homework here, in class, alone and silent --- this is the individual
practice, and the first minutes of it are your best formative read.} Hand the launch first ---
six items on paper, scored, due next class --- and work item~1(a) aloud in thirty seconds so

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Item~1's follow-up --- \emph{how many different numbers have an absolute value of 7?} --- is the
whole lesson in one question; if the room says ``one,'' you have found your hook and section~1 will
land hard. Item~2's last blank, $|x-6|$, is the first time they write a distance with a
\emph{variable} in it, and it is exactly the expression notes section~1 needs; do not rush past it.
Item~3's closing question is deliberately left unresolved --- \emph{could a shaded picture ever come
in two separate pieces?} Let them answer ``no'' or shrug, and do \emph{not} correct it. Notes
section~2 pays it off, and the surprise is worth more than the five seconds you would save. The
page is 12pt like every other student component and fits one side, blank and keyed.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes \& Practice]
\textbf{This one document is the lesson --- pace it 2 / 18 / 14, then 8 for the debrief, then 13
for the homework start.} Section~1 must move: five minutes on the distance meaning and the
$|x-12|=5$ split, four on isolating. \textbf{Do not cut the ``splitting at the first line''
sentence} --- Guided Practice (a) and homework 1(b) both target it.

\textbf{Spend the time in section~2}, which carries the misconception. Fill the two-column table
with the room on the \emph{same} $|x-12|$ and the \emph{same} $5$, and read the three notations
straight off the two pictures before naming anything --- the shaded line is the evidence the
bracket and the $\cup$ are read off. Then the gold box: the hiker at mile~3, distance~9. Let
someone say ``$9\le5$, sure'' and let the room catch it. Do not resolve it by repeating the rule.
Point at the two number lines.

\textbf{Do not skip the odd cases} at the end of section~2 --- $|x-12|<-2$ and $|x-12|\ge-2$
read off the picture, no algebra. Without them, homework 4 becomes a guessing game and the
vocabulary box's last row stays empty.

\textbf{Guided Practice is where the pen changes hands.} Part (a) is the isolation check, part
(b) is the ``and,'' part (c) is the ``or'' --- the same expression three times so that the only
thing changing is the symbol. If you only get through (a) and (c), that is the right trade.

\textbf{There is no independent practice set on this page, and that is deliberate --- the
homework is the individual practice.} Guided Practice is the last thing on the page; the period
ends with students starting the homework in front of you, which is where your second formative
read comes from. Three \texttt{work} blocks are printed here and two on the homework; the key
prints the same steps in the same space, so the two files paginate alike.

\textbf{Debrief (8 min), spoken.} Parts (b) and (c) side by side, with a student pointing at the
piece or the gap; it is the one move that cannot be cut. \textbf{Do not let the debrief eat the
last thirteen minutes} --- the homework start is not optional padding.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
\textbf{Scored, due next class.} The thirteen minutes at the end of the period are a \emph{start},
not a completion: put students on 1 and 2 while you can still catch a wrong start, and let 3--6
go home. Item~1(b) is the isolation check --- expect $5x+2-1=14$ from anyone who did not
internalise section~1. Item~3 is the only item that runs \emph{backwards}, from a picture to an
inequality, and it is the best single predictor of whether the distance meaning stuck: they must
find the centre ($3$) and the reach ($4$) before they can write $|x-3|\le4$. Item~4(c) is the trap
--- $k=0$ is \emph{included} in the no-solution answer, because ``distance $<0$'' is still
impossible; students who write $k<0$ have the idea but not the boundary. \textbf{When you score
the page, sort item~6 into three piles}: chose B (got it); chose A (solved the $<$ version --- the
target misconception, still alive); chose C or D (kept one ray, or confused $>3$ with $\ge-3$).
If more than a third land outside B, open Lesson~1.3 with a one-minute re-look at ``less
th\emph{AND}'' versus ``great\emph{OR}'' rather than letting it ride. Two \texttt{work} blocks
(items 1b and 5b).
\end{teachernote}

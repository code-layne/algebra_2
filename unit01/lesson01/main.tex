\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=forestbg, colframe=forest, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students can compute \emph{slope} as a rate of change from a graph
    or from any two points, \emph{write} the equation of a line from a graph, from two points, or
    from a slope and a point, and move fluently among the three algebraic forms ---
    \emph{slope-intercept} $y=mx+b$, \emph{point-slope} $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$, and \emph{standard}
    $Ax+By=C$ --- recognising that all three describe the \emph{same line}. They read a line as a
    transformation of the parent $y=x$ (a stretch or reflection changes the slope; a vertical shift
    changes the $y$-intercept), and identify parallel, perpendicular, horizontal, and vertical
    cases. Lesson~1.0 \emph{read} these features off a graph; today students \emph{produce} the
    equation.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} the Algebra~1 linear cluster \textbf{A.F.1}, reactivated as
    the foundation of Unit~1: \textbf{A.F.1a} (slope and intercepts as rate of change),
    \textbf{A.F.1b} (transformations of the parent $y=x$ change slope and $y$-intercept),
    \textbf{A.F.1c} (write and interpret slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard forms),
    \textbf{A.F.1d} (write the equation of a line from a graph, from two points, or from a slope
    and a point), and \textbf{A.F.1e} (parallel and perpendicular lines). It launches the
    transformational lens that \textbf{A2.F.1} extends to every later function family.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Lesson model:} \emph{Experience first, formalize later.} Students work the activity in
    small groups using only prior knowledge; the teacher circulates with questions, cues, and
    prompts (not answers); the debrief attaches the formal vocabulary to what the groups already
    found; an application is worked together; and Check Your Understanding applies it to new
    contexts. The four parts are one student document, \textbf{Experience \& Formalize}. No
    separate direct-instruction block, and \textbf{no homework} --- Check Your Understanding is the
    lesson's whole practice set and is \emph{not scored}.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Learning Targets \& Key Understandings]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Learning targets (student language)} & \textbf{Key understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item I can find the \textbf{slope} from a graph or from any two points.
  \item I can \textbf{write} a line's equation from a graph, from two points, or from a slope and
        a point --- even when the starting value is not given.
  \item I can rewrite a line in \textbf{slope-intercept}, \textbf{point-slope}, and
        \textbf{standard} form, and show the three are the same line.
  \item I can say how changing $m$ or $b$ transforms the parent line $y=x$, and identify
        \textbf{parallel}, \textbf{perpendicular}, horizontal, and vertical lines.
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Any two points give the same slope, because a line's rate of change is \emph{constant} ---
        the defining property from Lesson~1.0.
  \item Point-slope exists because the $y$-intercept is often the one point you \emph{do not} have.
  \item \textbf{Target misconception:} that two equations which \emph{look} different must be
        different lines. Priya's $D-24 = 2(n-3)$ and the group's $D = 2n+18$ are the same rule ---
        this is the crux of the activity and the reason the three forms are taught together.
  \item Every non-vertical line is $y=x$ after a stretch/reflection (slope) and a vertical shift
        (intercept); $x=a$ is the one line no $y=mx+b$ can express.
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Slope / rate of change} & \TallMath{m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}}: the constant change in output per unit change in input. \\
\textbf{Slope-intercept form} & $y=mx+b$: $m$ is the slope, $(0,b)$ the $y$-intercept. Reads off a rate and a starting value. \\
\textbf{Point-slope form} & $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$: built directly from a slope and \emph{any} known point $(x_1,y_1)$. \\
\textbf{Standard form} & $Ax+By=C$ with $A,B,C$ integers and $A\ge0$; convenient for intercepts. \\
\textbf{Parent function} & The simplest line $y=x$ (slope $1$, through the origin); every non-vertical line is a transformation of it. \\
\textbf{Transformation} & $kf(x)$ stretches/reflects (changes the slope); $f(x)+k$ shifts vertically (changes the $y$-intercept). \\
\textbf{Parallel / perpendicular} & Equal slopes / opposite-reciprocal slopes ($m_1m_2=-1$). \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{fixedskillbox}[Warm-Up --- Activate Prior Knowledge]{forestbg}
    The Warm-Up rehearses exactly what the activity leans on, without naming anything.
    \textbf{(1)} Counting steps between two marked points on a graphed line --- ``3 right, 4 up, so
    $\tfrac43$ up per step'' --- is the seed of \emph{slope from two points}; the follow-up
    (``where does it cross the vertical axis?'') seeds the $y$-intercept and is deliberately the
    awkward $-\tfrac73$, so the picture alone will not give it. \textbf{(2)} Solving $2x+3y=12$ and
    $5x-y=8$ for $y$ seeds \emph{standard $\to$ slope-intercept}. \textbf{(3)} Distributing
    $y-5=3(x-2)$ and $y+1=-2(x+4)$ and tidying seeds \emph{point-slope $\to$ slope-intercept} ---
    and is the exact algebra Priya's rule will need in 2d.
    \textbf{Debrief items 2 and 3 aloud, briefly}, and land only this: \emph{two equations that
    looked nothing alike turned into something much easier to read.} Do \emph{not} name any of the
    three forms yet --- the activity has to surface the idea first.
\end{fixedskillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Experience \& Formalize --- The Activity: \emph{Two Receipts}]{forestbg}
\textbf{Launch (2 min).} ``Two climbing gyms charge the same way --- something to walk in the door,
then a price per climb. One of them shows you a graph. The other one hands you two receipts.''
Groups of 3--4, one packet each but one shared conversation. Tell them: \emph{``Use what you already
know --- rates, tables, solving. For every number you write down, be ready to show me where it came
from.''}

\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{What students do.} Part~1 (Summit) is the easy case and builds the toolkit: read the
walk-in fee off the vertical axis, get \$3 per climb from $(0,6)$ to $(4,18)$, assemble
$C(n)=3n+6$, and check it against the picture at $n=7$. Part~2 (Basecamp) removes the graph and the
starting value: two receipts (3 climbs \$24, 7 climbs \$32) give \$2 per climb, and groups must
work \emph{backwards} to the \$18 walk-in fee before they can write $D(n)=2n+18$.

Then the \textbf{crux, 2d}: Priya never found the walk-in fee --- she wrote $D-24=2(n-3)$ straight
off the receipt she had. \emph{Is that the same rule or a different one?} Groups test $n=7$ (both
give \$32) and expand hers to $2n+18$. 2e adds the cheaper-gym comparison that has to switch
somewhere ($n=12$), and 2f asks for Basecamp's two numbers relative to Summit --- no drawing.

\columnbreak
\textbf{What the teacher does.} Circulate. Listen before speaking. When a group stalls, give a
\emph{question, cue, or prompt}, not an answer:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep]
  \item 1b: ``Put a finger on $(0,6)$ and another on $(4,18)$. How far up, and over how many
        climbs? Now do one climb.''
  \item 2b: ``You know 3 climbs and the fee together cost \$24. What do the three climbs cost on
        their own? So what is left?''
  \item 2d: \emph{Do not settle it.} ``Try $n=7$ in both. Now try $n=10$. Would you bet they always
        agree? What would convince you?''
  \item 2e: ``Which gym is winning at $n=2$? At $n=15$? Somewhere in between, what happened?''
\end{itemize}
While circulating, pick groups to put on the board for the debrief: a clean Part~1 rule, a group's
\emph{backwards} work for the \$18, and --- essential --- a group that has expanded Priya's rule.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Debrief --- Formalize (what goes on the board, in a second color)]{forestbg}
Work through the displayed student work in order, and \textbf{attach each formal name to something a
group already wrote}. Students record each line in their QuickNotes as you go.
\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em, nosep, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{The per-climb price is the slope.} Over the group's ``\$12 over 4 climbs'' write
        $m=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$. Ask why \emph{any} two points work,
        and answer with Lesson~1.0: the rate is constant.
  \item \textbf{$C(n)=3n+6$ is slope-intercept form}, $y=mx+b$ --- a rate and a starting value.
        Label $m$ and $b$ on their own rule.
  \item \textbf{Priya's rule is point-slope form}, $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$. This is the move of the
        lesson: display her rule beside the group's, then expand hers \emph{line by line} to
        $D=2n+18$. Say it out loud --- \emph{same line, different outfit} --- and name why the form
        exists: the $y$-intercept is often the one point you do not have.
  \item \textbf{Standard form} $Ax+By=C$: pose it yourself, since the activity has no example.
        Take $D=2n+18$ to $-2n+D=18$, then $2n-D=-18$; integers, $A\ge0$, and the intercepts fall
        straight out.
  \item \textbf{Transforming the parent.} Put $y=x$ and $y=2x-4$ on one grid: multiplying by $2$
        made it steeper (slope), subtracting $4$ slid it down (intercept). Every non-vertical line
        is $y=x$ after those two moves --- the lens Algebra~2 will reuse on every family.
  \item \textbf{The four special cases.} Parallel (equal slopes) and perpendicular (opposite
        reciprocals) from 2f's ``less steep''; then horizontal $y=c$ with slope $0$, and vertical
        $x=a$ with \emph{undefined} slope --- the one line $y=mx+b$ cannot write.
\end{enumerate}
\columnbreak
\textbf{QuickNotes.} Fill the packet's box together as you go, on $y=2x-4$ against the parent
$y=x$. Keep it to the printed lines --- it is a summary of what the groups discovered, not a second
lesson.

\textbf{Why this order.} Slope-intercept comes first because the groups built it themselves; the
other two forms then arrive as answers to real problems the activity raised (``I never found the
fee,'' ``I want integers''). Standard form and the parent-graph comparison are the two pieces the
activity deliberately does \emph{not} carry, so they are posed cold here --- late, and briefly.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Application --- \emph{The Drone Descent} (worked together, 7 min)]{forestbg}
Straight out of QuickNotes and into a model, with the class holding the pen: a drone at $46$~m when
$t=2$ and $30$~m when $t=6$. Item~1 gets $m=-4$ in a \texttt{work} block and asks what the negative
means. Item~2 does the rest of the conversion in one go: point-slope from $(2,46)$, expanded to
$h=-4t+54$ --- and the interpretation matters, $54$~m is where the descent \emph{started}, a moment
nobody measured --- then standard form, $4t+h=54$.

\textbf{Item~3 is the point of the box.} Same rate, but starting from $60$~m: $b$ changes, $m$ does
not. Ask it in that order --- \emph{``Which number is the rate? Which is the start? So which one
moved?''} A student who changes $m$ is still fusing the two roles; send them to the graph (a shift
up, not a tilt), not to the algebra.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Check Your Understanding --- the lesson's practice, \emph{not scored}]{redbox}
Six items in the packet, new contexts. \textbf{There is no homework:} this is the lesson's entire
practice set, done in class, and the student keeps the packet. \textbf{These carry no point value ---
do not collect them for a grade} (the cover's score column reads \textbf{NA}); spot-check instead.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item[\textbf{1.}] the core procedure --- slope from two points, three times, the last two being
        the horizontal ($0$) and vertical (undefined) cases;
  \item[\textbf{2.}] the \emph{contrast pair}: one graphed line written slope-intercept \emph{and}
        point-slope, then a \texttt{work} block showing they are equal --- Priya again, on paper;
  \item[\textbf{3.}] converting $y+2=-\tfrac34(x-4)$ through slope-intercept to standard, fractions
        and all;
  \item[\textbf{4.}] the transformation the activity dropped ($y=x \to y=-3x+2$) plus its boundary:
        of $y=4$ and $x=4$, only $x=4$ has no $y=mx+b$, and the reason is that there is no slope
        to supply;
  \item[\textbf{5.}] a model in a fresh context --- a candle, $17$~cm at $1$~h and $11$~cm at
        $4$~h --- write it, interpret the intercept, predict, and find when it burns out;
  \item[\textbf{6.}] an SOL-style multiple-choice item on perpendicular slopes.
\end{itemize}
Pairs for 1--3, then solo for 4--6. \textbf{Item~6 is the formative check:} sort responses into
``chose B (got it) / chose A (used the same slope --- parallel, not perpendicular) / chose C (flipped
the sign only) / chose D (took the reciprocal only)'' to decide whether Lesson~1.2 opens with a
one-minute re-look at opposite reciprocals. Ten minutes will not cover six items for everyone:
\textbf{priority is 1, 2, 5, 6} (procedure, the crux again, model, check); \textbf{3 and 4 are the
early-finisher bank}.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Watch For (while circulating)]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item computing slope as $\frac{x_2-x_1}{y_2-y_1}$, or subtracting the coordinates in a different
        order top and bottom (Warm-Up~1, CYU~1);
  \item in 2b, dividing \$24 by 3 to ``get the price per climb'' --- that folds the walk-in fee
        into the rate; push them back to the \emph{difference} between the two receipts;
  \item \textbf{2d: declaring Priya wrong because her equation looks different} --- the lesson's
        target misconception. Make them expand it, not vote on it;
  \item reading $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$ as needing the $y$-intercept for $(x_1,y_1)$ (CYU~2b);
  \item giving $x=4$ a slope of $0$ and $y=4$ an undefined one --- exactly backwards (CYU~4b);
  \item calling $y=\tfrac12x-2$ perpendicular to $y=\tfrac12x-4$ because ``the numbers changed''
        (CYU~6).
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts: ``Where did that number come from --- which two points?'' ``Same line or a
different line? Convince me.'' ``Which number is the rate and which is the start?''
\end{skillbox}

\textbf{Nothing is assigned.} Say it out loud --- there is no homework in this course; the practice
was Check Your Understanding and the students keep the packet. \textbf{What changed today:} they
walked in able to \emph{read} a line's features off a graph and walked out able to \emph{write} the
line themselves --- from a graph, from two points, or from a slope and any point at all --- and to
recognise the same line wearing three different outfits. \textbf{Preview:} Lesson~1.2,
\emph{Absolute Value Equations \& Inequalities} --- every line today crossed zero exactly once;
next comes a rule that reaches the same value \emph{twice}, because it measures distance.
\end{skillbox}

% ── Teacher notes — one per component, in packet order ──
\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Item~1's ``where does it cross the vertical axis?'' is deliberately $-\tfrac73$ --- ugly, and not
readable off the picture. Do not resolve it in the warm-up; let a group grumble about it, because
that grumble is the argument for point-slope form in 2d. Debrief only items 2 and 3, and only the
observation that two different-looking equations tidied into something readable. Five minutes; if it
runs long, drop the second equation in each of items 2 and 3.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Experience \& Formalize]
\textbf{Activity.} Part~1 should take about 7 of the 20 minutes --- it is the easy case and the
groups will move fast; protect the remaining 13 for Part~2, where the real work is. If a group
finishes early, ask them for Basecamp's rule \emph{starting from the other receipt} ($D-32=2(n-7)$)
and whether that one also matches. \textbf{Debrief.} The must-land moment is 2d: expand Priya's rule
line by line beside the group's, and say ``same line, different outfit'' out loud. If time is short,
cut the standard-form derivation to a single displayed line and let students finish that QuickNotes
bullet from the slides --- do \emph{not} cut the parent-graph comparison, since Unit~2 opens on it.
\textbf{Application.} Seven minutes: ask what the negative rate means before anyone computes, let a
student drive the \texttt{work} block, and save two minutes for item~3 (start from $60$~m) --- it is
the concept check, not a bonus. \textbf{Check Your Understanding.} Four \texttt{work} blocks
in this half of the packet (Application~1 and~2, CYU~2 and~3 --- the activity's 2b and 2d make six in
all) --- students solve in the reserved space and the key prints the steps in the same space, so the
two files paginate alike. Priority items are 1, 2, 5, 6; 3 and 4 are
the early-finisher bank. Item~6 is the formative check. \textbf{Not scored} --- spot-check item~6
rather than collecting the page.
\end{teachernote}

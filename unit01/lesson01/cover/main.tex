\begin{remindbox}
\small A \textbf{linear function} can be written three equivalent ways --- the \emph{same line in
different outfits}. \textbf{Slope-intercept} $y=mx+b$ shows the rate $m$ and the starting value
$b$; \textbf{point-slope} $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$ is built to use a slope and \emph{any} point;
\textbf{standard} $Ax+By=C$ (integers, $A\ge 0$) is tidy for intercepts. To \emph{write} a line you
need a \textbf{slope} and \textbf{one point} --- and two points give you the slope. To \emph{graph}
one, start from the parent $y=x$ and apply a vertical \emph{stretch/reflection} (that sets the
slope) and a vertical \emph{shift} (that sets the $y$-intercept).
\end{remindbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small \textbf{Slope} is $m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$, and \emph{any} two points on the line give the
same answer. One line can be written three ways: \textbf{slope-intercept} $y=mx+b$ hands you the rate
and the starting value; \textbf{point-slope} $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$ is built from a slope and \emph{any}
point --- useful exactly when the $y$-intercept is the number you were never given; and
\textbf{standard} $Ax+By=C$ is the tidy integer version. Two equations that look different can be the
same line: expand one and see. Every non-vertical line is the parent $y=x$ after a slope change and a
vertical shift. \textbf{Parallel} lines have equal slopes; \textbf{perpendicular} slopes are opposite
reciprocals ($m_1m_2=-1$).
\end{remindbox}

\parthead{Part D --- Extended Response (12 pts)}
Explain your reasoning in full sentences.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=16pt]
  \item \textbf{Three families, six questions.} Each table below is generated by a linear, a
        quadratic, or an exponential rule.
        \begin{center}
        \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
        \begin{tabular}{@{}c@{\hspace{1.1cm}}c@{\hspace{1.1cm}}c@{}}
        \begin{tabular}{@{}c|c@{}}
          $x$ & $A(x)$ \\ \hline
          $0$ & $5$ \\ $1$ & $8$ \\ $2$ & $11$ \\ $3$ & $14$ \\
        \end{tabular}
        &
        \begin{tabular}{@{}c|c@{}}
          $x$ & $B(x)$ \\ \hline
          $0$ & $3$ \\ $1$ & $6$ \\ $2$ & $12$ \\ $3$ & $24$ \\
        \end{tabular}
        &
        \begin{tabular}{@{}c|c@{}}
          $x$ & $C(x)$ \\ \hline
          $0$ & $1$ \\ $1$ & $2$ \\ $2$ & $5$ \\ $3$ & $10$ \\
        \end{tabular}
        \\
        \end{tabular}
        \end{center}
        \textbf{(a)} Name the family of each table and give the numerical evidence --- constant
        difference, constant \emph{second} difference, or constant ratio.
        \textbf{(b)} Write a rule for each function.
        \textbf{(c)} Which function is largest at $x=1$? At $x=5$? Show the values.
        \textbf{(d)} A classmate says ``$B$ is just the biggest one.'' Use your answers to (c) to
        explain why ``which is bigger'' is not a fair question until someone says \emph{where}.
        \textbf{(e)} If $B$ modeled a population of bacteria in hours, what domain would make sense,
        and why is that narrower than the domain of the rule by itself?
        \vspace{5.6cm}

  \item \textbf{Modeling and solution sets.} Two climbing gyms price a day pass differently.
        \\[3pt]
        \hspace*{1.2em}\textbf{Summit:} a one-time \$40 membership fee, then \$12 per visit.
        \\[2pt]
        \hspace*{1.2em}\textbf{Basecamp:} no fee, \$22 per visit.
        \\[4pt]
        \textbf{(a)} Write a function for the total cost at each gym after $v$ visits.
        \textbf{(b)} Find the number of visits at which the two costs are equal. Show every step,
        and name the property of equality that justifies each one.
        \textbf{(c)} Verify your answer by substituting it into \emph{both} functions.
        \textbf{(d)} Which gym is cheaper for $2$ visits? For $10$ visits? Give the costs.
        \textbf{(e)} Suppose Basecamp changed its pricing to a \$40 fee plus \$12 per visit. Setting
        the two costs equal would give $12v+40=12v+40$. What is the solution set, what is that kind
        of equation called, and what does it mean about the two gyms?
        \textbf{(f)} What domain makes sense for $v$ in this situation, and why?
        \vspace{5.4cm}
\end{enumerate}

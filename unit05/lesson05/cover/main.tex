\begin{learningtargetbox}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item \ldots{} \textbf{factor} a rational function completely, list its restrictions from the
        \emph{original} denominator, and use the \textbf{cancel test} to sort each one into a
        \textbf{hole} or a \textbf{vertical asymptote}.
  \item \ldots{} find the \textbf{horizontal asymptote} by comparing the degree of the whole top with
        the degree of the whole bottom, and say ``none'' when the top wins.
  \item \ldots{} \textbf{compute} the intercepts --- $x$-intercepts from the zeros of the
        \emph{simplified} numerator, the $y$-intercept from $f(0)$ --- rather than reading them off a
        picture.
  \item \ldots{} build a \textbf{sign chart} on the $x$-intercepts and vertical asymptotes, and use it
        to assemble and describe the whole graph, including its domain written as a union of
        intervals.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small In Lesson 5.4 the asymptotes were printed on the face of the equation. Not anymore. From here on,
\textbf{factor it, and every feature is already in there} --- the sign chart is what turns the list of
features into a picture. Run the same five steps, always in this order. \textbf{(1) Factor} the
numerator and the denominator completely. \textbf{(2) Restrict, then cancel:} the restrictions come off
the \emph{original} denominator, and the \textbf{cancel test} sorts them --- a factor that cancels
leaves a \textbf{hole} (find its height by putting the excluded value into the \emph{simplified}
expression), and a factor that survives leaves a \textbf{vertical asymptote} $x=$ that value.
\textbf{(3) Compare degrees} for the horizontal asymptote: top lower gives $y=0$, degrees equal gives
the ratio of the leading coefficients, and top higher gives \emph{no} horizontal asymptote --- which is
a complete answer. \textbf{(4) Compute the intercepts:} the $x$-intercepts are the zeros of the
\emph{simplified} numerator (a zero that cancelled is a hole, not an intercept), and the $y$-intercept
is $f(0)$. \textbf{(5) Build a sign chart} whose boundary points are the $x$-intercepts and the vertical
asymptotes --- not the holes --- and test one value in each interval to learn whether the graph runs
above or below the $x$-axis there. Then assemble. The finished graph never touches a vertical
asymptote, so the domain is written as a \textbf{union} of intervals and increasing/decreasing is
reported branch by branch. It \emph{may} cross its horizontal asymptote, and when it does, the crossing
is at an $x$-intercept --- which is exactly why the transformed parents of Lesson 5.4, whose numerators
were nonzero constants, never could.
\end{remindbox}

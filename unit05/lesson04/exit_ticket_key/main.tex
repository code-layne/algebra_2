\vspace{0.06in}

No notes. Find the asymptotes before you do anything else.

\vspace{0.04in}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=7pt]
  \item \textbf{Read the equation.} \; $g(x)=\dfrac{-3}{x+2}-1$

        \vspace{3pt}
        $a=$ \ans{$-3$} \quad $h=$ \ans{$-2$} \quad $k=$ \ans{$-1$} \quad
        Center: $($\ans{$-2$}$,\,$\ans{$-1$}$)$

        \vspace{3pt}
        Vertical asymptote: \ans{$x=-2$} \quad Horizontal asymptote: \ans{$y=-1$} \quad
        $y$-intercept: \ans{$(0,-\frac52)$}

        \vspace{3pt}
        Domain: \ans{all reals, $x\neq-2$} \quad Range: \ans{all reals, $y\neq-1$}

        \vspace{3pt}
        Does this graph ever \emph{cross} its horizontal asymptote? Answer and explain in one
        sentence. \ansline{No --- that would need $\frac{-3}{x+2}=0$, and a fraction is zero only when its numerator is.}

  \item \textbf{Write the equation.} The asymptotes are dashed and two points are marked.
        \begin{center}
        \begin{minipage}{0.42\linewidth}\centering
        \begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.30]
          \lgrid{-4}{8}{-5}{5}
          \draw[navy, dashed, semithick] (2,-5)--(2,5);
          \draw[navy, dashed, semithick] (-4,-1)--(8,-1);
          \node[navy, font=\scriptsize, above right] at (2,4.2) {$x=2$};
          \node[navy, font=\scriptsize, below left] at (-0.6,-1) {$y=-1$};
          \begin{scope}
            \clip (-4,-5) rectangle (8,5);
            \draw[very thick, forest, domain=-4:1.6, samples=110, smooth] plot (\x,{3/((\x)-2)-1});
            \draw[very thick, forest, domain=2.4:8, samples=110, smooth] plot (\x,{3/((\x)-2)-1});
          \end{scope}
          \fill[charcoal] (3,2) circle (3.4pt) node[above right, font=\scriptsize]{$(3,2)$};
          \fill[charcoal] (5,0) circle (3.4pt) node[above right, font=\scriptsize]{$(5,0)$};
        \end{tikzpicture}
        \end{minipage}%
        \begin{minipage}{0.55\linewidth}
        \small
        $h=$ \ans{$2$} \quad $k=$ \ans{$-1$}

        \vspace{3pt}
        Substitute $(3,2)$ and solve for $a$:

        \ans{$2=\dfrac{a}{3-2}-1 \;\Rightarrow\; a-1=2 \;\Rightarrow\; a=3$}

        $a=$ \ans{$3$} \quad Equation: \ans{$y=\frac{3}{x-2}-1$}

        \vspace{3pt}
        Check on $(5,0)$: \ans{$\frac33-1=0$, matches}
        \end{minipage}
        \end{center}

  \item \textbf{SOL-style multiple choice.} Which function has a vertical asymptote at $x=-4$ and a
        horizontal asymptote at $y=3$?
        \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*., leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=1pt]
          \item $y=\dfrac{2}{x-4}+3$
          \item $y=\dfrac{2}{x+4}+3$
                \quad \textcolor{keyred}{\textbf{$\leftarrow$ correct}}
          \item $y=\dfrac{2}{x+3}+4$
          \item $y=\dfrac{3}{x+4}+2$
        \end{enumerate}
        \vspace{2pt}
        {\small $x+4=0$ gives the wall at $x=-4$, and the $+3$ outside is the horizontal asymptote
        $y=3$. \textbf{A} shifts the wall the wrong way ($x-4$ puts it at $+4$). \textbf{C} swaps the
        two jobs, putting the wall at $-3$ and the floor at $4$. \textbf{D} reads the horizontal
        asymptote off the \emph{numerator} --- but the $3$ on top is $a$, the stretch, and it moves no
        asymptote at all.}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teachernote}
Graded for completion --- ``mistakes happen, blanks don't.'' Sort into four piles: \textbf{(1) got it};
\textbf{(2) sign of $h$} --- wrote $x=2$ in item 1, or chose \textbf{A}; \textbf{(3) the two jobs
crossed} --- range given as ``all reals,'' option \textbf{C}, or $a$ and $k$ swapped; \textbf{(4)
equation-from-a-graph} --- item 2 blank or $a$ wrong.

\vspace{3pt}
Pile 2 is the fastest fix. Pile 3 is the one to re-teach before tomorrow: 5.5 takes the asymptotes off
the face of the equation entirely, and a student who cannot say which letter moves which asymptote has
nothing to hold onto there.

\vspace{3pt}
Item 1's last question is the interpretation half, not a bonus --- and its answer (\emph{never}) is
stronger than 5.0's (\emph{may}), because a transformed parent has a constant numerator. Anyone who
wrote ``asymptotes can never be crossed'' is right for the wrong reason: correct it out loud, since
5.0's $\frac{2x}{x^2+1}$ crosses $y=0$ at the origin. Item 2 is the A2.F.1b item and the likeliest SOL
question here; a student who got $h$ and $k$ but stalled on $a$ needs a minute, not a reteach.
\end{teachernote}

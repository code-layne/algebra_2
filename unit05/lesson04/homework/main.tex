\begin{extensionbox}
\textbf{How far can you see?} On a clear day, from an eye height of $h$ feet above the water, the
distance to the horizon is about
\[
  d(h) = \sqrt{1.5h} \ \text{ miles.}
\]
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$h$ (feet of eye height) & $6$ & $24$ & $54$ & $96$ & $150$ \\ \hline
$d(h)$ (miles) & $3$ & $6$ & $9$ & $12$ & $15$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item Give the domain of $d$ from the radicand. Does the context restrict it any further?
  \item The graph of $d$ has its anchor point at $(0,0)$. What does that point mean for a swimmer
        with their eyes right at water level?
  \item From the table: going from $h=6$ to $h=24$ multiplies the eye height by $4$ but multiplies
        the viewing distance by only $2$. Use the shape of a square root graph to explain why.
  \item Use the product property to rewrite $d(h)=\sqrt{1.5h}$ in the form $a\sqrt{h}$, giving $a$ to
        two decimal places. Then finish this sentence: \emph{the horizon formula is just the parent
        $y=\sqrt{h}$ with a single \underline{\hspace{2.4cm}} applied to it.}
  \item A sailor climbs $h$ feet up the mast of a ship whose deck already sits $20$ feet above the
        water, so their eye height above the water is $h+20$ and the formula becomes
        $D(h)=\sqrt{1.5(h+20)}$. Describe how the graph of $D$ differs from the graph of $d$, and
        give the anchor point of $D$.
  \item Part (e)'s anchor point is not a place any sailor can stand. Explain why, and state the
        domain that the \emph{context} actually allows --- then give $D(0)$ and say what it means.
\end{enumerate}
\writelines{5}
\end{extensionbox}

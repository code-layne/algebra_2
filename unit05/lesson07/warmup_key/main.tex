\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Skills We'll Use Today}
\small Three skills, all of them yours already. Today is the last lesson of the unit, and it is where
$y=\dfrac{k}{x}$ takes off the lab coat and goes to work.

\emph{product} row. Decimals are fine.

In Table P the \ans{quotient} row came out constant. In Table Q the \ans{product} row did.
Hold on to that --- it is the entire test we build today.

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{2. The rational parent} (Lesson 5.4). Let $y=\dfrac{6}{x}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2.1em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item $x=2 \Rightarrow y=$ \ans{$3$}; \quad $x=3 \Rightarrow y=$ \ans{$2$}; \quad
        $x=12 \Rightarrow y=$ \ans{$0.5$}
  \item Vertical asymptote: \ans{$x=0$} \qquad Horizontal asymptote: \ans{$y=0$}
  \item As $x$ grows without bound, $y$ gets closer and closer to \ans{$0$} but never
        \ans{reaches} it.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{3. Solve the proportion} (Lesson 5.6 --- one fraction $=$ one fraction, so cross-multiply).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2.1em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item $\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{x}{20}$: \quad $x=$ \ans{$7.5$}
  \item $\dfrac{5}{x}=\dfrac{2}{9}$: \quad $x=$ \ans{$22.5$}
\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
\textbf{Item 1 is the whole lesson in disguise}, so debrief it before anything else. Both tables look
alike at a glance --- four inputs, four outputs, a clear pattern --- and the \emph{only} thing that
separates them is which row of arithmetic settles down to a single number. Do not name ``direct'' or
``inverse'' yet. Just get the two words \emph{quotient} and \emph{product} on the board and leave them
there; everything in the notes hangs off them. Table Q's quotient row is deliberately ugly
($15$, $3.75$, $1.\overline{6}$, $0.6$) so that ``not constant'' is unmistakable.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Item 2} is 5.4, and it is here because \emph{inverse variation is the rational parent}. When
students meet $y=\frac{240}{r}$ in the notes, the asymptotes are not new machinery --- they are the two
sentences they already know, now saying something about a car trip.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Item 3} is yesterday's cross-multiplying, and it returns today as the fast route through every
``find the missing value'' problem: a direct variation is exactly the statement that two quotients are
equal, which is a proportion. Watch 3(b) --- with the variable in the denominator, students reach for
the wrong pairing.
\end{teachernote}

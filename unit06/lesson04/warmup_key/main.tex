\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Skills We'll Use Today}
\small Three skills you already have. Today they become one method. Work quickly.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{1. Shifting a graph (Units 2--4).} Describe how each graph moves away from its parent, and
give the point the graph is built around.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X X@{}}
 & \textbf{How it moves from the parent} & \textbf{Vertex} \\
\hline
(a) \; $y=(x-4)^{2}+1$ & \ans{right $4$, up $1$} & \ans{$(4,1)$} \\
(b) \; $y=|x+2|-3$ & \ans{left $2$, down $3$} & \ans{$(-2,-3)$} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
One of the two numbers in each equation moves the graph in the \emph{same} direction as its sign,
and the other moves it in the \emph{opposite} direction. Which is which?
\ansline{The number \emph{outside} ($+1$, $-3$) moves the graph \emph{vertically} and in the same
direction as its sign. The number \emph{inside}, with the $x$, moves the graph \emph{horizontally}
and in the \emph{opposite} direction: $x-4$ moves right, $x+2$ moves left.}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{2. Yesterday's domains, one step further (Lesson 6.0).} Fill in the row.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X X X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Function} & \textbf{Solve radicand} $\ge 0$ & \textbf{Domain} & \textbf{Endpoint} \\
\hline
$g(x)=\sqrt{x-3}$ & \ans{$x\ge 3$} & \ans{$[3,\infty)$} & \ans{$(3,0)$} \\
$h(x)=\sqrt{x+5}$ & \ans{$x\ge -5$} & \ans{$[-5,\infty)$} & \ans{$(-5,0)$} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
Compare the number you see \emph{inside} each radical with the first coordinate of that function's
endpoint. What do you notice?
\ansline{They are the same number with the sign reversed. The $-3$ inside $\sqrt{x-3}$ puts the
endpoint at $x=+3$; the $+5$ inside $\sqrt{x+5}$ puts it at $x=-5$ --- the same
opposite-direction rule as item 1.}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{3. Pulling a number out (Lesson 6.2).} Simplify. Assume $x\ge 0$.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l l@{}}
(a) \; $\sqrt{9x}=$ \ans{$3\sqrt{x}$} & (b) \; $\sqrt{25x}=$ \ans{$5\sqrt{x}$} &
(c) \; $\sqrt[3]{8x}=$ \ans{$2\sqrt[3]{x}$} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Look hard at (a). You just claimed that $\sqrt{9x}$ and $3\sqrt{x}$ are the same expression. But one
of them is the parent $\sqrt{x}$ with its \emph{input} multiplied by $9$, and the other is the parent
with its \emph{output} multiplied by $3$. Can both of those really be the same graph?
\ansline{Yes --- they are equal for every $x\ge 0$, so they \emph{must} be the same graph. (Do not
resolve this now; it is Section 5.)}

\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
Four minutes. Each item is one third of today's lesson, disassembled.

\textbf{Item 1} is the whole $h$-and-$k$ half, and students have owned it since Unit~2. Today's only
new thing is \emph{which point} you track: a parabola had a vertex, an absolute value had a vertex,
and a square root has an \textbf{endpoint}. Get the phrase \emph{``inside moves it the opposite way''}
said aloud now, because the number under the radical is the one students will get backwards all
period.

\textbf{Item 2 is the hinge of the lesson.} In 6.0, finding the domain of $\sqrt{x-3}$ meant solving
an inequality. This item makes students solve it \emph{and} write the endpoint, side by side, so the
follow-up lands: the answer to the inequality was sitting in the equation the whole time. That is not
a coincidence, and it is the sentence the Hook is built to produce --- \emph{shifting the graph right
$3$ and losing every input below $3$ are the same event.} Do not say it for them.

\textbf{Item 3} looks like a leftover from 6.2. It is the setup for the day's genuine surprise: for a
radical, stretching the graph sideways and stretching it up and down are the \emph{same}
transformation, because the product property lets the number walk out from under the radical. Take
the last blank as a vote --- ``same graph or not?'' --- record the split on the board and leave it
unresolved. Section~5 collects on it.
\end{teachernote}

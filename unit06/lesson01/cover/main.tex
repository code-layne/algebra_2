\begin{remindbox}
\small Building an exponential model means finding exactly \textbf{two numbers}. The \textbf{initial
value} $a$ is what you start with --- the output at $x=0$, and the $y$-intercept of the graph. The
\textbf{factor} $b$ is what you \textbf{multiply} by for each one-unit step. A \emph{table} hands you
both: $a$ sits in the $x=0$ column, and $b$ is the constant ratio you get by dividing each output by
the one before it. \emph{Two points} give you $b$ by division --- writing both points into $y=ab^{x}$
and dividing cancels $a$, leaving $b$ raised to the gap between the $x$-values. A \emph{sentence},
though, almost never hands you $b$. It gives you a percent \textbf{rate} $r$, and you must translate:
$b=1+r$ when the quantity \textbf{grows}, and $b=1-r$ when it \textbf{decays}. Up $8\%$ leaves you
with $108\%$ of what you had, so $b=1.08$ --- \emph{not} $0.08$. Down $15\%$ leaves you with $85\%$,
so $b=0.85$ --- \emph{not} $1.15$. The base is always the fraction you \textbf{end up with}, never
the percent that changed. When you finish writing a model, run the two-second check: does $x=0$ give
back the starting amount, and is $b$ on the correct side of $1$?
\end{remindbox}

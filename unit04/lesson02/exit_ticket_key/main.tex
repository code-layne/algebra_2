\vspace{0.10in}

No notes. Factor \textbf{completely} --- check every factor before you move on.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=12pt]
  \item \textbf{Start with the GCF.} $3x^3 - 27x$

    \vspace{3pt}
    \emph{Step 1 --- pull out the GCF:} \ans{$3x(x^2 - 9)$}

    \vspace{5pt}
    \emph{Step 2 --- is anything left factorable? Finish:} \ans{$3x(x-3)(x+3)$}

  \item \textbf{Four terms.} $x^3 - 2x^2 - 25x + 50$

    \vspace{3pt}
    \emph{Group and take the GCF out of each pair:} \; \ans{$x^2$}$(x-2)$ \; \ans{$-25$}$(x-2)$

    \vspace{4pt}
    \emph{Factor out the shared binomial:} \ans{$(x-2)(x^2-25)$}

    \vspace{4pt}
    \emph{Now finish completely:} \ans{$(x-2)(x-5)(x+5)$}

    \vspace{4pt}
    If $f(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 - 25x + 50$, its zeros are \ans{$2,\ 5,\ -5$}, because a product equals zero
    exactly when \ans{one of its factors is zero}.

  \item \textbf{SOL-style multiple choice.} Which expression is the complete factorization of
    $8x^3 - 27$?
    \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*., leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=1pt]
      \item $(2x - 3)(4x^2 + 12x + 9)$
      \item $(2x - 3)(4x^2 - 6x + 9)$
      \item $(2x + 3)(4x^2 - 6x + 9)$
      \item $(2x - 3)(4x^2 + 6x + 9)$ \quad \textcolor{keyred}{\textbf{$\leftarrow$ correct}}
    \end{enumerate}
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, nosep]
      \item $8x^3-27$ is a difference of cubes with $a=2x$, $b=3$: $(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2) = (2x-3)(4x^2+6x+9)$.
      \item \textbf{A} uses $2ab = 12x$ --- that is the \emph{perfect-square} pattern, not the cube pattern; the cube identity's middle term is $ab$.
      \item \textbf{B} has the middle sign backwards. SOAP: the binomial's sign is \emph{Same} ($-$), so the trinomial's middle sign is \emph{Opposite} ($+$).
      \item \textbf{C} has the binomial sign backwards --- $(2x+3)$ would factor $8x^3 + 27$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teachernote}
Item~1 catches two different failures at once: an answer of $3x(x^2-9)$ means the GCF habit is there
but ``completely'' is not, while an answer of $(x-3)(x+3)$ with no $3x$ means the GCF was dropped
after being used. Item~2 is the same test one level up --- students who stop at $(x-2)(x^2-25)$ have
grouping but not the finish, and the zeros line will then be short by two. Item~3's distractors are
the three cube-identity errors in order: a $2$ in the middle term (\textbf{A}), a flipped SOAP middle
sign (\textbf{B}), and a flipped binomial sign (\textbf{C}).

\vspace{3pt}
Sort into four piles --- \emph{got it} / \emph{stopped early} / \emph{GCF skipped or dropped} /
\emph{SOAP sign}. A large ``stopped early'' pile is not a re-teach of methods; it is a five-minute
routine at the top of Lesson~4.3 (``read your answer back and name each factor prime''). A large
``SOAP'' pile does warrant re-deriving the identity by multiplication before division starts.
\end{teachernote}

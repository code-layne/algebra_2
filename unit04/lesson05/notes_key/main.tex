\begin{objectivebox}
\textbf{By the end of this lesson, I will be able to\dots}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item \textbf{factor} a rational function and sort every restriction into a \emph{hole} or a \emph{vertical asymptote} (A2.F.2h);
  \item find the \textbf{horizontal asymptote} by comparing degrees, and the \textbf{intercepts} by computing --- not by reading a picture (A2.F.2a/g);
  \item build a \textbf{sign chart} whose boundary points are the $x$-intercepts and the vertical asymptotes, and use it to say where the graph runs \emph{above} and \emph{below} the $x$-axis;
  \item \textbf{assemble and describe the whole graph} from its equation, and recognize when a function is really a transformed $\frac1x$ from Lesson 4.4 (A2.F.1c).
\end{itemize}
\end{objectivebox}

\begin{vocabbox}
\small
Fill in each term as we build it together.
\par\vspace{2pt}   % \par is required: \vocabans's \noindent is a no-op mid-paragraph
\vocabans{Factored form (and why it comes first)}{numerator and denominator each written as a product of factors. Every feature of the graph is visible in this form and in no other: the denominator's factors give the restrictions, a shared factor gives a hole, and the numerator's factors give the $x$-intercepts}
\vocabans{The cancel test: hole or wall}{take each restriction from the \emph{original} denominator; if its factor cancels, the graph has a \emph{hole} there --- if the factor survives, the graph has a \emph{vertical asymptote} there}
\vocabans{Degree comparison}{compare the degree of the whole top with the degree of the whole bottom: top lower $\Rightarrow$ HA $y=0$; degrees equal $\Rightarrow$ HA is the ratio of the leading coefficients; top higher $\Rightarrow$ no horizontal asymptote}
\vocabans{Sign chart \& boundary points}{a table that splits the $x$-axis at the $x$-intercepts and the vertical asymptotes (the \emph{boundary points}) and records, from one test value per interval, whether the graph is above or below the $x$-axis there}
\end{vocabbox}

\begin{notesbox}{1. The Build --- Five Steps, Always in This Order}
In Lesson 4.4 the asymptotes were printed on the face of the equation. Starting today they are not. The
denominator factors into \emph{two} pieces, the numerator may cancel one of them, and the graph has to
be \textbf{assembled} rather than read.

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}c l X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{\#} & \textbf{Step} & \textbf{What you write down} \\
\hline
1 & \textbf{Factor} everything & numerator and denominator, completely \\
2 & \textbf{Restrict, then cancel} & restrictions from the \emph{original} denominator; each one becomes a \ans{hole} or a \ans{vertical asymptote} \\
3 & \textbf{Compare degrees} & the horizontal asymptote: $y=$ \ans{$0$}, $y=$ ratio, or \ans{none} \\
4 & \textbf{Compute intercepts} & $x$-int from the simplified \ans{numerator}; $y$-int is $f($\ans{$0$}$)$ \\
5 & \textbf{Sign chart} & boundary points, one test value each, above or below \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{3pt}
\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\centering\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Factor it, and every feature is already in there.} The sign chart is what turns the list into a
picture.\vspace{2pt}}}
\end{center}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{2. Step 2 Up Close --- Hole or Wall?}
This is Lesson 4.0's \emph{cancel test}, and Lesson 4.1's algebra is what runs it.

\vspace{3pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item Restrictions always come off the \textbf{original} denominator, \emph{before} anything is
        cancelled. Simplify first and a restriction disappears --- along with the feature it names.
  \item A factor that \textbf{cancels} leaves a \ans{hole}: a single missing point. Find its
        height by substituting the excluded value into the \ans{simplified} expression.
  \item A factor that \textbf{survives} in the denominator leaves a \ans{vertical asymptote}, with equation
        $x=$ \ans{that excluded value}.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Quick sort.} Factor, then label each restriction.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X X X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Function} & \textbf{Restrictions} & \textbf{Hole at\dots} & \textbf{Vertical asymptote(s)} \\
\hline
\TallMath{\frac{x-4}{(x-4)(x+1)}} & \ans{$x\neq4,-1$} & \ans{$x=4$} & \ans{$x=-1$} \\
\TallMath{\frac{x+5}{(x-2)(x+3)}} & \ans{$x\neq2,-3$} & \ans{none} & \ans{$x=2$, $x=-3$} \\
\TallMath{\frac{x^2-1}{x^2-x-2}}  & \ans{$x\neq2,-1$} & \ans{$x=-1$} & \ans{$x=2$} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
\vspace{-2pt}
{\small (The third one: $\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x-2)(x+1)}$.)}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{3. Step 3 Up Close --- The Degree Comparison}
Nothing new here either; this is 4.0's rule, and it is the \emph{end behavior} statement (A2.F.2g)
written as a line.

\vspace{3pt}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l l X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Case} & \textbf{Horizontal asymptote} & \textbf{Example} \\
\hline
top degree $<$ bottom degree & $y=$ \ans{$0$} & $\dfrac{x-1}{x^2-x-6}$ \\
top degree $=$ bottom degree & $y=$ \ans{ratio of leading coeffs.} & $\dfrac{2x^2-8}{x^2-x-6}$, so $y=$ \ans{$2$} \\
top degree $>$ bottom degree & \ans{no HA at all} & $\dfrac{x^3}{x^2-1}$ \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{3pt}
Two cautions worth writing down.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item Use the degrees of the \emph{whole} top and the \emph{whole} bottom --- not of one factor. It
        does not matter whether you compare before or after cancelling, because cancelling a common
        factor lowers \ans{the top and the bottom} degree by the same amount.
  \item ``No horizontal asymptote'' is a complete answer. The graph still has end behavior --- the arms
        just run off to $\pm\infty$ instead of flattening.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{4. Worked Example A --- Two Walls, and a Sign Chart}
\[ f(x)=\frac{x-1}{x^2-x-6} \]

\textbf{Step 1 --- factor.} \quad $f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{(\text{\ans{$x+2$}})(\text{\ans{$x-3$}})}$

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Step 2 --- restrict and cancel.} Restrictions: $x\neq$ \ans{$-2$} and $x\neq$ \ans{$3$}.
Does anything cancel? \ans{no} \; So there are \ans{no} holes, and the vertical asymptotes
are \ans{$x=-2$} and \ans{$x=3$}.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Step 3 --- degrees.} Top degree \ans{$1$}, bottom degree \ans{$2$}, so the horizontal
asymptote is \ans{$y=0$}.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Step 4 --- intercepts.} $x$-intercept: the numerator is zero at $x=$ \ans{$1$}, so the point
is \ans{$(1,0)$}. \; $y$-intercept: $f(0)=\dfrac{-1}{-6}=$ \ans{$\frac16$}, so the point is
\ans{$(0,\frac16)$}.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Step 5 --- sign chart.} The boundary points are the $x$-intercept and the two vertical
asymptotes: $-2$, $1$, and $3$. They cut the axis into \ans{$4$} intervals. Test one value in each.

\vspace{2pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{|C{2.6cm}|C{1.6cm}|C{4.0cm}|C{1.6cm}|X|}
\hline
\rowcolor{forestbg}
\textbf{Interval} & \textbf{Test $x$} & \textbf{$\dfrac{x-1}{(x+2)(x-3)}$} & \textbf{Sign} &
\textbf{Above / below} \\ \hline
$x<-2$    & $-3$ & \ans{$\frac{-4}{(-1)(-6)}=-\frac23$} & \ans{$-$} & \ans{below} \\ \hline
$-2<x<1$  & $0$  & \ans{$\frac{-1}{(2)(-3)}=\frac16$}   & \ans{$+$} & \ans{above} \\ \hline
$1<x<3$   & $2$  & \ans{$\frac{1}{(4)(-1)}=-\frac14$}   & \ans{$-$} & \ans{below} \\ \hline
$x>3$     & $4$  & \ans{$\frac{3}{(6)(1)}=\frac12$}     & \ans{$+$} & \ans{above} \\ \hline
\end{tabularx}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Assemble it.} Far to the left the graph is just \ans{below} the $x$-axis, hugging $y=0$;
it dives to $-\infty$ at the wall $x=-2$, comes back from $+\infty$ on the other side, falls through
$(1,0)$, drops to $-\infty$ at $x=3$, and returns from $+\infty$ to flatten onto $y=0$ on the right.

\vspace{4pt}

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Now the question 4.4 could not ask.} Does this graph ever \emph{cross} its horizontal asymptote
$y=0$? Set $f(x)=0$: that happens exactly where the \ans{numerator} is zero --- at $x=$ \ans{$1$}.
So yes, and the crossing point is the \ans{$x$-intercept}. In 4.4 the numerator was a nonzero constant and a
crossing was impossible; here it is not.

\vspace{3pt}
Domain: \ans{$(-\infty,-2)\cup(-2,3)\cup(3,\infty)$} \quad {\small(three intervals, joined with $\cup$ --- an interval may never span
a wall)}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{5. Worked Example B --- A Hole, and an Old Friend}
\[ g(x)=\frac{x^2-4}{x^2-x-6} \]

\textbf{Steps 1--2.} \quad $g(x)=\dfrac{(\text{\ans{$x-2$}})(\text{\ans{$x+2$}})}%
{(\text{\ans{$x-3$}})(\text{\ans{$x+2$}})}$ \quad Restrictions: $x\neq$ \ans{$3$},
$x\neq$ \ans{$-2$}.

\vspace{3pt}
The factor \ans{$x+2$} cancels, so $x=$ \ans{$-2$} is a \ans{hole}; the factor
\ans{$x-3$} survives, so $x=$ \ans{$3$} is a \ans{vertical asymptote}. Simplified:
$g(x)=$ \ans{$\frac{x-2}{x-3}$}.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{The hole's coordinates.} Substitute the cancelled value into the \emph{simplified} expression:
$\dfrac{-2-2}{-2-3}=$ \ans{$\frac45$}, so the hole is at \ans{$(-2,\frac45)$}.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Step 3.} Degrees \ans{$2$} and \ans{$2$} --- equal --- so the HA is the ratio of the
leading coefficients, $y=$ \ans{$1$}.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Step 4.} $x$-intercept from the \emph{simplified} numerator: \ans{$(2,0)$}. \;
$y$-intercept: $g(0)=\dfrac{-4}{-6}=$ \ans{$\frac23$}.

\vspace{4pt}
\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\centering\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{A trap worth naming.} The original numerator is zero at $x=2$ \emph{and} at $x=-2$. But $-2$ is
not an $x$-intercept --- it is the hole. Only the zeros of the \emph{simplified} numerator are
intercepts.\vspace{2pt}}}
\end{center}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Step 5 --- sign chart.} Boundary points: the $x$-intercept $2$ and the wall $3$. \emph{The hole
is not a boundary point} --- the sign does not change there, because the graph on both sides of it is
the same simplified function.
\begin{center}

\hline
\rowcolor{forestbg}
\textbf{Interval} & \textbf{Test $x$} & \textbf{$\dfrac{x-2}{x-3}$} & \textbf{Above / below} \\ \hline
$x<2$     & $0$   & \ans{$\frac{-2}{-3}=\frac23$}  & \ans{above} \\ \hline
$2<x<3$   & $2.5$ & \ans{$\frac{0.5}{-0.5}=-1$}    & \ans{below} \\ \hline
$x>3$     & $4$   & \ans{$\frac{2}{1}=2$}          & \ans{above} \\ \hline

\textbf{And now look again.} Simplify all the way and rewrite the numerator using the denominator
(Lesson 4.4's disguise):
\[ \frac{x-2}{x-3}=\frac{(x-3)+{\color{keyred}\mathbf{1}}}{x-3}={\color{keyred}1+\frac{1}{x-3}} \]
So the whole graph is the rational \emph{parent}, shifted \ans{right} $3$ and \ans{up}

\begin{notesbox}{6. Reading the Finished Picture --- and Settling the Hook}
Whatever the equation, the assembled graph has to obey all of this:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item It \textbf{never} touches a vertical asymptote --- that input is not in the \ans{domain}.
  \item It \textbf{may} cross a horizontal asymptote, and if it does, the crossing is at an
        \ans{$x$-intercept}. (A transformed parent from 4.4 never can; its numerator is a nonzero
        constant.)
  \item Domain and range are written as \ans{unions} of intervals, because an interval may never
        span a wall.
  \item Increasing/decreasing intervals are reported \emph{branch by branch}, for the same reason.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Back to the Hook.} Your sign chart for $f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{(x+2)(x-3)}$ said: below, above,
below, above.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item Graph \textbf{B} is wrong because \ansline{every sign is reversed --- it is above the axis on the far left, but the test value $x=-3$ gives a negative output}
  \item Graph \textbf{C} is wrong because \ansline{it does not change sign at $x=-2$ --- both of its branches there go up, but the chart says below on the left of $-2$ and above on the right}
  \item The correct graph is \ans{A}.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em, itemsep=5pt]

        {(\text{\ans{$x-2$}})(\text{\ans{$x+2$}})}$ \quad Restrictions: $x\neq$ \ans{$2$},
        $x\neq$ \ans{$-2$}
  \item Hole at \ans{$(-2,\frac54)$}; \quad vertical asymptote \ans{$x=2$}.
  \item Horizontal asymptote \ans{$y=1$}. \quad $x$-intercept \ans{$(3,0)$}. \quad
        $y$-intercept \ans{$(0,\frac32)$}.
  \item Sign chart --- boundary points \ans{$2$ and $3$}. Test $x=0$: sign \ans{$+$}; \;
        test $x=2.5$: sign \ans{$-$}; \; test $x=4$: sign \ans{$+$}.
  \item Domain: \ans{$(-\infty,-2)\cup(-2,2)\cup(2,\infty)$}
\end{enumerate}
\end{practicebox}

\begin{teachernote}
\textbf{Pacing.} Sections 1--3 are fast; they are 4.0 and 4.1 restated as a procedure, and the class
should reach Worked Example A with most of the period left. Sections 4 and 5 are the lesson. Section 6
takes three minutes and pays for the Hook.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{The Hook is the argument for the sign chart.} Do not resolve it early. All three graphs really
do have the stated asymptotes, the stated intercept, and the stated end behavior --- so a student who
believes ``find the features and you have the graph'' has no way to choose. Let them guess, write the
guesses on the board, and leave them there. When the Section 4 sign chart reads \emph{below, above,
below, above}, graphs B and C are eliminated by arithmetic rather than by authority.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Worked Example A's payoff} is that it \emph{crosses} its horizontal asymptote --- at $(1,0)$,
its own $x$-intercept. That sharpens the two claims students now hold: 4.0 said a graph \emph{may}
cross its HA, 4.4 said a transformed parent \emph{never} does, and today explains the difference in one
sentence --- a fraction is zero only when its numerator is, so a nonzero constant numerator makes
crossing impossible and a numerator with a zero makes it certain. Ask where the crossing has to be
\emph{before} anyone graphs.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Worked Example B is the continuity argument for the unit.} After the hole and the asymptote are
sorted, the leftover is $\frac{x-2}{x-3}=1+\frac{1}{x-3}$ --- yesterday's anchor family, with one point
removed. Students who did 4.4's disguise section will recognize it; say out loud that 4.5 has not
replaced 4.4, it has surrounded it.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Errors to expect, in order of cost.} (1) Restrictions read off the \emph{simplified} form, which
loses the hole --- and which becomes an extraneous solution in 4.6, so correct it by name today.
(2) Calling $x=-2$ an $x$-intercept in Example B because the original numerator vanishes there.
(3) Announcing a vertical asymptote at every excluded value without running the cancel test.
(4) Making the hole a boundary point on the sign chart. (5) Comparing the degree of one \emph{factor}
instead of the whole top and bottom. (6) A single interval spanning a wall.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=forestbg, colframe=forest, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students can take a rational function whose asymptotes are \emph{not}
    visible in the equation and \textbf{assemble} its graph with the same five steps every time:
    \textbf{factor} completely; take the restrictions off the \emph{original} denominator and run the
    \textbf{cancel test} to sort each one into a \textbf{hole} (with coordinates, from the simplified
    expression) or a \textbf{vertical asymptote}; get the \textbf{horizontal asymptote} from the
    \textbf{degree comparison}, including answering ``none''; \textbf{compute} both intercepts, taking
    $x$-intercepts from the \emph{simplified} numerator; and build a \textbf{sign chart} on the
    $x$-intercepts and the vertical asymptotes to decide, interval by interval, whether the graph runs
    above or below the $x$-axis. They then describe the finished graph --- domain as a union of
    intervals, end behavior, branch-by-branch behavior --- and interpret it in context.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} \textbf{A2.F.2h} --- determine the equations of vertical and
    horizontal asymptotes. \textbf{A2.F.2a} --- domain, range, zeros, and intercepts, including graphs
    with discontinuities. \textbf{A2.F.2g} --- describe end behavior (the horizontal asymptote
    \emph{is} the end-behavior statement). Applied: \textbf{A2.F.1c} (graphing from the equation ---
    Worked Example B turns out to be a transformed parent), \textbf{A2.F.2c}
    (increasing/decreasing, reported branch by branch), and \textbf{A2.F.2f} (interpreting $f(x)$ in
    context --- the round-trip and pollution-cost models). Builds on \textbf{A2.EO.3b} (3.2
    factoring), \textbf{A2.EO.1b/d} (4.1 simplifying and restrictions), and the 3.6 sign chart.
    \textbf{Slant asymptotes are not in the standard} --- A2.F.2h covers vertical and horizontal only.
    They appear once, as Tier E enrichment, and are \emph{never} assessed. Prerequisite for
    \textbf{4.6}, where today's excluded values return as extraneous solutions.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Ideas \& Skills} & \textbf{Key Understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Factor a rational function completely, before anything else is attempted.
  \item List restrictions from the \emph{original} denominator and sort each with the cancel test: hole or vertical asymptote.
  \item Give a hole's \emph{coordinates} by substituting into the simplified expression.
  \item Get the horizontal asymptote from the degree comparison, and answer ``none'' when the top outranks the bottom.
  \item Compute both intercepts --- $x$-intercepts from the \emph{simplified} numerator only.
  \item Build a sign chart whose boundary points are the $x$-intercepts and the vertical asymptotes (never the holes).
  \item Assemble and describe: domain as a union, end behavior, branch-by-branch behavior, and where the graph crosses $y=$ HA.
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item \textbf{Factor it, and every feature is already in there.} The sign chart is what turns the list into a picture.
  \item A feature list does \emph{not} determine a graph --- the Hook proves it with three graphs that share every listed feature.
  \item Restrictions are facts about the \emph{original} expression. Cancelling never restores an input (4.1), and forgetting that is tomorrow's extraneous solution.
  \item A fraction is zero only when its numerator is. That single fact gives the $x$-intercepts \emph{and} explains every crossing of a horizontal asymptote.
  \item A hole is not a boundary point: the sign cannot change at a point where the function is the same on both sides.
  \item 4.5 does not replace 4.4 --- it surrounds it. Worked Example B is yesterday's anchor family with one point punched out.
  \item ``No horizontal asymptote'' is a complete answer; the graph still has end behavior.
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Factored form} & Numerator and denominator each written as a product. Every feature of the graph lives here and nowhere else. \\
\textbf{The cancel test (4.0)} & A restriction whose factor cancels is a \emph{hole}; one whose factor survives in the denominator is a \emph{vertical asymptote}. \\
\textbf{Hole (removable discontinuity)} & A single missing point. Its height comes from substituting the excluded value into the \emph{simplified} expression. \\
\textbf{Degree comparison (4.0)} & \TallMath{n<m \Rightarrow y=0; \quad n=m \Rightarrow y=\tfrac{a_n}{b_m}; \quad n>m \Rightarrow \text{none}} \\
\textbf{Boundary point} & An $x$-intercept or a vertical asymptote --- the only places the sign of $f(x)$ can change. \emph{Not} a hole. \\
\textbf{Sign chart} & One test value per interval; records whether the graph is above or below the $x$-axis there. \\
\textbf{Slant asymptote} & \emph{Enrichment only, never assessed.} When the top outranks the bottom by one, division leaves a linear quotient the graph runs alongside. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{fixedskillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review]{forestbg}
    The Warm-Up rehearses the three steps that \emph{are} today's lesson. \textbf{(1)} \emph{Factor,
    restrict, cancel, sort} on $\frac{x^2-9}{x^2-2x-3}$ --- restrictions $x\neq3,-1$, the factor $x-3$
    cancels, so $3$ is a hole and $-1$ is a wall. Insist on the order: restrictions come off the
    \emph{original} denominator. \textbf{(2)} \emph{Degree comparison}, three items, with
    $\frac{x^3}{x^2-1}$ deliberately last --- ``none'' is a complete answer, and a student who asks what
    the graph does instead has asked the Tier E question. \textbf{(3)} \emph{Signs, not values}: fill a
    sign table for $\frac{x+1}{(x-2)(x+3)}$ at four inputs without computing a single output. Debrief
    the last line out loud --- a sign chart is cheaper than evaluating, and a quotient of signs behaves
    exactly like the products they have been doing since 3.6. Do \emph{not} promise the signs always
    alternate; item 1(f) of tonight's homework breaks that promise.
\end{fixedskillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{forestbg}
    Put three graphs on the board (they are in the notes) and tell the class the truth about all three:
    each has a vertical asymptote at $x=-2$, a vertical asymptote at $x=3$, an $x$-intercept at
    $(1,0)$, and branches that flatten toward $y=0$ at both ends. \textbf{``Exactly one of these is
    $f(x)=\frac{x-1}{(x+2)(x-3)}$. Which one, and how would you know?''} Take guesses, write them on
    the board, and \emph{leave them there}. The point is the thing students discover when they try to
    justify a choice: every feature on the checklist is true of all three, so the checklist cannot
    decide. What separates them is which \emph{side} of the $x$-axis the curve sits on in each region
    --- and that is what a sign chart, and only a sign chart, answers. This is the argument for the
    tool, made before the tool. Resolve it in Section 6, after the Worked Example A chart reads
    \emph{below, above, below, above}, so that B and C are eliminated by arithmetic rather than by the
    teacher.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson]{forestbg}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{1. Name the change.} Yesterday every function was $\frac{a}{x-h}+k$ and both asymptotes were
printed on the face of the equation. Today the denominator factors into two pieces, the numerator may
cancel one of them, and nothing can be read off directly. State the replacement sentence and post it:
\textbf{factor it, and every feature is already in there --- the sign chart is what turns the list into
a picture.} Then give the five steps as a fixed order and hold to that order all period.

\textbf{2. Steps 2 and 3, fast.} These are 4.0 recalled, so run them as a quick sort, not a lecture.
Step 2 is the cancel test: restrictions off the \emph{original} denominator, then a cancelled factor
gives a hole and a surviving factor gives a wall. The one addition today is that a hole gets
\emph{coordinates} --- substitute the excluded value into the simplified expression. Step 3 is the
degree comparison, with two cautions worth writing down: compare the \emph{whole} top with the
\emph{whole} bottom, not one factor with another, and ``none'' is a complete answer.

\textbf{3. Worked Example A --- $f(x)=\frac{x-1}{x^2-x-6}$.} The full build, out loud, in order.
Factors $(x+2)(x-3)$; nothing cancels, so two walls and no holes; degrees $1$ and $2$, so HA $y=0$;
$x$-intercept $(1,0)$; $y$-intercept $(0,\frac16)$. Then stop and make the sign chart the event of the
period: three boundary points, four intervals, one test value each, signs $-,+,-,+$. Assemble from the
chart and only then show the picture.

\columnbreak

\textbf{4. The question 4.4 could not ask.} \textbf{``Does this graph cross its horizontal asymptote?''}
Setting $f(x)=0$ needs the numerator to be zero, which happens at $x=1$ --- so yes, and the crossing is
at the $x$-intercept. Put the three claims side by side: 4.0 said a rational graph \emph{may} cross its
HA; 4.4 said a transformed parent \emph{never} does; today explains both in one sentence, because a
nonzero constant numerator makes a crossing impossible and a numerator with a zero makes it certain.
Ask where the crossing must be \emph{before} anyone looks at the graph.

\textbf{5. Worked Example B --- $g(x)=\frac{x^2-4}{x^2-x-6}$.} Now a hole. Restrictions $3$ and $-2$;
$(x+2)$ cancels, so $-2$ is a hole at $(-2,\frac45)$ and $3$ is a wall; equal degrees, so HA $y=1$;
$x$-intercept $(2,0)$ --- and here is the trap worth naming out loud: the \emph{original} numerator is
also zero at $-2$, and $(-2,0)$ is not an intercept, because $-2$ is not in the domain. The sign chart
has boundary points $2$ and $3$ only; a hole is not a boundary point.

\textbf{6. The payoff, and the Hook.} Simplify $\frac{x-2}{x-3}=1+\frac{1}{x-3}$: Worked Example B is
yesterday's anchor family with one point removed. Say plainly that 4.5 has not replaced 4.4, it has
surrounded it. Then return to the board, read the Section 4 sign chart back --- below, above, below,
above --- and let the class eliminate B (all signs reversed) and C (no sign change at $x=-2$).
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Explicit Instruction: The Five-Step Build]{forestbg}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.44\linewidth} X@{}}
\vspace{0pt}
\textbf{Post these. Always in this order.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item \textbf{Factor} the top and the bottom completely.
  \item \textbf{Restrictions} off the \emph{original} denominator; cancel test $\rightarrow$ hole (with coordinates) or vertical asymptote.
  \item \textbf{Degree comparison} $\rightarrow$ horizontal asymptote, or ``none.''
  \item \textbf{Intercepts, computed.} $x$-int: zeros of the \emph{simplified} numerator. $y$-int: $f(0)$.
  \item \textbf{Sign chart.} Boundary points $=$ $x$-intercepts $+$ vertical asymptotes. One test value per interval.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Then assemble} and report: domain as a union, end behavior, above/below by interval, and any
crossing of the horizontal asymptote.
&
\vspace{0pt}
\textbf{Worked, on the anchor.} \; $f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{x^2-x-6}=\dfrac{x-1}{(x+2)(x-3)}$
\[ \text{VA } x=-2,\; x=3; \qquad \text{HA } y=0 \;(1<2) \]
\[ x\text{-int } (1,0); \qquad f(0)=\tfrac{-1}{-6}=\tfrac16 \]
\centerline{\small Signs at $-3,\,0,\,2,\,4$: \quad $-\;+\;-\;+$ \quad (below, above, below, above)}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{And with a hole:} \; $g(x)=\dfrac{(x-2)(x+2)}{(x-3)(x+2)}=\dfrac{x-2}{x-3}=1+\dfrac{1}{x-3}$
\[ \text{hole } \left(-2,\tfrac45\right); \quad \text{VA } x=3; \quad \text{HA } y=1 \]
\vspace{-6pt}
\textbf{The two questions students must answer aloud:} \emph{did that factor cancel all the way?} and
\emph{is this zero of the numerator an intercept or a hole?}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
Circulate during the guided practice and Tier work. Watch for and correct:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{restrictions read off the simplified form} --- the hole vanishes and the domain is wrong; this is the single most expensive error today, and it is tomorrow's extraneous solution;
  \item \textbf{a vertical asymptote announced at every excluded value} without running the cancel test;
  \item \textbf{a cancelled zero called an $x$-intercept} --- Worked Example B's $(-2,0)$, which is the hole;
  \item \textbf{the hole used as a boundary point} on the sign chart --- the sign cannot change where the function is the same on both sides;
  \item \textbf{degrees compared factor-by-factor} instead of whole top to whole bottom;
  \item \textbf{``no $x$-intercept'' refused} when the numerator is a nonzero constant --- students want every graph to cross the axis;
  \item \textbf{signs assumed to alternate} rather than tested --- true for these examples, false in general (homework 1(f));
  \item \textbf{one interval spanning a wall} --- 4.0's union rule, still in force;
  \item \textbf{intercepts guessed off the picture} instead of computed from the equation.
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts: ``Did that factor cancel \emph{all the way}?'' ``Hole or wall --- and how do you
know?'' ``Where is the hole, exactly? Give me both coordinates.'' ``Which zeros of the numerator are
intercepts?'' ``Is the hole a boundary point? Why not?'' ``Your chart says positive there --- prove it
with a number.'' ``Does this one cross its horizontal asymptote, and where must the crossing be?''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Group Work \& Differentiation]{redbox}
\textbf{Assemble the Graph} activity (tiered; mirrors the notes).
\begin{multicols}{3}
\textbf{Tier R --- Remediate.} One step at a time. A four-row \textbf{hole-or-wall} sort
($\frac{x-1}{(x-1)(x+4)}$, $\frac{x+2}{(x-3)(x+5)}$, $\frac{x^2-9}{(x+3)(x-1)}$,
$\frac{5}{(x-2)(x+2)}$), four \textbf{degree-comparison} items including a ``none,'' then one guided
build on $f(x)=\frac{3}{(x-1)(x+3)}$. That build is the item to watch: its numerator is the constant
$3$, so it has \emph{no} $x$-intercept, its sign chart has only two boundary points, and it can never
cross $y=0$. A group that writes ``$x$-intercept $=3$'' has confused the numerator's value with its
zero --- a two-minute fix that prevents a much worse error later.

\columnbreak
\textbf{Tier A --- Approaching.} \emph{Part 1:} a full build of $\frac{x-2}{x^2-x-12}$ checked against
a pre-drawn graph, closing on the crossing at $(2,0)$ and why 4.4 could never produce one.
\emph{Part 2:} a build with a hole, $\frac{x^2-2x-3}{x^2-9}$ --- hole $(3,\frac23)$, wall $x=-3$,
HA $y=1$ --- whose item 4 asks why $(3,0)$ is \emph{not} an intercept even though the original
numerator vanishes there. \emph{Part 3:} an \textbf{error analysis} on $\frac{x^2-16}{x^2-3x-4}$, where
a student claims two walls and two intercepts; exactly one of the three claims (the HA) survives,
because it is the only feature the cancel test does not touch.

\columnbreak
\textbf{Tier E --- Extension.} \emph{Part 1:} build one \textbf{backwards} --- hole at $x=1$, wall at
$x=-4$, HA $y=2$, $x$-intercept $(3,0)$ --- giving $\frac{2(x-3)(x-1)}{(x+4)(x-1)}$, plus the hole's
coordinates and why the intercept could not have been at $x=1$. \emph{Part 2:} \textbf{enrichment
only} --- divide $\frac{x^2-4}{x+1}$ (3.3 long division) to get $x-1-\frac{3}{x+1}$ and meet the
\textbf{slant asymptote} $y=x-1$ on a pre-drawn graph. Say out loud that it is not in the standard and
will not be assessed; its job is to show that ``no HA'' still means end behavior, and that the rewrite
is 4.4's move with a linear quotient. \emph{Part 3:} \textbf{the round trip} --- $30$ miles out at $x$
mph and back at $x+10$, so $T(x)=\frac{30}{x}+\frac{30}{x+10}=\frac{60x+300}{x(x+10)}$ (4.3 run
forwards). The wall at $x=0$ and the floor at $y=0$ both mean something; the wall at $x=-10$ and the
intercept $(-5,0)$ are negative speeds. Doubling $10\to20$ saves $2$ hours, $20\to40$ saves only
$1.15$.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment]{redbox}
\textbf{Exit Ticket} (independent, no notes): \textbf{(1)} a full build of
$f(x)=\frac{x^2-1}{x^2+2x-3}$ --- factors $\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x+3)(x-1)}$, restrictions $1$ and $-3$,
hole $(1,\frac12)$, wall $x=-3$, HA $y=1$, $x$-intercept $(-1,0)$, $y$-intercept $(0,\frac13)$, domain
$(-\infty,-3)\cup(-3,1)\cup(1,\infty)$. \textbf{(2)} the sign chart on those boundary points
($+,-,+$; below on $(-3,-1)$) and the crossing question --- \emph{no}, because once the common factor
cancels this is a transformed parent again and $\frac{x+1}{x+3}=1$ would need $x+1=x+3$.
\textbf{(3)} an \textbf{SOL-style multiple-choice} item: which function has a hole at $x=1$, a
vertical asymptote at $x=-2$, and a horizontal asymptote $y=1$? Each distractor breaks exactly one
idea --- $\frac{x-1}{(x-1)(x+2)}$ gets both restrictions right but its degrees give $y=0$,
$\frac{(x+1)(x+3)}{(x+1)(x-2)}$ flips every sign, and $\frac{(x-1)(x+2)}{(x+2)(x+3)}$ cancels the
wrong factor. Collect and sort into ``got it / cancel test / degree comparison / sign chart.'' The
degree pile is the fastest fix. \textbf{The cancel-test pile is the one to re-teach before tomorrow},
because in 4.6 that same error --- a restriction read off the simplified form --- stops being a
mislabeled feature and becomes a wrong answer.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
\textbf{Homework:} six \textbf{hole-or-wall} sorts, ending on $\frac{x^2-9}{x^2-6x+9}$ --- where
$x-3$ sits on \emph{both} top and bottom and there is still no hole, because the bottom has two copies
and only one cancels; four \textbf{degree-comparison} items including a ``none''; two \textbf{full
builds} --- $\frac{x+2}{x^2-2x-8}$, which simplifies to $\frac{1}{x-4}$ and so has a hole at
$(-2,-\frac16)$, no $x$-intercept, and only one boundary point, and $\frac{x-3}{x^2-4}$, with four
intervals and a crossing of $y=0$ at $(3,0)$; a \textbf{graph to identify} (hole, wall $x=1$, HA
$y=1$) among four candidates, followed by rewriting $\frac{x+1}{x-1}=1+\frac{2}{x-1}$ as a transformed
parent; an \textbf{error analysis} in which a student simplifies $\frac{x^2-x-6}{x^2-9}$ correctly and
still gets the domain wrong --- the unit's spine, answered with 4.1's sentence, \emph{cancelling never
restores an input}; and a \textbf{pollution-cost model}, $C(p)=\frac{25p}{100-p}$ thousand dollars to
remove $p\%$, where the run $25\to100\to225\to475\to2475$ makes the vertical asymptote at $p=100$ mean
something (no finite budget reaches total removal) and the horizontal asymptote $y=-25$ mean nothing at
all --- the reverse of the activity's model, and deliberately so. \textbf{Extension:} design a function
with walls at $x=-1$ and $x=4$, HA $y=0$, and \emph{no} $x$-intercept (the two conditions together force
a nonzero constant numerator, and every such constant works), plus the careful question of whether a
hole and a wall can share an $x$-value, tested against $\frac{x-3}{(x-3)^2}$. \textbf{Preview:} Lesson
4.6, \emph{Solving Rational Equations \& Extraneous Solutions} --- the excluded values students have
been computing all unit stop being features of a graph and come back as answers that must be thrown
away.
\end{skillbox}

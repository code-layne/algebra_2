\begin{remindbox}
\small A \textbf{rational function} $R(x)=\dfrac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$ is a \emph{fraction of polynomials}, and
the \textbf{denominator runs the show}. Every input making $Q(x)=0$ is a \textbf{domain restriction},
thrown out because division by zero is undefined --- which is why this is the first family of the year
whose graph can be \textbf{discontinuous} (it comes in pieces). A restriction whose factor
\emph{does not cancel} becomes a \textbf{vertical asymptote} $x=a$: a wall the graph approaches but can
\emph{never} touch. A restriction whose factor \emph{does cancel} becomes a \textbf{hole} --- a single
missing point. At the far ends the graph usually flattens toward a \textbf{horizontal asymptote}
$y=b$, which \emph{is} the end-behavior statement, and which the graph \emph{may} cross in the middle.
Everything else is old work on a new picture: \textbf{zeros} come from the \emph{numerator},
the \textbf{$y$-intercept} from $R(0)$, and \textbf{domain}, \textbf{range},
\textbf{increasing/decreasing}, and \textbf{positive/negative} intervals now get written as
\textbf{unions}, because no interval may cross a vertical asymptote.
\end{remindbox}

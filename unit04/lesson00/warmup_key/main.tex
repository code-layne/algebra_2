\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Skills We'll Use Today}
\small These skills power today's lesson on reading polynomial graphs. Work quickly.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{1. Powers of a negative number.} Watch the signs.
\[
  (-2)^2 = \ans{4} \qquad (-2)^3 = \ans{-8} \qquad (-2)^4 = \ans{16}
  \qquad -2^4 = \ans{-16}
\]
Complete the rule: an \textbf{even} power of a negative number is \ans{positive}; an \textbf{odd}
power \ans{keeps} the sign.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{2. Evaluate a cubic.} Let $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 2$. Complete the table.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$x$ & $-2$ & $-1$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ \\ \hline
$f(x)$ & \ans{0} & \ans{4} & \ans{2} & \ans{0} & \ans{4} \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Read your outputs left to right: they go \emph{up}, then \emph{down}, then \emph{up} again. A
straight line never does this and a parabola turns only once --- so this graph must turn
\ans{2} times.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{3. Zeros from a factored form.} The \textbf{zeros} of a function are the inputs that make it
$0$. Set each factor equal to $0$ and solve.
\[
  g(x) = (x-1)^2(x+2): \qquad x = \ans{1} \quad \text{and} \quad x = \ans{-2}
\]

\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
Item~1 seeds \emph{even/odd} behavior and end behavior (even powers erase the sign; odd powers keep
it). Item~2's outputs $0,4,2,0,4$ are exactly the points students will see on today's anchor graph
$g(x)=x^3-3x+2$ --- note aloud that the two zeros ($x=-2$ and $x=1$) and the ``up-down-up'' pattern
foreshadow \emph{two} turning points. Item~3 previews \emph{multiplicity}: the repeated factor
$(x-1)^2$ is why the graph will \emph{touch} at $x=1$ rather than cross.
\end{teachernote}

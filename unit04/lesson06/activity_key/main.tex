\begin{headlinebox}{forestbg}
\small\textbf{Solve, Then Check.} Same four steps every time, and always in this order:
\textbf{(1)} factor every denominator and \emph{write the restriction list first}; \textbf{(2)} multiply
every term by the LCD; \textbf{(3)} solve what is left; \textbf{(4)} check each candidate against the
list, throw away any match, and verify the survivors. A number is not an answer until Step 4 says so.
\end{headlinebox}

\vspace{0.06in}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40, title=\textbf{Tier R --- Remediate},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=1.5mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm, breakable]
\textbf{Part 1 --- The list, before anything else.} Do \emph{not} solve. Just factor the denominators
and write every excluded value.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.7}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X X@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Equation} & \textbf{Restrictions} & \textbf{LCD} \\
\hline
\TallMath{\frac{3}{x}+\frac{1}{x-2}=1}          & \ans{$x\neq0,\,2$}  & \ans{$x(x-2)$} \\
\TallMath{\frac{x}{x+5}=\frac{4}{x-1}}          & \ans{$x\neq-5,\,1$} & \ans{$(x+5)(x-1)$} \\
\TallMath{\frac{2}{x-4}+\frac{1}{x+4}=\frac{6}{x^2-16}} & \ans{$x\neq4,\,-4$} & \ans{$(x-4)(x+4)$} \\
\TallMath{\frac{5}{x^2-x}=\frac{1}{x}}          & \ans{$x\neq0,\,1$}  & \ans{$x(x-1)$} \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Part 2 --- Solution, or not?} Each candidate below is already given to you. Decide, and say
\emph{why} in a few words. (Two of them are fine. Two are not.)
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2.1em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item $\dfrac{x}{x-5}=\dfrac{5}{x-5}$, \; candidate $x=5$: \; \ans{not a solution} \; because
        \ans{$5$ makes both denominators $0$ --- it is extraneous}
  \item $\dfrac{4}{x}+\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{5}{x}$, \; candidate $x=2$: \; \ans{solution} \; because
        \ans{$2+\frac12=\frac52$, and $2$ is not excluded}
  \item $\dfrac{1}{x+3}=\dfrac{2}{x^2-9}$, \; candidate $x=3$: \; \ans{not a solution} \; because
        \ans{$x^2-9=0$ at $x=3$; the right side does not exist}
  \item $\dfrac{2}{x-1}=\dfrac{x}{x+2}$, \; candidate $x=-1$: \; \ans{solution} \; because
        \ans{both sides equal $-1$, and $-1$ is not excluded}
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{2pt}
{\small One of these is a \emph{negative} answer that is perfectly good. Being negative is not a reason
to reject anything.}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Part 3 --- Twins.} These two equations differ by a single digit. Run all four steps on each.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{(a)} \; $\dfrac{x}{x-4}=\dfrac{4}{x-4}+3$ &
\textbf{(b)} \; $\dfrac{x}{x-4}=\dfrac{2}{x-4}+3$ \\[6pt]
Restrictions: $x\neq$ \ans{$4$} & Restrictions: $x\neq$ \ans{$4$} \\[4pt]
Multiply by $x-4$: \; $x=$ \ans{$4+3(x-4)$} & Multiply by $x-4$: \; $x=$ \ans{$2+3(x-4)$} \\[4pt]
Solve: \; $x=$ \ans{$4$} & Solve: \; $x=$ \ans{$5$} \\[4pt]
Check: \ans{$4$ is excluded} & Check: \ans{$5$ is not excluded} \\[4pt]
Answer: \ans{no solution} & Answer: \ans{$x=5$} \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
\vspace{-2pt}
The algebra was the same both times. What made the answers different? \ansline{where the candidate
landed --- in (a) it landed on the restriction list, in (b) it did not}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40, title=\textbf{Tier A --- Approaching Proficiency},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=1.5mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm, breakable]
\textbf{Part 1 --- A full solve, with one to throw away.} \;
$\dfrac{x}{x-2}+\dfrac{2}{x+2}=\dfrac{8}{x^2-4}$
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*., leftmargin=2.1em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item Factor the last denominator: $x^2-4=({\color{keyred}\mathbf{x-2}})({\color{keyred}\mathbf{x+2}})$. \;
        Restrictions $x\neq$ \ans{$2,\,-2$}. \; LCD $=$ \ans{$(x-2)(x+2)$}
  \item Multiply every term and cancel: \;
        $x({\color{keyred}\mathbf{x+2}})+2({\color{keyred}\mathbf{x-2}})={\color{keyred}\mathbf{8}}$
  \item Expand and collect: \; $0=x^2+{\color{keyred}\mathbf{4}}\,x-{\color{keyred}\mathbf{12}}
        =(x+{\color{keyred}\mathbf{6}})(x-{\color{keyred}\mathbf{2}})$. \; Candidates \ans{$-6,\;2$}
  \item Check against the list. Extraneous: \ans{$2$} \; Survivor: \ans{$-6$}
  \item Verify the survivor in the \emph{original}: left side $=$ \ans{$\frac34-\frac12=\frac14$}, right side
        $=$ \ans{$\frac{8}{32}=\frac14$}. \; Solution set \ans{$\{-6\}$}
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{Part 2 --- Find the answer on a graph.} Consider $\dfrac{x^2}{x+3}=\dfrac{9}{x+3}$.

\vspace{2pt}
Subtract the right side from the left and combine into one fraction:
\[ \frac{x^2}{x+3}-\frac{9}{x+3}=\frac{{\color{keyred}\mathbf{x^2-9}}}{x+3}
   ={\color{keyred}\mathbf{x-3}},\qquad x\neq{\color{keyred}\mathbf{-3}} \]
Solving the equation means finding where that combined function equals \ans{$0$}. Here is its
graph.
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.38]
  \lgrid{-7}{7}{-9}{5}
  \begin{scope}
    \clip (-7,-9) rectangle (7,5);
    \draw[very thick, forest] (-7,-10)--(7,4);
  \end{scope}
  \draw[forest, very thick, fill=white] (-3,-6) circle (4.4pt);
  \node[charcoal, font=\scriptsize, right] at (-2.6,-6.6) {$(-3,-6)$};
  \fill[charcoal] (3,0) circle (3.4pt) node[above left, font=\scriptsize]{$(3,0)$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\vspace{-4pt}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2.1em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item Read the solution off the graph: $x=$ \ans{$3$}, because that is the \ans{$x$-intercept}.
  \item Now solve the equation algebraically. Candidates \ans{$3,\;-3$}; extraneous \ans{$-3$};
        solution set \ans{$\{3\}$}.
  \item The extraneous candidate is at the \ans{hole} in the picture. In one sentence, explain
        what the picture is telling you that the candidate list alone did not. \ansline{the function
        has no point at all at $x=-3$, so there is nothing there to equal zero --- the candidate is a
        gap in the graph, not a crossing}
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Part 3 --- Error analysis.} A student solved $\dfrac{2}{x-5}+\dfrac{3}{x}=\dfrac{10}{x^2-5x}$
and wrote:
\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.80\linewidth}{\centering \vspace{2pt}
``LCD is $x(x-5)$. So $2x+3(x-5)=10$, \\
$5x-15=10$, \; $5x=25$, \; $x=5$. \\
The solution is $x=5$.''\vspace{2pt}}}
\end{center}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2.1em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item Is the LCD correct? \ans{yes} \; Is the cleared equation correct? \ans{yes} \;
        Is the arithmetic correct? \ans{yes}
  \item So the student made no mistake at all in Steps 1--3. What did they skip? \ans{Step 4 --- the
        check against the restriction list}
  \item Restrictions: $x\neq$ \ans{$0,\;5$}. \; Correct answer: \ans{no solution}
  \item Write the one sentence the student should have written, using the words \emph{extraneous} and
        \emph{denominator}. \ansline{$x=5$ is extraneous, because it makes the denominators $x-5$ and
        $x^2-5x$ equal to zero, so the original equation is undefined there and has no solution}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40, title=\textbf{Tier E --- Extension},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=1.5mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm, breakable]
\textbf{Part 1 --- Build the equation yourself.} Mia and Dev are painting a mural. Working alone, Dev
takes \textbf{3 hours longer} than Mia. Working together, they finish in \textbf{2 hours}.

\vspace{2pt}
{\small In one hour, a person who needs $t$ hours for the whole job finishes $\frac{1}{t}$ of it.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2.1em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item Let $x=$ the hours \emph{Mia} needs alone. Then Dev needs \ans{$x+3$} hours. In one hour
        Mia paints \ans{$\frac1x$} of the mural and Dev paints \ans{$\frac{1}{x+3}$}, and together they
        paint \ans{$\frac12$} of it.
  \item Write the equation: \; \ans{$\dfrac{1}{x}+\dfrac{1}{x+3}=\dfrac12$}
  \item Restrictions (from the algebra): $x\neq$ \ans{$0,\;-3$}. \; Solve it. Candidates
        \ans{$3,\;-2$}
  \item Neither candidate is on the restriction list --- so \emph{both} of them really do solve the
        equation. Check $x=-2$ in your equation to confirm it: \ans{$-\frac12+1=\frac12$, true}
  \item You still have to reject $x=-2$. \textbf{Why?} And why is that a \emph{different} kind of
        rejection from an extraneous solution? \ansline{a job cannot take $-2$ hours, so $-2$ is
        rejected by the \emph{context}, not by the algebra --- it genuinely satisfies the equation,
        while an extraneous root does not satisfy it at all}
  \item Answer the question that was asked: Mia alone takes \ans{$3$} hours; Dev alone takes
        \ans{$6$} hours.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{Part 2 --- Make one on purpose.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2.1em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item Write a rational equation whose \emph{only} candidate is $x=3$ and for which $x=3$ is
        extraneous --- so the equation has no solution. \;
        \ans{e.g.\ $\dfrac{x}{x-3}=\dfrac{3}{x-3}$ (answers vary)}
  \item Explain how you forced it. \ansline{put the target value in the denominator so it is excluded,
        then arrange the numerators so that clearing produces an equation whose only root is that same
        value}
  \item Harder: write one with candidates $x=4$ and $x=-1$, where $x=-1$ is extraneous and $x=4$ is a
        genuine solution. \; \ans{e.g.\ $\dfrac{x^2-3x}{x+1}=\dfrac{4}{x+1}$ (answers vary)}
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{Part 3 --- Can clearing the denominators ever \emph{lose} a solution?} Multiplying by the LCD
can hand you extra answers. This question asks about the opposite direction.

\vspace{2pt}
Take $\dfrac{x^2}{x-1}=\dfrac{x}{x-1}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2.1em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item Restrictions \ans{$x\neq1$}. Clear the denominator: \; \ans{$x^2=x$}. \; Candidates
        \ans{$0,\;1$}. \; Extraneous \ans{$1$}. \; Solution set \ans{$\{0\}$}
  \item A second student clears the denominator, gets $x^2=x$, and then \emph{divides both sides by
        $x$} to get $x=1$. They report $x=1$. Two things went wrong at once --- name them both.
        \ansline{dividing by $x$ threw away the root $x=0$, which was the only real solution; and they
        never checked, so they kept $x=1$, which is extraneous}
  \item Fill in the two rules. Multiplying both sides by an expression that could be zero can
        \ans{add} solutions, so you must \ans{check} at the end. Dividing both sides by an
        expression that could be zero can \ans{lose} solutions, so you should
        \ans{factor and set each factor to zero} instead.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{teachernote}
\textbf{Tier R Part 3 is the tier's whole point.} The two equations are identical except for one digit,
and the four steps run the same way on both. Groups that finish quickly will notice that the ``$+3$''
never mattered and the numerator did --- push them to say \emph{why}: in (a) the cleared equation's root
happens to coincide with the excluded value, and in (b) it does not. There is nothing about the
\emph{process} that predicts which. That is exactly why the check is mandatory.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Tier R Part 2(d) is deliberate.} Students routinely reject negative answers on reflex. Here
$x=-1$ is a genuine solution and the number they should reject, in 2(a) and 2(c), is positive. If a
group rejects (d), stop and make them substitute.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Tier A Part 2 is the graphical-verification requirement} (A2.EI.4b/c) and it is the item to
debrief with the whole class if you debrief anything. The combined function is a \emph{line with a
hole} --- $y=x-3$, with the point at $x=-3$ removed --- so the picture is unmistakable: the true
solution is where the graph crosses the $x$-axis, and the extraneous candidate is the missing point.
Students with graphing technology can reproduce this in ten seconds for any equation on the page.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Tier A Part 3 is the error analysis with no error in it.} That framing matters: every line of
the student's algebra is correct, and the answer is still wrong. It is the cleanest available argument
that checking is part of solving rather than a proofreading habit. Expect at least one group to insist
the student must have made an arithmetic slip.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Tier E Part 1 draws the distinction the standard asks for} (A2.EI.4c vs.\ A2.EI.4d). Both
candidates satisfy the equation; $-2$ is thrown out because a job cannot take negative hours, not
because it breaks a denominator. Do not let ``extraneous'' become a synonym for ``rejected.''

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Tier E Part 3 answers a question sharp students ask} the first time they meet extraneous roots:
if multiplying can add solutions, can it also lose them? It cannot --- but \emph{dividing} can, and the
example is engineered so that the careless division discards the only real solution and keeps the
extraneous one. Both failure modes in a single line of work.
\end{teachernote}

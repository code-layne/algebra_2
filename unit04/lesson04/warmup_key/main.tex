\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Finishing Yesterday's Problem}
\small Lesson 4.3 gave you the tools to crack any polynomial --- \emph{once somebody told you which
number to test}. Today you build that list of numbers yourself. Two pieces of arithmetic and one
unfinished problem are all it takes.

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{1. Divisors.} List \emph{every} integer that divides each number evenly, positives and
negatives.

\vspace{2pt}
Divisors of $6$: \ans{$\pm 1,\ \pm 2,\ \pm 3,\ \pm 6$} \qquad Divisors of $2$: \ans{$\pm 1,\ \pm 2$}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{2. Every fraction you can build.} Take a numerator from the first list and a denominator
from the second. Write each fraction $\dfrac{p}{q}$ in lowest terms, and cross out repeats.

\vspace{3pt}
Numerators $p$: $1, 2, 3, 6$ \qquad Denominators $q$: $1, 2$

\vspace{3pt}
All the different positive values of $\dfrac{p}{q}$: \ans{$1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 6,\ \tfrac12,\ \tfrac32$}
\; \ans{($\tfrac22 = 1$ and $\tfrac62 = 3$ are repeats)}

\vspace{3pt}
How many values is that, counting both signs? \ans{$12$}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{3. Unfinished business.} Yesterday your group tested the divisors of $6$ on
$h(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 11x + 6$ and found that $r = -2$ and $r = 3$ both gave $0$. Divide out $(x-3)$.

\vspace{2pt}
The divisor is $x - 3$, so $r = $ \ans{$3$}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{r|C{1.4cm}C{1.4cm}C{1.4cm}C{1.4cm}}
\ans{$3$} & $2$ & $-3$ & $-11$ & $6$ \\
    &     & \ans{$6$} & \ans{$9$} & \ans{$-6$} \\ \cline{2-5}
    & \ans{$2$} & \ans{$3$} & \ans{$-2$} & \ans{$0$} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{2pt}
Depressed polynomial: \ans{$2x^2 + 3x - 2$} \quad Remainder: \ans{$0$}

\vspace{3pt}
Factor that quotient (Lesson 4.2 tools): \ans{$(2x-1)(x+2)$} \; so $h(x) = $
\ans{$(x-3)(2x-1)(x+2)$}

\vspace{3pt}
The three zeros of $h$ are \ans{$3,\ \tfrac12,\ -2$}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{4. The gap.} Your candidate list yesterday was $\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \pm 6$ --- the divisors
of the constant term $6$. One zero of $h$ is \emph{not} on that list.

\vspace{2pt}
Which one? \ans{$\tfrac12$} \; Where do you think its denominator came from? \ans{the leading
coefficient, $2$}

\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
Every piece of today's theorem is on this page before it is named. Item~1 produces the two divisor
lists the Rational Root Theorem asks for; item~2 assembles them into $\frac{p}{q}$ and --- crucially
--- makes students reduce and discard duplicates, which is the single most common bookkeeping error
in the lesson. Twelve candidates is a big number; let them feel it, because the payoff line in the
notes is that twelve is finite and infinity is not.

\vspace{3pt}
Item~3 is the Tier E preview task from Lesson~4.3's activity, finished. Groups who ran out of time
yesterday get closure; groups who finished get a second look at the number that matters. The
depressed polynomial $2x^2+3x-2$ factors as $(2x-1)(x+2)$, and the factor $2x-1$ --- with a leading
coefficient that is not $1$ --- is exactly where the fractional zero comes from.

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Debrief items 2 and 4 together.} Ask: \textbf{``Your fraction list in item 2 --- is
$\tfrac12$ on it?''} It is. \textbf{``And where did the $2$ in the denominator come from?''} Take
answers; the leading coefficient of $h$ is $2$. Do not state the theorem yet. That is the first
minute of the notes, and it lands much harder if students have already built the list by hand.
\end{teachernote}

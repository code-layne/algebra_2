\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: From Reading Lines to Writing Them}
\small Yesterday you \emph{read} a line's features off its graph. Today you'll \emph{write} the
equation. These three quick skills are the tools you'll need.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{1. Read it off the graph.} The line below is graphed on the grid.
\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.42\linewidth}\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.42]
  \lgrid{-3}{3}{-4}{5}
  \draw[very thick, forest] (-1,-3)--(3,5);
  \fill[charcoal] (0,-1) circle (3.5pt) node[above left, font=\scriptsize]{$(0,-1)$};
  \fill[charcoal] (1,1) circle (3.5pt);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{minipage}%
\begin{minipage}{0.5\linewidth}
  $y$-intercept: $(0,\ $\ans{$-1$}$)$ \\[10pt]
  Slope $m = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = $ \ans{$\tfrac{2}{1}=2$} \\[10pt]
  {\footnotesize (Count from $(0,-1)$ to $(1,1)$.)}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{2. Slope from two points.} Use $m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$ for the points $(-2,5)$ and
$(2,-3)$.
\[
  m = \frac{\textcolor{keyred}{\mathbf{-3}}-\textcolor{keyred}{\mathbf{5}}}%
           {\textcolor{keyred}{\mathbf{2}}-(\textcolor{keyred}{\mathbf{-2}})}
    = \frac{\textcolor{keyred}{\mathbf{-8}}}{\textcolor{keyred}{\mathbf{4}}}
    = \textcolor{keyred}{\mathbf{-2}}
\]

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{3. Name the parts.} A line is written $y = \tfrac{3}{4}x - 5$. Without graphing, its
slope is $m = $ \ans{$\tfrac{3}{4}$} and its $y$-intercept is $(0,\ $\ans{$-5$}$)$.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
Item~2 is the engine for the whole lesson --- keep an eye on subtraction order: top and bottom must
run in the \emph{same} direction. Item~3 previews the payoff of slope-intercept form: once a line is
written $y=mx+b$, $m$ and $b$ are simply \emph{read off}, no work required. That contrast (compute
vs.\ read) motivates why we learn to convert among forms.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{remindbox}
\small A quadratic function graphs as a \textbf{parabola} you can build by transforming the parent
$y=x^2$. Its equation comes in three \emph{forms}, each cut to reveal a different feature:
\textbf{vertex form} $f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k$ hands you the \textbf{vertex} $(h,k)$ and axis $x=h$;
\textbf{standard form} $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$ hands you the \textbf{$y$-intercept} $(0,c)$ and the axis
$x=-\tfrac{b}{2a}$; \textbf{intercept form} $f(x)=a(x-p)(x-q)$ hands you the \textbf{zeros} $p$ and $q$.
The number $a$ is the same in all three: its \emph{sign} sets the opening direction (up if $a>0$, down
if $a<0$) and its \emph{size} $|a|$ sets the width. Three costumes --- one curve.
\end{remindbox}

\boxguard[8]
\begin{remindbox}
\small
Every quadratic is the \textbf{parent} $y=x^2$ moved and reshaped. In \textbf{vertex form}
$y=a(x-h)^2+k$ the vertex is $(h,k)$ and the axis of symmetry is $x=h$; $a<0$ flips it to open
down (the vertex is a \textbf{maximum}), and $|a|>1$ makes it narrower, $|a|<1$ wider. In
\textbf{standard form} $y=ax^2+bx+c$ the $y$-intercept is $c$ and the axis is $x=-\frac{b}{2a}$.
In \textbf{intercept form} $y=a(x-p)(x-q)$ the zeros are $p$ and $q$. Watch the bracket: the
sign flips --- $(x-1)^2$ moves \emph{right}, and $(x+1)$ means a zero at $-1$.
\end{remindbox}

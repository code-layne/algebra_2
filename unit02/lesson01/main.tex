\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=forestbg, colframe=forest, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students can move fluently among the three algebraic forms of a
    linear function --- \emph{slope-intercept} $y = mx + b$, \emph{point-slope}
    $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$, and \emph{standard} $Ax + By = C$ --- computing \emph{slope} as a rate
    of change and \emph{writing the equation of a line} from a graph, from two points, or from a
    slope and a point. They also graph a line by \emph{transforming the parent function} $y = x$,
    reading a vertical stretch/reflection as a change in slope and a vertical shift as a change in
    the $y$-intercept. Lesson~2.0 \emph{read} these features off a graph; today students
    \emph{produce} the equation and the graph.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} This lesson reactivates and consolidates the Algebra~1
    linear-function cluster \textbf{A.F.1} as the foundation of Unit~2:
    \textbf{A.F.1a} (slope and intercepts as rate of change), \textbf{A.F.1b} (transformations of
    the parent $y=x$ affect slope and $y$-intercept), \textbf{A.F.1c} (write and interpret the
    slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard forms), \textbf{A.F.1d} (write the equation of a
    line from a graph, from two points, or from a slope and a point), and \textbf{A.F.1e}
    (parallel and perpendicular lines). It launches the \emph{transformational} lens
    ($f(x)+k,\ kf(x),\ f(x+k)$) that Algebra~2 \textbf{A2.F.1} extends to every later function
    family.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Ideas \& Skills} & \textbf{Key Understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Compute \textbf{slope} from two points: $m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$.
  \item Write a line in \textbf{slope-intercept} form $y=mx+b$ from a slope and $y$-intercept.
  \item Write a line in \textbf{point-slope} form $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$ from a slope and \emph{any} point.
  \item Rewrite a line in \textbf{standard} form $Ax+By=C$ and convert among all three forms.
  \item \textbf{Graph} a line by transforming the parent $y=x$ (stretch/reflect $\to$ slope; shift $\to$ intercept).
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item The three forms are the \emph{same line in different outfits} --- each makes different information easy to read.
  \item \textbf{Slope-intercept} shows the rate and starting value; \textbf{point-slope} is built for ``a slope and a point''; \textbf{standard} is tidy for intercepts and integer coefficients.
  \item Every non-vertical line is the parent $y=x$ after a vertical \emph{stretch/reflection} (new slope) and a vertical \emph{shift} (new $y$-intercept).
  \item \textbf{Parallel} lines share a slope; \textbf{perpendicular} slopes are opposite reciprocals ($m_1 m_2 = -1$).
  \item Vertical lines $x=a$ (undefined slope) and horizontal lines $y=c$ (slope $0$) are the special cases.
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Slope / rate of change} & $m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$: the constant change in output per unit change in input. \\
\textbf{Slope-intercept form} & $y=mx+b$, where $m$ is the slope and $(0,b)$ is the $y$-intercept. \\
\textbf{Point-slope form} & $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$: built directly from a slope $m$ and a known point $(x_1,y_1)$. \\
\textbf{Standard form} & $Ax+By=C$ with $A,B,C$ integers and $A\ge 0$; convenient for intercepts. \\
\textbf{Parent function} & The simplest line $y=x$ (slope $1$, through the origin); all lines are transformations of it. \\
\textbf{Transformation} & $kf(x)$ stretches/reflects (changes the slope); $f(x)+k$ shifts vertically (changes the $y$-intercept). \\
\textbf{Parallel / perpendicular} & Same slope / opposite-reciprocal slopes: $m_2=m_1$ vs.\ $m_2=-\dfrac{1}{m_1}$. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{fixedskillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review]{forestbg}
    The Warm-Up rehearses exactly what Lesson~2.0 built and what today extends: \textbf{(1)} read the
    \emph{slope} and \emph{$y$-intercept} straight off a graphed line; \textbf{(2)} compute a slope
    from two points with $m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$ (Algebra~1 prerequisite); and \textbf{(3)}
    identify $m$ and $b$ in a function already written as $y=mx+b$. Debrief item~2 aloud: the slope
    formula is just ``rise over run'' done with subtraction --- and it is the engine for every
    equation we write today.
\end{fixedskillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{forestbg}
    A streaming service charges a \textbf{\$5 signup fee} plus \textbf{\$2 each month}. Ask students
    to fill a quick cost table ($m=0,1,2,3$ months $\to \$5,\$7,\$9,\$11$) and pose:
    \textbf{``What stays the same every month?''} (the $+\$2$ rate $=$ \emph{slope}),
    \textbf{``What is the cost before any months pass?''} (the $\$5$ start $=$ \emph{$y$-intercept}),
    and \textbf{``Can you write one rule for the cost after $m$ months?''} Students should assemble
    $C(m) = 2m + 5$ on their own. Land the point: \emph{a rate of change plus a starting value is
    exactly slope-intercept form} --- today we learn three ways to write and rewrite that rule, and
    how to build it when the ``starting value'' isn't handed to us.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson]{forestbg}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{1. Slope as a rate of change.} From any two points on a line,
$m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$. Model it on $(1,4)$ and $(3,0)$: $m=\dfrac{0-4}{3-1}=-2$.
\textbf{Why can we use \emph{any} two points?} Because a line's rate of change is constant --- the
defining property from Lesson~2.0.

\textbf{2. Slope-intercept form $y=mx+b$.} Read $m$ (rate) and $b$ ($y$-intercept) directly. For
$y=2x-4$: slope $2$, $y$-intercept $(0,-4)$. This is the form the Hook produced, $C(m)=2m+5$.

\textbf{3. Point-slope form $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$.} When you know a slope and \emph{a point that isn't
the $y$-intercept}, this form writes the line \emph{immediately}. Using $(2,0)$ with slope $2$:
$y-0=2(x-2)$, which simplifies to $y=2x-4$ --- the same line as step~2.

\columnbreak

\textbf{4. Standard form $Ax+By=C$.} Rearrange $y=2x-4$ to $2x-y=4$. Stress the conventions:
integer $A,B,C$ and $A\ge 0$. \textbf{Which form makes each intercept easiest?} Standard form ---
set $x=0$ then $y=0$.

\textbf{5. Writing equations (the main event).} Three givens, one strategy each: \emph{(a)} slope
$+$ $y$-intercept $\to$ plug into $y=mx+b$; \emph{(b)} slope $+$ any point $\to$ point-slope, then
simplify; \emph{(c)} two points $\to$ compute $m$ first, then use point-slope. Work $(1,4),(3,0)$:
$m=-2$, $y-0=-2(x-3)$, so $y=-2x+6$.

\textbf{6. Graphing by transforming $y=x$.} Every line is the parent $y=x$ after a vertical
\emph{stretch/reflection} (the $kf(x)$ that sets the slope) and a vertical \emph{shift} (the
$f(x)+k$ that sets the $y$-intercept). For $y=2x-4$: stretch by $2$ (slope $1\to2$), then shift
down $4$. Preview: this $f(x)+k,\ kf(x)$ language is how we will graph \emph{every} function family
in this course.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Explicit Instruction: Writing a Line from Two Points]{forestbg}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{Steps.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, nosep]
  \item Label the points $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$.
  \item Slope $m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$ --- subtract in the \emph{same order} top and bottom.
  \item Substitute $m$ and \emph{either} point into $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$.
  \item Simplify to $y=mx+b$; if standard form is asked, clear to $Ax+By=C$ with $A\ge 0$.
\end{enumerate}

\columnbreak
\textbf{Worked: $(1,4)$ and $(3,0)$.}
\[ m=\frac{0-4}{3-1}=\frac{-4}{2}=-2 \]
Point-slope with $(3,0)$:
\[ y-0=-2(x-3) \]
Slope-intercept: $y=-2x+6$.\\
Standard: $2x+y=6$.\\
{\footnotesize Check with $(1,4)$: $-2(1)+6=4$~\checkmark}
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
Circulate during the notes practice and Tier work. Watch for and correct:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item slope computed with mismatched order --- $\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_1-x_2}$ --- flipping the sign;
  \item ``upside-down'' slope, $\dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta y}$ (run over rise);
  \item point-slope sign slips with negatives: $y-(-1)=m(x-(-3))$ becoming $y-1=m(x-3)$;
  \item standard form left with fractions or a negative leading coefficient (enforce integers, $A\ge 0$);
  \item confusing the \emph{stretch} ($kf(x)$, changes slope) with the \emph{shift} ($f(x)+k$, changes intercept).
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts: ``Which form should you \emph{start} from with these givens, and why?''
``What does $b$ mean in the streaming context?'' ``Same line or different --- how do you know?''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Group Work \& Differentiation]{redbox}
\textbf{Building Lines} activity (tiered; mirrors the notes).
\begin{multicols}{3}
\textbf{Tier R --- Remediate.} Identify $m$ and $b$ from lines already in $y=mx+b$, then match each
to a pre-drawn graph by its slope and $y$-intercept.

\columnbreak
\textbf{Tier A --- Approaching.} Write equations from a slope and a point and from two points; use
point-slope, then convert to slope-intercept and standard form.

\columnbreak
\textbf{Tier E --- Extension.} Transform the parent $y=x$ to a target line and describe the
stretch/shift; write parallel and perpendicular lines through a given point; interpret slope and
intercept in a context.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment]{redbox}
\textbf{Exit Ticket} (independent, no notes): from a graphed line, write the equation in
slope-intercept form and convert it to standard form; write the equation of a line through two given
points; and a \textbf{justification check} --- decide whether a second equation names the
\emph{same} line and explain how the forms show it. Also included: one \textbf{SOL-style
multiple-choice} item (identify the standard-form equation of a given line). Collect and sort into
``got it / slope-formula slip / point-slope sign slip / form-conversion slip'' to decide whether
Lesson~2.2 opens with a quick re-teach.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
\textbf{Homework:} compute slopes from two points; write equations from all three types of givens;
convert a line among slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard form; and describe the
transformations of $y=x$ that produce a given line. \textbf{Extension:} write parallel and
perpendicular lines through a point, and interpret the slope and intercept of the streaming-cost
model $C(m)=2m+5$ in context. \textbf{Preview:} Lesson~2.2, \emph{Absolute Value Equations \&
Inequalities} --- solving $|ax+b|=c$ and related inequalities, the algebra that Lesson~2.3 will
turn into the V-shaped graph.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Item~2 is the engine for the whole lesson --- keep an eye on subtraction order: top and bottom must
run in the \emph{same} direction. Item~3 previews the payoff of slope-intercept form: once a line is
written $y=mx+b$, $m$ and $b$ are simply \emph{read off}, no work required. That contrast (compute
vs.\ read) motivates why we learn to convert among forms.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes]
The through-line is ``a slope and a point.'' Point-slope is the workhorse: emphasize that students
can use \emph{either} given point and still land on the same simplified equation --- have a fast
group check with $(1,4)$ in Section~3. Two errors to pre-empt: (1) subtraction-order sign flips in
the slope formula; (2) double-negative slips in point-slope, e.g.\ $y-(-5)$ becoming $y-5$. In
Section~4, keep ``stretch/reflect $\to$ slope'' and ``shift $\to$ intercept'' as two separate moves
--- this is the exact language Units 3--8 reuse.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Group Activity]
Tier~A item~3 is the conceptual crux: ``same line, different form'' is verified by \emph{algebra},
not appearance. In Tier~E, watch the perpendicular slope --- students often negate \emph{or}
reciprocate but not both; $m=2 \Rightarrow m_\perp = -\tfrac12$. Item~3's break-even ties slope
(rate) and intercept (fixed cost) back to the streaming Hook; accept a clear table or an equation
for full credit.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Exit Ticket]
Sort responses into ``got it / slope-formula slip / point-slope sign slip / form-conversion slip.''
Item~1b and item~3 both test clearing fractions and keeping $A\ge 0$ --- the most common miss is
leaving a negative leading coefficient (choice C) or a sign error (choice B). Item~2's telling error
is subtraction order in the slope; a student who gets $-3$ likely subtracted the coordinates in
opposite orders. If several miss item~3, reteach ``multiply through to clear the fraction, then move
$x$ to the left'' before Lesson~2.2.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
Items 3 and 4 are the conversion core --- watch for a lost negative when clearing $-2y$ in item~4
($4x-2y=10 \Rightarrow y=2x-5$, \emph{not} $y=2x+5$). Item~7 is the conceptual target: parallel
means equal $m$, different $b$; a full-credit answer names both. In the Extension, the common slip is
using $\tfrac13$ (not $-3$) for the perpendicular slope --- reinforce ``opposite \emph{and}
reciprocal.''
\end{teachernote}

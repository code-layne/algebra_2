    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students can read the graph of a linear function and name its key
    characteristics --- \emph{domain} and \emph{range}, the \emph{$x$- and $y$-intercepts}
    (the $x$-intercept is the \emph{zero}), the \emph{slope} as a \emph{constant rate of change},
    whether the line is \emph{increasing}, \emph{decreasing}, or \emph{constant}, and the intervals
    where the function is \emph{positive} or \emph{negative} --- and justify each feature from the
    graph. This is the ``read-a-graph'' toolkit the whole course will reuse.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} \textbf{A2.F.2} --- investigate and analyze the characteristics
    of functions graphically. This Lesson~0 launches that toolkit on linear graphs:
    \textbf{A2.F.2a} (determine domain, range, zeros, and intercepts from a graph),
    \textbf{A2.F.2c} (intervals on which a graph is increasing, decreasing, or constant), and
    \textbf{A2.F.2f} (determine $f(x)$ from a graph and interpret $x$ and $f(x)$ in context). Slope
    as a constant rate of change is reactivated Algebra~1 prerequisite knowledge.

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Ideas \& Skills} & \textbf{Key Understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Read the \textbf{domain} and \textbf{range} of a line from its graph.
  \item Locate the \textbf{$y$-intercept} $(0,b)$ and the \textbf{$x$-intercept / zero} (where $f(x)=0$).
  \item Compute \textbf{slope} as a constant rate of change $m=\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$.
  \item State whether a line is \textbf{increasing}, \textbf{decreasing}, or \textbf{constant}.
  \item Give the \textbf{positive / negative} intervals (graph above / below the $x$-axis).
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item A function is \emph{linear} exactly when its rate of change is \emph{constant} --- the same slope everywhere.
  \item Intercepts are where the graph meets an axis; the $x$-intercept is the input that makes the output $0$.
  \item The \emph{sign} of the slope decides increasing vs.\ decreasing as you read left to right.
  \item ``Positive/negative'' describes the \emph{output} $f(x)$, and it switches sign at the zero.
  \item Every non-vertical line has domain \emph{all reals}; the range is all reals unless the line is horizontal.
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Linear function} & A function whose graph is a straight line; it can be written $f(x)=mx+b$ and has a \emph{constant} rate of change. \\
\textbf{Slope / rate of change} & $m=\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$: how much the output changes per unit change in the input. \\
\textbf{$y$-intercept} & The point $(0,b)$ where the graph crosses the $y$-axis --- the output when $x=0$. \\
\textbf{$x$-intercept (zero)} & The point $(a,0)$ where the graph crosses the $x$-axis; the input $a$ with $f(a)=0$. \\
\textbf{Domain / range} & The set of allowed inputs ($x$) / the set of resulting outputs ($y$), read from the graph. \\
\textbf{Increasing / decreasing} & The graph rises ($m>0$) / falls ($m<0$) as you read it left to right. \\
\textbf{Constant function} & A horizontal line $f(x)=b$; slope $0$, neither increasing nor decreasing. \\
\textbf{Positive / negative interval} & The $x$-values where the graph is \emph{above} the $x$-axis ($f(x)>0$) / \emph{below} it ($f(x)<0$). \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{fixedskillbox}[Activate Prior Knowledge \& Spiral Review]{forestbg}
    The Warm-Up rehearses the Unit~1 skills this lesson stands on: \textbf{(1)} plotting and reading
    ordered pairs on the coordinate plane; \textbf{(2)} evaluating a function $f(x)$ at given inputs
    and reading those outputs as points $(x,f(x))$; and \textbf{(3)} recognizing a \emph{constant
    difference} in a table as the fingerprint of a line. Debrief item~3 aloud: a constant change in
    output for equal steps in input is exactly the \emph{slope} we formalize today.
\end{fixedskillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Hook]{forestbg}
    A water tank drains at a steady rate. Its volume after $t$ minutes is $V(t) = -3t + 12$ gallons.
    Show the graph (a falling line). Pose the questions that name every feature we will study:
    \textbf{``How much water did it start with?''} (the $y$-intercept $(0,12)$),
    \textbf{``How fast is it draining?''} (the slope $-3$: down $3$ gallons each minute),
    \textbf{``When is the tank empty?''} (the $x$-intercept / zero $(4,0)$),
    \textbf{``Is the amount of water rising or falling?''} (decreasing), and
    \textbf{``When does the tank still hold water?''} (the positive interval $0 \le t < 4$).
    Land the point: \emph{every question about this situation is a question about a characteristic of
    the graph.}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Lesson]{forestbg}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{1. What makes a function linear.} Build a table for $f(x)=2x-4$ and read down the outputs:
each step of $+1$ in $x$ adds the \emph{same} $+2$ to $y$. That constant change \emph{is} the
\textbf{slope}, and constant slope is exactly what makes a graph a straight \textbf{line}. Name the
form $f(x)=mx+b$.

\textbf{2. Intercepts.} On the graph of $f(x)=2x-4$, mark where it crosses each axis. The
\textbf{$y$-intercept} is $(0,-4)$: set $x=0$. The \textbf{$x$-intercept} is $(2,0)$: set
$f(x)=0$ and solve $2x-4=0$. Stress the language: the $x$-intercept is the function's \textbf{zero}.

\textbf{3. Slope as rate of change.} From any two points, $m=\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$. Count
rise-over-run on the grid: up $2$, right $1$. \textbf{Why does it not matter which two points you
pick?} Because the rate of change is constant.

\columnbreak

\textbf{4. Increasing / decreasing / constant.} Read left to right: $f(x)=2x-4$ \emph{rises}, so it
is \textbf{increasing} on all reals. Contrast $g(x)=-x+3$ (\emph{falls}: decreasing) and
$h(x)=2$ (\emph{flat}: constant, slope $0$). Tie the verdict to the \emph{sign} of $m$.

\textbf{5. Domain, range, and sign.} For any non-vertical line the \textbf{domain} is all reals; the
\textbf{range} is all reals \emph{unless} the line is horizontal (then the range is a single value).
Finally, split the $x$-axis at the zero: $f(x)=2x-4$ is \textbf{positive} for $x>2$ (graph above the
axis) and \textbf{negative} for $x<2$ (below).

\textbf{6. Bring it home.} Return to the tank $V(t)=-3t+12$ and label all six features on it, so
students see the abstract vocabulary describe a real quantity. Preview: Lesson~2.1 uses these
features to \emph{write} and \emph{transform} linear equations.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring]{redbox}
Circulate during the notes practice and Tier work. Watch for and correct:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item swapping the intercepts --- reading the $y$-intercept off the $x$-axis or vice versa;
  \item slope sign and ``upside-down'' rise/run (writing $\tfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta y}$, or calling a
        falling line's slope positive);
  \item confusing ``positive interval'' (where $f(x)>0$) with ``increasing'' (where the graph rises)
        --- a decreasing line can still be positive;
  \item giving the range as ``all reals'' for a \emph{horizontal} line.
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts: ``Where is the zero, and what does $f$ equal there?'' ``Rising or falling --- and
what is the sign of the slope?'' ``Name an $x$ where $f(x)<0$.''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Group Work \& Differentiation]{redbox}
\textbf{Reading Lines} activity (tiered; mirrors the notes).
\begin{multicols}{3}
\textbf{Tier R --- Remediate.} From a pre-drawn line with integer intercepts, read the $y$-intercept
and $x$-intercept, count the slope as rise/run, and say whether the line rises or falls.

\columnbreak
\textbf{Tier A --- Approaching.} Given two graphed lines, report the full feature list for each
(domain, range, intercepts, slope, increasing/decreasing, positive/negative intervals) and
\emph{justify} the sign intervals from the zero.

\columnbreak
\textbf{Tier E --- Extension.} Match an applied context (the draining tank) to its graph, interpret
every feature \emph{in context}, and handle the horizontal-line special case (slope $0$, range a
single value, no zero).
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Individual Work \& Assessment]{redbox}
\textbf{Exit Ticket} (independent, no notes): from one graphed line, read the intercepts and slope;
state increasing/decreasing with the domain/range; and a \textbf{justification check} ---
name the positive interval and explain, using the zero, why the sign switches there. Collect and
sort into ``got it / intercept slip / slope-sign slip / positive-vs-increasing slip'' to decide
whether Lesson~2.1 opens with a quick re-teach.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Reinforcement \& Extension]{goldbox}
\textbf{Homework:} read the full feature list from several graphed lines (including a decreasing line
and a horizontal line); connect a table's constant difference to slope; and one short justification
item on positive/negative intervals. \textbf{Extension:} interpret every feature of the tank model
$V(t)=-3t+12$ in context, including the meaningful domain $0\le t\le 4$. \textbf{Preview:}
Lesson~2.1, \emph{Linear Functions: slope, rate of change, and forms of a line} --- using these
characteristics to write equations and graph transformations.
\end{skillbox}

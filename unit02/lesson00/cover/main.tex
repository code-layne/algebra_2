\begin{remindbox}
\small A \textbf{linear function} has a graph that is a straight line and a \textbf{constant rate of
change} (the same \emph{slope} everywhere). Its whole ``personality'' can be read off that line: the
\textbf{$y$-intercept} $(0,b)$ where it crosses the $y$-axis, the \textbf{$x$-intercept / zero}
$(a,0)$ where $f(a)=0$, whether it is \textbf{increasing} or \textbf{decreasing} (the sign of the
slope), its \textbf{domain} and \textbf{range}, and the intervals where it is \textbf{positive}
(above the $x$-axis) or \textbf{negative} (below). Every one of these is a picture you can point to.
\end{remindbox}

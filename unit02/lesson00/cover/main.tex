\begin{remindbox}
\small A \textbf{quadratic function} $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$ ($a\ne0$) graphs as a \textbf{parabola} --- a
symmetric U-shaped curve. Its whole ``personality'' can be read off that curve: the \textbf{vertex}
(the turning point, a \emph{minimum} if it opens up, a \emph{maximum} if it opens down), the
\textbf{axis of symmetry} through the vertex, the \textbf{$y$-intercept} and the \textbf{zeros} (up to
two $x$-intercepts), its \textbf{domain} and \textbf{range}, where it is \textbf{increasing} or
\textbf{decreasing} (it turns at the vertex), where it is \textbf{positive} or \textbf{negative}, and
its \textbf{end behavior} (both arms rise if $a>0$, both fall if $a<0$). Every one of these is a
picture you can point to.
\end{remindbox}

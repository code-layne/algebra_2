\boxguard[8]
\begin{remindbox}
\small
\textbf{Completing the square} adds $\left(\frac b2\right)^2$ to both sides so that
$x^2+bx+\left(\frac b2\right)^2=\left(x+\frac b2\right)^2$. The \textbf{quadratic formula},
$x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$, solves every $ax^2+bx+c=0$ --- get the equation equal to
zero first, and keep the signs: $(-3)^2=9$. The \textbf{discriminant} $D=b^2-4ac$ predicts the
solutions: $D>0$, two real (two $x$-intercepts); $D=0$, one repeated real (the graph touches
once); $D<0$, \emph{no real} solutions and no $x$-intercepts --- but still \textbf{two complex}
solutions. A negative discriminant never means ``no solution.''
\end{remindbox}

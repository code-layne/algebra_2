\boxguard[8]
\begin{remindbox}
\small
A \textbf{complex number} in \textbf{standard form} is $a+bi$, with real part $a$ and imaginary
part $b$. To \textbf{add or subtract}, combine real with real and imaginary with imaginary --- a
minus sign reaches \emph{both} parts. To \textbf{multiply}, distribute every term, then replace
$i^2$ with $-1$: an $i^2$ term is real and flips sign, so $-3i^2=+3$. \textbf{Complex
conjugates} $a+bi$ and $a-bi$ multiply to the real number $a^2+b^2$ --- a sum, never
$a^2-b^2$. With a negative under a root, pull the $i$ out \emph{before} you multiply:
$\sqrt{-4}\cdot\sqrt{-25}=2i\cdot5i=-10$.
\end{remindbox}

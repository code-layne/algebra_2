\begin{vocabbox}
\small
Fill in each term as we build it together.
\par\vspace{2pt}
\vocabans{Imaginary unit $i$}{the number with $i=\sqrt{-1}$, so $i^2=-1$}
\vocabans{Complex number ($a+bi$)}{a number with a real part $a$ and an imaginary part $b$ ($a,b$ real)}

\begin{hookbox}
Lesson 2.3 kept hitting a wall: $x^2=-4$ has \textbf{no real} solution, because no real number squares
to a negative. On a graph, $y=x^2+4$ (below) \textbf{never crosses the $x$-axis} --- no real zeros.
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.40]
  \lgrid{-3}{3}{-1}{7}
  \begin{scope}\clip (-3,-1) rectangle (3,7);
    \draw[very thick, forest, domain=-1.7:1.7, samples=70, smooth] plot (\x,{\x*\x+4});
  \end{scope}
  \fill[charcoal] (0,4) circle (3pt) node[right, font=\scriptsize]{$(0,4)$};
\end{tikzpicture}

\vspace{-4pt}
Instead of quitting, mathematicians \emph{invented} a number whose square \emph{is} negative. Today we
meet it and learn to compute with it.
\end{hookbox}

\begin{notesbox}{1. The imaginary unit $i$ --- break the wall}
Define a brand-new number, the \textbf{imaginary unit}:
\[ i=\sqrt{-1}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad i^2=\ans{$-1$}. \]
That single rule, $i^2=-1$, is the whole engine. Now negative square roots have meaning:
\[ \sqrt{-4}=\sqrt{4}\cdot\sqrt{-1}=\ans{$2i$}, \qquad \sqrt{-25}=\ans{$5i$}. \]
And the wall from 2.3 finally opens:
\[ x^2=-4 \ \Rightarrow\ x=\pm\sqrt{-4}=\ans{$\pm2i$}. \]
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{2. Negative radicals and $a+bi$ form}
\textbf{Factor out the $i$ first, then simplify the radical.} For $k>0$, \ $\sqrt{-k}=i\sqrt{k}$.
\[
  \sqrt{-9}=\ans{$3i$} \qquad \sqrt{-50}=\ans{$5i\sqrt{2}$} \qquad \sqrt{-12}=\ans{$2i\sqrt{3}$}
\]
\emph{Caution:} pull the $i$ out \textbf{before} multiplying radicals --- $\sqrt{-2}\cdot\sqrt{-2}=i\sqrt2\cdot i\sqrt2=2i^2=-2$, \emph{not} $\sqrt{4}=2$.

\smallskip
Every complex number can be written in \textbf{standard form} $a+bi$:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l l X@{}}
$a$ is the \textbf{real part}, & $b$ is the \textbf{imaginary part}. & If $b=0$ it's \emph{real}; if $a=0$ (and $b\ne0$) it's \emph{pure imaginary}. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
Write each in $a+bi$ form and name the parts:
\[ 7-\sqrt{-16}=\ans{$7-4i$}, \quad a=\ans{$7$},\ b=\ans{$-4$}; \qquad \sqrt{-9}=\ans{$3i$},\ a=\ans{$0$},\ b=\ans{$3$}. \]

\textbf{Read the complex plane.} Real part $\to$ horizontal, imaginary part $\to$ vertical. Name the plotted point:
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.42]
  \draw[step=1, linegray!40, very thin] (-1,-1) grid (4,3);
  \draw[->, semithick, charcoal] (-1,0)--(4,0) node[below right, font=\scriptsize]{Re};
  \draw[->, semithick, charcoal] (0,-1)--(0,3) node[left, font=\scriptsize]{Im};
  \fill[forest] (3,2) circle (3.5pt);
  \draw[forest, dashed] (3,0)--(3,2)--(0,2);
  \node[charcoal, font=\scriptsize, below] at (3,0){$3$};
  \node[charcoal, font=\scriptsize, left] at (0,2){$2$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\vspace{-4pt}
The point above is \ans{$3+2i$} (real part \ans{$3$}, imaginary part \ans{$2$}).
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{3. Add, subtract, and multiply}
\textbf{Add / subtract:} combine like terms --- reals with reals, $i$-terms with $i$-terms.
\[ (3+2i)+(5-8i)=\ans{$8-6i$}, \qquad (4-i)-(6-3i)=\ans{$-2+2i$}. \]
\textbf{Multiply:} distribute (FOIL), then replace $i^2=-1$ and re-collect into $a+bi$.
\[ (2+3i)(4-i)=8-2i+12i-3i^2=8+10i-3(\ans{$-1$})=\ans{$11+10i$}. \]
\textbf{Complex conjugate.} The conjugate of $a+bi$ is $a-bi$. Their product is \emph{always real}:
\[ (a+bi)(a-bi)=a^2-b^2i^2=\ans{$a^2+b^2$}. \qquad (5+2i)(5-2i)=\ans{$29$}. \]
\end{notesbox}

\boxguard[20]
\begin{notesbox}{4. Powers of $i$, and where complex numbers come from}
Powers of $i$ \textbf{cycle} with period $4$. Fill the cycle, then reduce by taking the exponent mod $4$:
\[ i^1=i,\quad i^2=\ans{$-1$},\quad i^3=i^2\cdot i=\ans{$-i$},\quad i^4=\ans{$1$},\quad i^5=\ans{$i$}. \]
\[ i^{23}=\ans{$-i$} \qquad i^{40}=\ans{$1$} \]
\smallskip
\textbf{Why we needed them.} Complete the square on the 2.3 leftover $x^2+2x+5=0$:
\[ (x+1)^2=-4 \ \Rightarrow\ x+1=\pm\ans{$2i$} \ \Rightarrow\ x=\ans{$-1\pm2i$}. \]
The two solutions $-1\pm2i$ are \textbf{complex conjugates} --- a preview of Lesson 2.5.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{practicebox}
\textbf{Simplify. Write every answer in $a+bi$ form.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=6pt]
  \item $\sqrt{-45}$
    \begin{work}
      \sqrt{-45} &= i\sqrt{45} \\
                 &= 3i\sqrt{5}
    \end{work}
  \item $(6-i)+(-2+7i)$
    \begin{work}
      (6-i)+(-2+7i) &= (6-2)+(-1+7)i \\
                    &= 4+6i
    \end{work}
  \item $(3+i)(2-5i)$
    \begin{work}
      (3+i)(2-5i) &= 6-15i+2i-5i^2 \\
                  &= 6-13i-5(-1) \\
                  &= 11-13i
    \end{work}
  \item $i^{31}$
    \begin{work}
      31 &= 4(7)+3 \\
      i^{31} &= i^3 = -i
    \end{work}
\end{enumerate}
\end{practicebox}

    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students add, subtract, and multiply complex numbers and write
    every result in standard form $a+bi$ --- combining real parts and imaginary parts, distributing
    a product and replacing $i^2$ with $-1$, and multiplying conjugates and negative radicals to
    a real number with the correct sign. One skill, three cases of rising difficulty.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} \textbf{A2.EO.4c} (apply properties to add, subtract, and
    multiply complex numbers), revisiting \textbf{A2.EO.4b} (equivalent radical expressions with
    negative radicands and expressions in $a+bi$ form) from 2.3.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Lesson model:} \emph{Slides first, one skill.} The deck is the lesson and its printed
    handout is the student's notes, and students are \emph{solving the whole hour}: a warm-up on
    screen as they walk in; three rounds of a short \textbf{definition} and \textbf{example}
    ($\le$3 min), a \textbf{ladder} of three problems (\rung1\ \rung2\ \rung3), and a
    \textbf{Show me} or \textbf{Find the mistake}; a self-scored \textbf{Final Round}; and the
    \textbf{homework} started in class. \textbf{Homework source:} DeltaMath, online --- set
    \textbf{A2: Lesson 2.4: Operations with Complex Numbers} (not in the packet).
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Ideas \& Skills} & \textbf{Key Understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Add and subtract complex numbers by combining real parts and imaginary parts; distribute
        a subtraction to \emph{both} parts.
  \item Rewrite a negative radical with $i$ before combining: $\sqrt{-16}=4i$.
  \item Multiply by distributing every term, then $i^2=-1$; answer in $a+bi$, real part first.
  \item Multiply complex conjugates to $a^2+b^2$; multiply negative radicals with $i$ pulled out
        first.
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Adding complex numbers is combining like terms --- $i$ behaves like a variable.
  \item Multiplying is binomial multiplication plus one fact: $i^2=-1$, so an $i^2$ term is a
        real number and changes sign.
  \item A conjugate product is real because the middle terms cancel and $-b^2i^2=+b^2$.
  \item \textbf{Target misconception: treating $i^2$ as $+1$ (or dropping its sign).} Students
        write $(6+3i)(6-3i)=36-9=27$ (it is $45$), $-3i^2=-3$, or
        $\sqrt{-4}\cdot\sqrt{-25}=\sqrt{100}=10$ (it is $-10$).
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems --- one definition frame each}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Complex number} & $a+bi$ (\textbf{standard form}), $a$ and $b$ real: $a$ is the
                          \textbf{real part}, $b$ the \textbf{imaginary part}. (From 2.3.) \\
\textbf{Add / subtract} & Combine the real parts and the imaginary parts:
                          $(a+bi)+(c+di)=(a+c)+(b+d)i$; a minus reaches both parts. \\
\textbf{Multiply} & Distribute every term, replace every $i^2$ with $-1$, combine into $a+bi$. \\
\textbf{Complex conjugates} & $a+bi$ and $a-bi$; their product $a^2+b^2$ is always real. \\
\textbf{Negative radicals} & $\sqrt a\cdot\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}$ only for $a,b\ge0$; otherwise
                             $\sqrt{-a}\cdot\sqrt{-b}=i\sqrt a\cdot i\sqrt b=-\sqrt{ab}$. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Engagement --- how this hour keeps everyone solving]{goldbox}
\textbf{Nobody waits:} every practice frame is a ladder --- \rung1\ everyone (a quick win),
\rung2\ everyone, \rung3\ early finishers. \textbf{One skill, climbing:} the three rounds are the
same arithmetic getting harder, so a student who wins Round 1 starts Round 2 already confident.
\textbf{Everyone commits, safely:} \emph{Show me} is 1--4 fingers on the count of three, all at
once (Rounds 1 and 2). \textbf{Critique a stranger:} Round 3's \emph{Find the mistake} puts the
crux error on an anonymous Student A. \textbf{Their world:} the dorm escape-room lock code
(Round 1 \rung3) and the school-store puzzle bonus. \textbf{Short clock:} every practice frame
carries its minutes. Praise the climb more than the answer.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Warm-Up (5 min, on the slides as they walk in)]{forestbg}
Three quick wins; the answers are one click. \textbf{(1)} $\sqrt{-36}=6i$ --- last class, and the
move Round~1(c) needs. \textbf{(2)} $(3x+5)-(x-4)=2x+9$ --- the minus hits the $-4$; that is
Round~1's subtraction exactly. \textbf{(3)} $(x+2)(x-5)=x^2-3x-10$ --- the distributing Round~2
needs. The reveal ends on \emph{Hold on to this}: what if the $x$ were an $i$ --- what happens to
the $x^2$? Leave it open; Round~2 answers it ($i^2=-1$). Then the targets frame (30 seconds).
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[The Lesson --- three rounds of definition, example, ladder, check (40 min)]{forestbg}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{Round 1 --- add and subtract (13 min).} Definition and Example 1 in 3 minutes: point at
the side-by-side --- $(5+3x)+(2-7x)$ and $(5+3i)+(2-7i)$ are the same move. Example:
$(7+2i)+(-3+5i)=4+7i$; $(6-4i)-(2-9i)=4+5i$ (stop on $-(-9i)$); $(3+\sqrt{-16})+(1-\sqrt{-4})
=4+2i$ ($i$ out first). Ladder (7 min): \rung1\ $7+4i$; \rung2\ $5+5i$ (watch $5-11i$: the minus
reached only the $4$); \rung3\ escape room, $(12+7i)-(5-3i)=7+10i$, code $7$--$10$. Show me
$(5-2i)-(3-6i)$: answer \textbf{2}, $2+4i$; \textbf{1} = minus didn't reach $-6i$; \textbf{3} =
added; \textbf{4} = order flipped.

\textbf{Round 2 --- multiply (13 min).} Definition with the box for $(2+3i)(4-i)$: four
products, then $-3i^2=+3$. This answers the warm-up's question --- an $x^2$ stays, an $i^2$
turns into $-1$. Example 2: $3i(4-5i)=15+12i$; $(2+3i)(4-i)=11+10i$ --- pause on the $-3i^2$
line and ask what it becomes; $(1-3i)^2=-8-6i$, written out twice. Ladder (7 min): \rung1\
$-2+10i$ (watch $2+10i$); \rung2\ $10-10i$ (watch $2-10i$: $i^2$ taken as $+1$); \rung3\
$-21+20i$ (watch $4+25i^2$: the lost middle term). Show me $(4+i)(2-3i)$: answer \textbf{2},
$11-10i$; \textbf{1} = $i^2=+1$; \textbf{3} = lost the middle terms; \textbf{4} = unfinished.

\columnbreak
\textbf{Round 3 --- conjugates and negative radicals: the crux (14 min).} Definition and
Example 3 in 3 minutes: write $(a+bi)(a-bi)$ out and watch the middle cancel and $-b^2i^2$ turn
to $+b^2$. Example: $(5+2i)(5-2i)=29$; the conjugate of $-3+7i$ and the product $58$;
$\sqrt{-4}\cdot\sqrt{-25}=2i\cdot5i=-10$, not $\sqrt{100}$. Ladder (8 min): \rung1\ $10$ (watch
$8$); \rung2\ $6+5i$, $61$ (watch $11$); \rung3\ $\sqrt{-3}\cdot\sqrt{-12}=-6$ and
$(2+3i)(2-3i)=13$. \textbf{Find the mistake --- the frame not to rush:} Student A wrote
$(6+3i)(6-3i)=36-18i+18i-9i^2=36-9=27$. Give 3 minutes; ask ``what would you tell Student A?''
before revealing. The fix: $-9i^2=+9$, so $45$; conjugates give $a^2+b^2$, a sum. This is the
target misconception.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Final Round (5 min)]{forestbg}
Four problems on the one skill, alone, 5-minute timer: $(8-3i)-(2+5i)=6-8i$;
$4i(3-2i)=8+12i$; $(2-i)(5+3i)=13+i$; $(7-2i)(7+2i)=53$ (the crux's twin --- $45$ means
$49-4$, the $i^2$ sign again). \textbf{Bonus:} the school-store puzzle,
$\sqrt{-8}\cdot\sqrt{-2}+(3+i)(3-i)=-4+10=6$. Students score themselves out of 4; ask for
scores on fingers --- that is your exit read. Say the \emph{Watch out} aloud as you reveal:
$i^2=-1$, so an $i^2$ term flips sign.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard

\textbf{Preview:} 2.5 --- solving a quadratic by completing the square.
\end{skillbox}

\boxguard
\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring --- Watch For]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{Round 1 ladder and Show me:} the minus reaching only the real part of the
        second number; answers written $bi+a$ or with $\sqrt{-k}$ left in.
  \item \textbf{Round 2 \rung1--\rung2:} stopping at $\ldots-4i^2$, or $i^2$ taken as $+1$.
  \item \textbf{Round 2 \rung3:} $(2+5i)^2=4+25i^2$ (lost middle term).
  \item \textbf{Round 3 Find the mistake / Final Round 4:} conjugates as $a^2-b^2$;
        $\sqrt{-a}\cdot\sqrt{-b}=+\sqrt{ab}$ (\emph{the target misconception}).
  \item \textbf{Attitude:} a student parked at \rung1\ with nothing written --- crouch, point at
        the example on the handout, ask for just the first step.
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts (after the fingers, never before): ``Where did you use $i^2=-1$?'' \;
``Why is a conjugate product always real?''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Slides]
Pacing: warm-up 5, rounds 13 / 13 / 14, Final Round 5, homework 10 (60). In each round the
definition and example together take \textbf{at most 3 minutes} --- if you catch yourself
explaining past that, move to the ladder and explain at desks. Reveal a ladder when most of the
room has \rung2; the \rung3\ students keep going. If behind, shorten Round 1's Show me, never
Round 3's Find the mistake. Students write in the handout's notes column, which prints without
the answers.
\end{teachernote}

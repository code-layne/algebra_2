\begin{remindbox}
\small One formula solves \emph{every} quadratic: write it in \textbf{standard form} $ax^2+bx+c=0$,
identify $a,b,c$ (watch the signs), then
$x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$. Compute the \textbf{discriminant} $b^2-4ac$ \emph{first} --- it
predicts the answer: $>0$ gives \textbf{two real} roots (two $x$-intercepts), $=0$ gives \textbf{one
repeated} real root (vertex on the $x$-axis), $<0$ gives \textbf{two complex-conjugate} roots (no
$x$-intercepts). When the radicand is negative, use $i$ (Lesson~2.4) and write answers as $a+bi$. Always
simplify radicals and reduce the fraction.
\end{remindbox}

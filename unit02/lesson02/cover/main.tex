\boxguard[8]
\begin{remindbox}
\small
To \textbf{factor} is to write a polynomial as a product --- take out the \textbf{GCF} first. For
$x^2+bx+c$, find two integers with product $c$ and sum $b$; its special cases are the
\textbf{difference of squares} $x^2-k^2=(x+k)(x-k)$ and the \textbf{perfect square}
$x^2+2kx+k^2=(x+k)^2$, and a sum of squares does not factor. For $ax^2+bx+c$, find two with
product $a\cdot c$ and sum $b$, split the middle term, and \textbf{group}. The \textbf{Zero
Product Property} says that if $AB=0$ then $A=0$ or $B=0$ --- so get everything on one side
\emph{equal to zero} before you factor, and never divide both sides by $x$ (that loses the root
$x=0$). The \textbf{roots} are the graph's $x$-intercepts.
\end{remindbox}

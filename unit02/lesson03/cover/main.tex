\begin{remindbox}
\small When a quadratic \emph{won't factor}, you can still solve it. If you can write it as ``(a square)
$=$ (a number),'' use the \textbf{Square Root Property}: $x^2=k \Rightarrow x=\pm\sqrt{k}$ --- keep the
\textbf{$\pm$} (a number has two square roots) and simplify the radical. If a $bx$ term is in the way,
\textbf{complete the square}: move the constant, add $\left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2$ to \emph{both} sides to
build a perfect square $\left(x+\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2$, then take square roots. That same move rewrites a
quadratic in \textbf{vertex form} and hands you the vertex. Golden rule: \emph{isolate the square before
you undo it}, and never drop the $\pm$.
\end{remindbox}

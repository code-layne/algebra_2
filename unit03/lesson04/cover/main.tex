\begin{remindbox}
\small Every square-root method stalls at a \emph{negative} radicand ($x^2=-4$, $\sqrt{-9}$). Define the
\textbf{imaginary unit} $i=\sqrt{-1}$, so $i^2=-1$; then $\sqrt{-k}=i\sqrt{k}$ (pull the $i$ out
\emph{first}, then simplify). Write answers in \textbf{standard form} $a+bi$: $a$ is the real part, $b$ the
imaginary part. \textbf{Add/subtract} by combining like terms (reals with reals, $i$'s with $i$'s);
\textbf{multiply} by FOIL, then replace $i^2=-1$. The \textbf{conjugate} $a-bi$ times $a+bi$ is the real
number $a^2+b^2$. \textbf{Powers of $i$} cycle $i,\,-1,\,-i,\,1$: reduce the exponent mod $4$.
\end{remindbox}

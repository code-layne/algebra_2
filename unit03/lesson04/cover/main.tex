\boxguard[8]
\begin{remindbox}
\small
The \textbf{leading term} is the term with the \emph{highest power}, not the term written first:
its exponent is the \textbf{degree} and its coefficient is the \textbf{leading coefficient}
($2x^2-x^5$ has degree $5$ and leading coefficient $-1$). In factored form, add the degrees and
multiply the leading coefficients. \textbf{End behavior} comes from the leading term alone: an
even degree sends both ends the same way, an odd degree sends them opposite ways, and a positive
leading coefficient makes the right end rise. A function is \textbf{even} when $f(-x)=f(x)$
(mirror in the $y$-axis) and \textbf{odd} when $f(-x)=-f(x)$ (half-turn about the origin) ---
and an odd \emph{degree} does not make an odd \emph{function}: $x^3+1$ is neither.
\end{remindbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=forestbg, colframe=forest, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students find a polynomial's \emph{degree} and \emph{leading
    coefficient} from standard or factored form, use them to state its \emph{end behavior} in
    arrow notation (and read end behavior off a pre-drawn graph), and classify a function as
    \emph{even}, \emph{odd}, or \emph{neither} from its graph and with the $f(-x)$ test --- always
    reading the \emph{leading term}, never the first term written.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} \textbf{A2.F.2} --- characteristics of polynomial
    functions: \textbf{A2.F.2g} (end behavior, from the degree and the sign of the leading
    coefficient) and \textbf{A2.F.2b} (compare and contrast odd- and even-degree behavior);
    symmetry (even/odd) is taught as part of the A2.F.2 characteristics toolkit, as in the old
    Lesson~3.0. Zeros, multiplicity, turning points, extrema, and intervals
    (\textbf{A2.F.2a, c, d, e}) are Lesson~3.5.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Lesson model:} \emph{Packet first.} A warm-up on paper as they walk in; three rounds
    in the Guided Notes, each a short \textbf{definition} and \textbf{example} ($\le$3 min), a
    \textbf{ladder} (\rung1\ \rung2\ \rung3), and a \textbf{Show me} or \textbf{Find the
    mistake}; the self-scored \textbf{Closing Practice}; the homework started in class. The deck
    mirrors the notes frame for frame. \textbf{Homework source:} DeltaMath, online --- set
    \textbf{A2: Lesson 3.4: End Behavior \& Symmetry} (not in the packet). Optional graded
    \textbf{Extra Credit} (\texttt{alg2-u03-l04-ec}) at the back of the packet.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Ideas \& Skills} & \textbf{Key Understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Rewrite in standard form and read the \textbf{leading term}: its exponent is the
        \textbf{degree}, its coefficient the \textbf{leading coefficient}.
  \item In factored form, add the factors' degrees and multiply their leading coefficients.
  \item State \textbf{end behavior} in arrow notation from the parity/sign rule; read it off a
        graph and infer the parity and sign.
  \item Classify \textbf{even} / \textbf{odd} / \textbf{neither} from a graph and by computing
        $f(-x)$.
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Far from the origin the highest power swamps every other term, so the leading term
        alone decides the ends.
  \item Two questions fix both ends: even or odd degree? positive or negative leading
        coefficient?
  \item $(-x)^n$ keeps the sign for odd $n$ and loses it for even $n$ --- the reason even powers
        give $y$-axis symmetry and odd powers give origin symmetry.
  \item \textbf{Target misconception: reading the degree and leading coefficient (and so the end
        behavior) from the first-written term or factor instead of the leading term.}
        $2x^2-x^5$ has degree $5$ and leading coefficient $-1$, not $2$;
        $-(x-1)(x+2)^2$ leads with $-x^3$. A second, related slip: \emph{an odd degree is not
        an odd function} --- $x^3+1$ is neither.
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems --- one definition frame each}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Standard form} & Terms listed from the highest power of $x$ down to the constant. \\
\textbf{Leading term / degree / leading coefficient} & The term with the highest power; its
      exponent is the degree and its coefficient is the leading coefficient. In factored form:
      degree = sum of the factors' degrees; leading coefficient = product of their leading
      coefficients. \\
\textbf{End behavior} & What $f(x)$ does as $x\to\infty$ and $x\to-\infty$. Even degree: ends
      the same way; odd: opposite ways. Positive leading coefficient: right end rises
      ($f(x)\to\infty$); negative: it falls. \\
\textbf{Even / odd / neither} & Even: $f(-x)=f(x)$, symmetric about the $y$-axis (all even
      powers). Odd: $f(-x)=-f(x)$, symmetric about the origin (all odd powers). A mix: neither. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Warm-Up (5 min, on paper as they walk in)]{forestbg}
Three quick wins; the deck reveals the answers in one click. \textbf{(1)} Synthetic division
(spiral 3.3): $x^3-2x^2-5x+6$ by $x-1$ gives $x^2-x-6$, remainder $0$. \textbf{(2)} Powers of a
negative: $(-x)^2=x^2$, $(-x)^3=-x^3$, $(-x)^4=x^4$, $(-x)^5=-x^5$; for $f(x)=x^2+3$,
$f(-x)=x^2+3$ --- yes, the same: the seed of Round 2's parity rule and Round 3's test.
\textbf{(3)} Standard form: $g(x)=-3x^4-x^2+5x+2$; highest power $4$, coefficient $-3$. The
reveal ends on \emph{Hold on to this}: $5x$ was written first --- which term controls the ends?
(Answer $-3x^4$; Round 1 names it the leading term.)
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[The Lesson --- three rounds and Closing Practice (45 min)]{forestbg}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{Round 1 --- degree \& leading coefficient (13 min).} Definition and Example 1 in 3
minutes; point at the underbrace on $-7x^5$. Example: $4x^2-7x^5+x-9$ has degree $5$, l.c.\ $-7$;
$-2(x-3)(x+1)^2$ has degree $1+2=3$, l.c.\ $-2$. Ladder (7 min): \rung1\ $3x^4-2x^6+5$: degree
$6$, l.c.\ $-2$ (watch ``$4$, $3$''); \rung2\ $(2x-1)(x+4)^3$: degree $4$, l.c.\ $2$ (watch
degree $3$ or $2$ --- the cube); \rung3\ egg-drop box $x(20-2x)(12-2x)$: degree $3$, l.c.\ $4$
(watch $240$: multiplying the constants). Show me $-x(3x+2)(x-5)^2$: answer \textbf{2} (degree
$4$, l.c.\ $-3$); \textbf{1} = counted factors; \textbf{3} = lost the minus; \textbf{4} =
biggest exponent and constants multiplied.

\textbf{Round 2 --- end behavior (13 min).} Point at the four thumbnails: \emph{same way} or
\emph{opposite}, then which way the \emph{right} end goes. Example 2: read $f$ (both ends fall
$\Rightarrow$ even, negative); predict $5x^2-2x^3+1$: leading term $-2x^3$, as $x\to-\infty$,
$g(x)\to\infty$; as $x\to\infty$, $g(x)\to-\infty$ --- stop here and say ``highest power, not
first written.'' Ladder (7 min): \rung1\ $x^5-4x^2+7$: $-\infty$ left, $\infty$ right;
\rung2\ graph of $p$: $\infty$ left, $-\infty$ right, odd and negative; \rung3\
$(x+2)(3-x)(x-1)^2$ leads with $-x^4$: both ends $\to-\infty$ (watch ``both up'' --- the
$(3-x)$ minus). Show me $-3x^5+x^2$: answer \textbf{4} (up-left, down-right); \textbf{1}/\textbf{2}
= treated as even; \textbf{3} = ignored the sign.

\columnbreak
\textbf{Round 3 --- symmetry: the crux (13 min).} Definition with the three graphs: mirror in
the $y$-axis, half-turn about the origin, neither. Example 3: $2x^4-x^2+3$ is even;
$x^3-4x$ is odd (every term flips). Ladder (7 min): \rung1\ A odd, B even; \rung2\
$x^5-2x^3+x$ odd; \rung3\ $x^4+x^3$: $k(-x)=x^4-x^3$, neither (watch ``odd because $x^3$'').
\textbf{Find the mistake --- the frame not to rush:} Student A on $f(x)=2x^2-x^5$: step~1
(neither) is right; \textbf{step~2 is wrong} --- the leading term is $-x^5$, so degree $5$,
l.c.\ $-1$, and the ends go up-left, down-right. Give 3 minutes; ask ``what would you tell
Student A?'' before revealing. This is the target misconception.

\textbf{Closing Practice (6 min), alone, score out of 4.} 1.\ $(3x+1)^2(2-x)$: degree $3$, l.c.\
$-9$. 2.\ $6x^3-2x^4+x$: both ends $\to-\infty$ (\emph{the crux's twin} --- ``down-left,
up-right'' means they read $6x^3$). 3.\ $x^3+2$: $r(-x)=-x^3+2$, neither (the odd-degree slip).
4.\ graph of $s$: both ends $\to\infty$, even. \textbf{Bonus:} formal tickets
$P(x)=40x^2-x^3$: as $x\to\infty$, $P(x)\to-\infty$ --- a very high price loses money. Say the
\emph{Watch out} aloud: the leading term is the highest power, not the first written --- and an
odd degree does not make an odd function.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Extra Credit --- optional, graded (\texttt{alg2-u03-l04-ec}), 16 points]{goldbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{q1 (combine R1 + R2, 4 pts):} $f(x)=-x(2x-1)^2(x+3)$: degree $4$, l.c.\ $-4$,
        left end down, right end down --- stretches the factored-form rule to a squared factor
        whose leading coefficient is $2^2$.
  \item \textbf{q2 (reverse, 4 pts):} (a) $(kx+1)(x-2)^2(3-x)$ with l.c.\ $12$: $-k=12$,
        $k=-12$; (b) $x^4+(c-3)x^3+5$ even: $c=3$ --- runs Rounds 1 and 3 backwards.
  \item \textbf{q3 (model, 4 pts):} garden club $T(x)=30x^2-2x^3$: degree $3$, l.c.\ $-2$;
        (c, rubric 2) as $x\to\infty$, $T(x)\to-\infty$ --- negative tomatoes, impossible;
        $T(15)=0$, so the model stops there.
  \item \textbf{q4 (Find the mistake, rubric 3):} ``$x^3+1$ is odd because its degree is odd''
        --- step~2; $g(-x)=-x^3+1$ is neither $g(x)$ nor $-g(x)$, so neither.
  \item \textbf{q5 (SOL-style MC, 1 pt):} both ends down: \textbf{B} $x^2-3x^4$; \textbf{C}
        $-2x+x^4$ is the crux distractor (reads the first term).
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring --- Watch For]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{The crux --- Round 2 Example (b), Round 3 Find the mistake, Closing Practice 2,
        EC q5:} degree and leading coefficient read from the \emph{first-written} term or factor
        ($2x^2-x^5$ called degree $2$; $-(x-1)(x+2)^2$ read off the $-(x-1)$ alone).
  \item \textbf{Odd degree $\ne$ odd function --- Round 3 \rung3, Closing Practice 3, EC q4:}
        $x^3+1$ or $x^4+x^3$ called odd because of the $x^3$.
  \item \textbf{Round 1 \rung2--\rung3:} the cube's degree counted as $1$; $(20-2x)$ read with
        leading coefficient $20$.
  \item \textbf{Round 2 \rung3:} the minus in $(3-x)$ lost, so ``both up.''
  \item \textbf{Attitude:} a student parked at \rung1\ with nothing written --- crouch, point at
        the example in her notes, ask for just the first step.
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts (after the fingers, never before): ``Which term is the leading term, and how
do you know?'' \; ``Even or odd degree --- and what does that tell you about the two ends?'' \;
``Is that odd \emph{degree} or odd \emph{function}?''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes \& Practice]
Pacing: rounds 13 / 13 / 13, Closing Practice 6 (45). Definition and example together take
\textbf{at most 3 minutes} a round --- students fill the example's work space as you work it on
the deck. Reveal a ladder when most have \rung2. If behind, shorten Round 1's Show me, never
Round 3's Find the mistake. All graphs are pre-drawn: students read and label, never sketch.
Zeros, turning points and intervals are 3.5 --- if a student asks, park it there.
\end{teachernote}

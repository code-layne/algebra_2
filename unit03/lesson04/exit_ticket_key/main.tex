\vspace{0.10in}

No notes. Constant term on top, leading coefficient on the bottom --- and do not lose the $\pm$.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=11pt]
  \item \textbf{The whole workflow.} Solve $3x^3 + 2x^2 - 7x + 2 = 0$.

    \vspace{3pt}
    $p$ divides \ans{$2$} \quad $q$ divides \ans{$3$} \quad
    Candidates: \ans{$\pm 1,\ \pm 2,\ \pm\tfrac13,\ \pm\tfrac23$}

    \vspace{3pt}
    A candidate that works: $r = $ \ans{$1$}
    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
    \begin{tabular}{r|C{1.5cm}C{1.5cm}C{1.5cm}C{1.5cm}}
    \ans{$1$} & \ans{$3$} & \ans{$2$} & \ans{$-7$} & \ans{$2$} \\
        &     & \ans{$3$} & \ans{$5$} & \ans{$-2$} \\ \cline{2-5}
        & \ans{$3$} & \ans{$5$} & \ans{$-2$} & \ans{$0$} \\
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}

    \vspace{2pt}
    Depressed polynomial: \ans{$3x^2 + 5x - 2$} \quad Completely factored: \ans{$(x-1)(3x-1)(x+2)$}

    \vspace{3pt}
    Solutions: \ans{$x = -2,\ \tfrac13,\ 1$} \quad Verify one of them by substituting:
    \ans{$3(\tfrac13)^3+2(\tfrac13)^2-7(\tfrac13)+2 = \tfrac19+\tfrac29-\tfrac73+2 = 0$}

  \item \textbf{Error analysis.} For $f(x) = 2x^3 + 5x^2 - x - 6$, a classmate writes the candidate
    list $\pm 1,\ \pm 2,\ \pm\tfrac12,\ \pm\tfrac13,\ \pm\tfrac16$.

    \vspace{3pt}
    What did they do wrong? \ans{They flipped the fraction --- they used divisors of the leading
    coefficient $2$ on top and divisors of the constant $6$ on the bottom. It is
    $\frac{\text{divisors of the constant}}{\text{divisors of the leading coefficient}}$.}

    \vspace{3pt}
    Write the correct list: \ans{$\pm 1,\ \pm 2,\ \pm 3,\ \pm 6,\ \pm\tfrac12,\ \pm\tfrac32$}

  \item \textbf{SOL-style multiple choice.} Which value is \emph{not} a possible rational zero of
    $f(x) = 4x^3 - x^2 + 6x - 3$?
    \begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*., leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=1pt]
      \item $-3$
      \item $\dfrac{3}{4}$
      \item $\dfrac{2}{3}$ \quad \textcolor{keyred}{\textbf{$\leftarrow$ correct}}
      \item $\dfrac{1}{2}$
    \end{enumerate}
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, nosep]
      \item The list is $\pm\frac{p}{q}$ with $p \mid 3$ and $q \mid 4$: \; $\pm 1, \pm 3, \pm\frac12, \pm\frac32, \pm\frac14, \pm\frac34$.
      \item \textbf{C} fails twice: $2$ does not divide the constant $3$, and $3$ does not divide the leading coefficient $4$.
      \item \textbf{A}, \textbf{B}, and \textbf{D} are all on the list. (Being on the list does not make them zeros --- only \emph{possible} zeros.)
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teachernote}
Item~1 separates four different failures. A list built from divisors of $3$ over divisors of $2$ is
the flipped fraction (same error as item~2, so a student who misses both has one problem, not two).
A list with no negatives loses half the candidates --- survivable here, since $r=1$ works, but fatal
on the homework. A correct division that stops at $3x^2+5x-2$ is the ``forgot to finish'' pile that
has now followed us from Lesson~3.2 through 3.3. And zeros listed as $1$, $-\frac13$, $2$ is a sign
error reading zeros off the factors $(3x-1)$ and $(x+2)$ --- the non-monic factor is the new hazard
this lesson introduces, since $3x-1=0$ gives $x=\frac13$, not $x=3$ or $x=-\frac13$.

\vspace{3pt}
Item~3 checks the theorem as a \emph{filter} rather than a procedure --- students must read a
candidate backward and ask whether its numerator and denominator qualify. Watch for students who try
to evaluate $f$ at all four choices; that works but burns four minutes, and it means they are
treating the list as arithmetic instead of structure.

\vspace{3pt}
Sort into four piles --- \emph{got it} / \emph{list built wrong (flipped or no $\pm$)} /
\emph{division or arithmetic} / \emph{stopped before solving}. A large ``list built wrong'' pile is
the one to fix at the top of Lesson~3.5, because 3.5 assumes students can produce a candidate list
without help. If the sign-reading error on $(3x-1)$ shows up widely, spend two minutes on ``set each
factor equal to zero and \emph{solve},'' which is the habit that transfers to every remaining lesson
in the unit.
\end{teachernote}

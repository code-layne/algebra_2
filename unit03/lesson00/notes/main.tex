A polynomial's whole shape --- its arms, its hills and valleys, and how it meets the $x$-axis --- is

\begin{notesbox}{1. What Makes a Function a Polynomial}
A \textbf{polynomial function} is a sum of terms $a_kx^k$ whose exponents are all \emph{whole
numbers}:
\[
  f(x) = a_nx^n + a_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1x + a_0 .
\]
Its graph is always \textbf{smooth} (no sharp corners) and \textbf{continuous} (no breaks or holes).
Written in \emph{standard form} (powers high to low):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item the \textbf{degree} is the \blank{3.2cm} power, and
  \item the \textbf{leading coefficient} is the number multiplying that \blank{3.6cm} term.
\end{itemize}
For $g(x)=x^3-3x+2$: degree $=\blank{0.8cm}$, leading coefficient $=\blank{0.8cm}$.
\emph{Not} polynomials (why?): $\dfrac{1}{x}=x^{-1}$ and $\sqrt{x}=x^{1/2}$ --- their exponents are
\blank{4.6cm}.
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{2. End Behavior: The Two-Question Rule}
The two \emph{arms} of a polynomial (what happens as $x\to-\infty$ and $x\to+\infty$) are decided by
only \textbf{two} questions: \emph{Is the degree even or odd?} and \emph{Is the leading coefficient
$+$ or $-$?}
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.1}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.32]
  \draw[->, charcoal] (-2,0)--(2,0); \draw[->, charcoal] (0,-0.6)--(0,4);
  \draw[very thick, forest, domain=-1.7:1.7, samples=40, smooth] plot (\x,{(\x)*(\x)});
\end{tikzpicture} &
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.32]
  \draw[->, charcoal] (-2,0)--(2,0); \draw[->, charcoal] (0,-4)--(0,0.6);
  \draw[very thick, forest, domain=-1.7:1.7, samples=40, smooth] plot (\x,{-1*(\x)*(\x)});
\end{tikzpicture} &
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.32]
  \draw[->, charcoal] (-2,0)--(2,0); \draw[->, charcoal] (0,-3.2)--(0,3.2);
  \draw[very thick, forest, domain=-1.45:1.45, samples=50, smooth] plot (\x,{(\x)*(\x)*(\x)});
\end{tikzpicture} &
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.32]
  \draw[->, charcoal] (-2,0)--(2,0); \draw[->, charcoal] (0,-3.2)--(0,3.2);
  \draw[very thick, forest, domain=-1.45:1.45, samples=50, smooth] plot (\x,{-1*(\x)*(\x)*(\x)});
\end{tikzpicture} \\[2pt]
{\scriptsize even, $a>0$} & {\scriptsize even, $a<0$} & {\scriptsize odd, $a>0$} & {\scriptsize odd, $a<0$} \\
{\scriptsize both arms \blank{0.9cm}} & {\scriptsize both arms \blank{0.9cm}} & {\scriptsize \blank{1.4cm}} & {\scriptsize \blank{1.4cm}} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Even degree} $\Rightarrow$ both arms go the \blank{2.0cm} way (like a parabola).
        \textbf{Odd degree} $\Rightarrow$ the arms go \blank{2.4cm} ways.
  \item The \textbf{sign of the leading coefficient} sets the \emph{right} arm: $a>0 \Rightarrow$
        right arm \blank{1.2cm}; \; $a<0 \Rightarrow$ right arm \blank{1.2cm}.
  \item So for $g(x)=x^3-3x+2$ (odd degree, $a>0$): as $x\to+\infty,\ g(x)\to\blank{1.0cm}$; \; as
        $x\to-\infty,\ g(x)\to\blank{1.0cm}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{3. Turning Points, Extrema, Zeros \& Multiplicity}
Read every feature off the anchor graph $g(x)=x^3-3x+2=(x-1)^2(x+2)$ (same curve as the Hook).
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.6]
  \lgrid{-3}{3}{-3}{5}
  \begin{scope}
    \clip (-3,-3) rectangle (3,5);
    \draw[very thick, forest, domain=-2.15:2.1, samples=100, smooth]
      plot (\x,{(\x)*(\x)*(\x) - 3*(\x) + 2});
  \end{scope}
  \fill[charcoal] (-1,4) circle (2.6pt) node[above right, font=\scriptsize]{$(-1,4)$ rel.\ max};
  \fill[charcoal] (1,0) circle (2.6pt) node[above right, font=\scriptsize]{$(1,0)$ rel.\ min};
  \fill[charcoal] (-2,0) circle (2.6pt) node[above left, font=\scriptsize]{$(-2,0)$};
  \fill[charcoal] (0,2) circle (2.6pt) node[right, font=\scriptsize]{$(0,2)$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Turning points:} each place the curve changes rise$\leftrightarrow$fall. This graph
        has \blank{0.8cm} turning points. (A degree-$n$ polynomial has \emph{at most} $n-1$; here
        $n=3$.)
  \item \textbf{Relative (local) extrema:} the outputs \emph{at} the turning points. Relative
        \textbf{maximum} at $(-1,\,\blank{0.6cm})$; relative \textbf{minimum} at
        $(\blank{0.6cm},\,0)$. ``Relative'' means highest/lowest \emph{near there}.
  \item \textbf{Absolute (global) extrema:} the highest/lowest output of the \emph{whole} function.
        Because both arms run off to $\pm\infty$, this polynomial has \blank{1.4cm} absolute
        maximum and \blank{1.4cm} absolute minimum.
  \item \textbf{$y$-intercept:} set $x=0$: the point $(0,\,\blank{0.7cm})$.
  \item \textbf{Zeros \& multiplicity:} the \emph{multiplicity} of a zero is the exponent of its
        factor. At $x=-2$ the factor $(x+2)$ has multiplicity~$1$ (\textbf{odd}), so the graph
        \blank{1.6cm} the axis. At $x=1$ the factor $(x-1)^2$ has multiplicity~$2$ (\textbf{even}),
        so the graph \blank{1.6cm} the axis (bounces). \emph{Odd crosses; even touches.}
  \item \textbf{Domain / range:} domain is \blank{2.6cm}; because the arms cover every height, the
        range is \blank{2.6cm}.
  \item \textbf{Increasing / decreasing:} increasing on \blank{3.6cm}; decreasing on \blank{2.0cm}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{notesbox}{4. Symmetry: Even vs.\ Odd}
Symmetry is tested with $f(-x)$. A polynomial with \emph{only even powers} is \textbf{even}; with
\emph{only odd powers}, \textbf{odd}; with a mix, \textbf{neither}.
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.46]
  % Even: y = x^4
  \begin{scope}
    \lgrid{-2}{2}{-1}{5}
    \draw[navy, dashed, semithick] (0,-1)--(0,5);
    \begin{scope}
      \clip (-2,-1) rectangle (2,5);
      \draw[very thick, forest, domain=-1.5:1.5, samples=60, smooth] plot (\x,{(\x)^4});
    \end{scope}
    \fill[charcoal] (-1,1) circle (2.6pt); \fill[charcoal] (1,1) circle (2.6pt);
    \node[charcoal, font=\scriptsize] at (0,-2){$y=x^4$ \; (even: $y$-axis mirror)};
  \end{scope}
  % Odd: y = x^3
  \begin{scope}[xshift=9cm]
    \lgrid{-2}{2}{-4}{4}
    \begin{scope}
      \clip (-2,-4) rectangle (2,4);
      \draw[very thick, forest, domain=-1.55:1.55, samples=60, smooth] plot (\x,{(\x)*(\x)*(\x)});
    \end{scope}
    \fill[charcoal] (1,1) circle (2.6pt); \fill[charcoal] (-1,-1) circle (2.6pt);
    \node[charcoal, font=\scriptsize] at (0,-5){$y=x^3$ \; (odd: $180^\circ$ about origin)};
  \end{scope}
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Even:} $f(-x)=f(x)$. For $y=x^4$: $(-1)^4=(1)^4=\blank{0.7cm}$, so the two sides
        mirror across the \blank{1.6cm}-axis.
  \item \textbf{Odd:} $f(-x)=-f(x)$. For $y=x^3$: $(-1)^3=\blank{0.8cm}$ while $(1)^3=1$ --- opposite
        outputs, so the graph maps onto itself under a $180^\circ$ turn about the \blank{1.6cm}.
  \item The anchor $g(x)=x^3-3x+2$ has \emph{both} an odd-power term and a constant, so it is
        \blank{1.8cm}.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

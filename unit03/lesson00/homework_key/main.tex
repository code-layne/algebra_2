      \item \textbf{End behavior:} as $x\to-\infty,\ f(x)\to\inblank{\blank{1.0cm}}{\gradedblank[id=q1c, type=short, points=1, answer={-inf}, check=text, display={$-\infty$}]{1.0cm-2pt}{10pt}}$; as

        and \emph{no} absolute minimum? Refer to what its two arms do.
        \gradedlines{2}{\gradedblank[id=q4, type=rubric, points=2, display={Its arms go in \emph{opposite} directions --- one to $+\infty$, the other to $-\infty$. \begin{extensionbox}
\textbf{Reading a model in context.} A start-up's monthly profit (in thousands of dollars) is
modeled by $P(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$ for $0 \le t \le 3$ months.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item Factor $P(t)$ completely and list its zeros with their multiplicities.
        \ans{$P(t)=t(t-3)^2$; zeros $t=0$ (multiplicity 1) and $t=3$ (multiplicity 2).}
  \item The graph \emph{touches} the $t$-axis at $t=3$ instead of crossing. What does that
        \emph{mean} for the company's profit at $t=3$?
        \ans{Profit reaches $\$0$ (breaks even) at month 3 but does not go negative --- the even multiplicity makes it touch and turn back up.}
  \item On $0\le t\le 3$, the greatest profit occurs at the relative maximum. Read it from the model
        ($P(1)$) and interpret it in context.
        \ansline{$P(1)=1-6+9=4$, so the greatest profit on this interval is $\$4{,}000$, reached in month $t=1$.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{extensionbox}

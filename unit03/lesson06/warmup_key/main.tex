\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Skills We'll Use Today}
Three things you already know. Today we count a polynomial's zeros, build a polynomial from its
zeros, and match it to its graph --- work quickly, then check with a neighbour.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{1. Multiply (Lesson 3.1 and Unit 2).} Remember that $i^2=-1$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=8pt]
  \item $(x+4)(x-1)=$ \ans{$x^2+3x-4$}
  \item $(x-3i)(x+3i)=$ \ans{$x^2-9i^2=x^2+9$}
\end{itemize}

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{2. Zeros and multiplicity (Lessons 3.4--3.5).} Let $f(x)=(x-4)(x+1)^2$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=8pt]
  \item The zeros of $f$ are $x=$ \ans{$4$} and $x=$ \ans{$-1$}.
  \item At $x=-1$ the graph \ans{touches} the $x$-axis (crosses / touches), because the
        multiplicity there is \ans{$2$}.
  \item $f$ has degree \ans{$3$}, so as $x\to\infty$, $f(x)\to$ \ans{$\infty$}.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{3. Find \emph{every} solution.} Solve $(x-2)(x^2+9)=0$. Set each factor equal to zero.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=8pt]
  \item From $x-2=0$: \quad $x=$ \ans{$2$}
  \item From $x^2+9=0$: \quad $x=$ \ans{$3i$ or $-3i$}
  \item How many solutions in all? \ans{$3$} \quad What is the degree of $(x-2)(x^2+9)$?
        \ans{$3$}
  \item Same number? \ans{yes} \; Coincidence --- or a rule? Hold that thought.
\end{itemize}
\end{notesbox}

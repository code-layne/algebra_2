\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=forestbg, colframe=forest, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students count a polynomial's zeros with the Fundamental Theorem
    of Algebra and split them into real and imaginary from the degree and a pre-drawn graph;
    write a polynomial from its zeros (supplying the conjugate, expanding, solving for $a$); and
    match a factored polynomial to its graph --- or write one from a graph --- defending each
    match with end behavior, cross/touch at each zero, and the $y$-intercept.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} \textbf{A2.EI.6a} --- determine a factored form of a
    polynomial equation from its zeros or the $x$-intercepts of its graph (Rounds 2--3);
    \textbf{A2.EI.6b} --- determine the number and type of solutions, real or imaginary, of a
    polynomial equation of degree three or higher (Round 1); \textbf{A2.F.2} --- graph
    characteristics: zeros and intercepts, end behavior, and the behavior at each zero (Round 3).
    The Remainder/Factor and Rational Root Theorems are cut from Unit 3 and are not taught.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Lesson model:} \emph{Packet first} (course shape of 2026-11-14). The student works in the
    packet --- \textbf{warm-up} on paper, then the \textbf{guided notes}: three rounds of a short
    \textbf{definition} and \textbf{example} ($\le$3 min), a \textbf{ladder} (\rung1\ \rung2\
    \rung3), and a \textbf{Show me} or \textbf{Find the mistake}, then the self-scored
    \textbf{Closing Practice}. The deck projects the same notes frame for frame; its 3-up
    handout is a work product, not in the packet. The graded, optional \textbf{Extra Credit}
    tears off the back. \textbf{Homework source:} DeltaMath online, set \emph{A2: Lesson 3.6:
    Zeros \& Graphs of Polynomials}.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Ideas \& Skills} & \textbf{Key Understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item State how many complex zeros a polynomial has from its degree, and split them into
        real (the $x$-intercepts, a touch counted twice) and imaginary (the rest).
  \item Name the conjugate of an imaginary zero and include it whenever the coefficients are
        real.
  \item Write $a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)\cdots$ from a list of zeros, multiply a conjugate pair to a real
        quadratic, expand to standard form, and solve for $a$ from a point.
  \item Match a factored polynomial to one of several pre-drawn graphs and defend it with a
        feature; write a least-degree equation from a graph.
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item The Fundamental Theorem \emph{counts} before anything is solved --- the warm-up's
        ``three solutions for degree three'' was a rule, not luck.
  \item A graph is an honest but incomplete witness: imaginary zeros never show, so the count
        is degree minus real.
  \item \textbf{Target misconception (the crux):} a zero at $x=3$ written as the factor
        $(x+3)$ --- the sign inside the factor is the \emph{opposite} of the zero. Its partner:
        treating a \emph{touch} as multiplicity $1$. Rounds 1--2 also flag \emph{forgetting
        the conjugate zero}.
  \item The $y$-intercept alone cannot catch a sign error inside a squared factor
        ($3^2=(-3)^2$); check every intercept.
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems --- one definition frame each}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Fundamental Theorem of Algebra} & A polynomial of degree $n\ge1$ has exactly $n$
  complex zeros, counted with multiplicity --- the real zeros and the imaginary zeros together. \\
\textbf{Conjugate pairs} & If a polynomial has real coefficients and $a+bi$ is a zero, then its
  conjugate $a-bi$ is also a zero, so imaginary zeros always come in pairs. \\
\textbf{Reading a graph (number and type)} & Each real zero is an $x$-intercept (a touch counts
  twice); imaginary zeros never show on the graph, so the number of imaginary zeros is the
  degree minus the number of real zeros. \\
\textbf{Factored form} & \TallMath{f(x)=a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)\cdots(x-r_n)}; each zero $r$ gives the
  factor $(x-r)$, $a$ is the leading coefficient, found by substituting one more point. \\
\textbf{Cross / touch} & An odd power crosses the $x$-axis; an even power touches it and turns
  back. \\
\textbf{End behavior; $y$-intercept} & From the degree and the sign of $a$; the $y$-intercept
  is $f(0)$. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Engagement --- how this hour keeps everyone solving]{goldbox}
\textbf{Nobody waits:} every round's practice is a ladder --- \rung1\ everyone (a quick win),
\rung2\ everyone, \rung3\ early finishers. \textbf{Everyone commits, safely:} \emph{Show me} is
1--4 fingers on the count of three, all at once (Rounds 1 and 2). \textbf{Critique a stranger:}
Round 3's \emph{Find the mistake} puts the crux error on an anonymous Student A.
\textbf{Their world:} the dorm common-room puzzle board (Round 2 \rung3), the Math Night
scavenger-hunt clue (Closing Practice bonus), and --- in the extra credit --- the cross-country
team's course profile. \textbf{Short clock:} every practice frame carries its minutes. Praise the
climb more than the answer.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Warm-Up (5 min, on paper)]{forestbg}
\textbf{1.} $(x+4)(x-1)=x^2+3x-4$ and $(x-3i)(x+3i)=x^2-9i^2=x^2+9$ --- 3.1 multiplication and
the Unit 2 fact $i^2=-1$; the second product is Round 2's conjugate-pair quadratic.
\textbf{2.} $f(x)=(x-4)(x+1)^2$: zeros $4$ and $-1$; touches at $-1$ (multiplicity $2$); degree
$3$, $f(x)\to\infty$ --- 3.4--3.5 spiral, and Round 3's reading moves. \textbf{3.} $(x-2)(x^2+9)=0$
gives $2,\ 3i,\ -3i$: three solutions for degree $3$. \textbf{Hand-off:} ``Coincidence, or a
rule?'' --- Round 1's definition answers it.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[The Lesson --- three rounds and Closing Practice (45 min)]{forestbg}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{Round 1 --- Counting zeros: FTA \& conjugate pairs (13 min).} Read the three definition
blocks in one breath; point at ``counted with multiplicity'' and ``never show on the graph.''
Example: $x^4-x^3+2x^2-4x-8$ --- $4$ zeros, $2$ real (crosses at $-1$, $2$), $2$ imaginary,
$\pm2i$. Ladder: \rung1\ $6$ zeros; $4+5i$. \rung2\ $1$ real, $4$ imaginary. \rung3\ $3$ real
(touch at $-2$ counts twice), $2$ imaginary --- the slip is counting the touch once and getting
$3$ imaginary, an odd number, which is itself the alarm. \emph{Show me} (cubic, zeros $4$ and
$2+i$): answer \textbf{2} ($2-i$). Fingers on 1 paired a real zero; 3 flipped both signs;
4 forgot the conjugate.

\textbf{Round 2 --- Writing a polynomial from its zeros (14 min).} Point at ``a zero at $x=3$
gives $(x-3)$'' --- the crux, planted early. Example: zeros $3$, $2i$, through $(0,24)$ $\Rightarrow$
$-2x^3+6x^2-8x+24$; say aloud why $-2i$ joins. Ladder: \rung1\ $x^2+3x-10$. \rung2\
$x^3-x^2+9x-9$. \rung3\ (dorm puzzle) $a=-2$, $-2x^3+4x^2+14x+8$. \emph{Show me} (zeros $2$,
$1+i$): answer \textbf{3} $(x-2)(x^2-2x+2)$. 1 = forgot the conjugate; 2 = $(x+2)$, the crux
sign error; 4 = middle-term sign.

\columnbreak
\textbf{Round 3 --- Graphs from zeros \& end behavior: the crux (12 min).} Graphs A--D differ
only in the sign of $a$ and the signs inside the factors --- they force students to read every
feature. Example: $-(x+2)(x-1)^2$ is \textbf{A} (crosses $-2$, touches $1$, rises left, $f(0)=-2$).
Ladder: \rung1\ $-4$ crosses, $3$ touches, falls left/rises right, $k(0)=72$. \rung2\ \textbf{D}.
\rung3\ $\tfrac12(x+3)(x-2)^2$. \textbf{Find the mistake (the crux --- do not rush it):} Student A
wrote $(x+1)(x+3)^2$ for a graph touching at $3$; fix is $(x+1)(x-3)^2$. Make the class notice that
her $y$-intercept check \emph{passed} --- $3^2=(-3)^2$ --- so ``check the intercepts, not just
$f(0)$.''

\textbf{Closing Practice (6 min).} 1.\ $5i,-5i,1-2i,1+2i$ (Round 1). 2.\ $x^3+2x^2+16x+32$
(Round 2). 3.\ $5$ crosses, $-2$ touches, $g(0)=20$ (Round 3). 4.\ $-(x+4)(x-1)^2$ --- the
crux's twin. Bonus (Math Night): $-2x^3+8x^2-12x+8$. Say the \emph{Watch out} aloud: a zero at
$3$ is $(x-3)$; a touch is squared; an imaginary zero brings its conjugate. Scores on fingers.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Extra Credit --- optional, graded (alg2-u03-l06-ec, 14 points)]{goldbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{1 (combine, Rounds 1+2; 2 pt).} Leading coefficient $1$, degree $4$, double zero
        $3$ and $1-2i$: $(x^2-6x+9)(x^2-2x+5)=x^4-8x^3+26x^2-48x+45$. Stretches: a double zero
        \emph{and} a conjugate in one build.
  \item \textbf{2 (reverse, Rounds 3+1).} A pre-drawn quartic touching at $-1$, crossing at $2$
        and $4$, $y$-intercept $-4$: (a) $-\tfrac12(x+1)^2(x-2)(x-4)$ (2 pt) --- a fractional
        $a$; (b) $0$ imaginary zeros (1 pt).
  \item \textbf{3 (SOL multiple choice; 1 pt).} Degree-4, real coefficients --- what must be
        true? \textbf{B} (conjugate pairs). A = all real; C = odd-degree reasoning on an even
        degree; D = an odd count of imaginary zeros.
  \item \textbf{4 (model; 5 pt).} Cross-country course, zeros $0,2,6$, $h(1)=5$: (a)
        $x(x-2)(x-6)$, (b) $h(4)=-16$, (c) rubric (2): $16$ m below the start at $400$ m; below
        the start from $200$ m to $600$ m. Stretches: interpreting a zero-built model.
  \item \textbf{5 (Find the mistake; rubric, 3 pt).} Student A gave a touch multiplicity $1$:
        $2(x+2)(x-3)$; correct $(x+2)^2(x-3)$ ($a=1$). The crux's partner misconception.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring --- Watch For]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{The crux:} a zero $r$ written as $(x+r)$ --- Round 2 Show me option 2, Round 3
        Find the mistake, Closing Practice 4. Ask: ``what $x$ makes that factor zero?''
  \item \textbf{A touch counted once:} Round 1 \rung3\ (``$3$ imaginary'' --- odd, impossible),
        Round 3 \rung3\ and Closing Practice 4 written with $(x-2)$ or $(x-1)$ unsquared.
  \item \textbf{Forgotten conjugate:} Round 1 Show me option 4, Round 2 \rung2\ stopping at
        $(x-1)(x+3i)$ --- have her multiply it out and find the $i$ left in the coefficients.
  \item \textbf{$a$ skipped:} Round 2 \rung3\ and Round 3 \rung3\ left with $a=1$; point at
        the $y$-intercept on the graph.
  \item \textbf{Attitude:} a student parked at \rung1\ with nothing written --- crouch, point at
        the example in her notes, ask for just the first step.
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts (after the fingers, never before): ``How many zeros before you solve
anything?'' \; ``Which number makes $(x+3)$ zero?'' \; ``How did Student A's check pass?''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
Five minutes, on paper, as they walk in; the deck's warm-up frame reveals all three at 3
minutes. Item 1's second product is the one to name aloud --- ``an $i$ pair multiplies to a real
quadratic'' is Round 2's engine. Do not explain item 3; leave ``coincidence or a rule?'' hanging
for Round 1.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes \& Practice]
Rounds of about 13 / 14 / 12 minutes, then 6 for the Closing Practice. Definition + example
\textbf{at most 3 minutes} a round (Round 2's example is the longest --- keep its six lines moving;
the notes have every step). Reveal each ladder when most have \rung2. If behind, shorten Round 1's
ladder to \rung1--\rung2\ and Round 2's to \rung1--\rung2; \textbf{never} cut Round 3's Find the
mistake or the Closing Practice. Students write in their notes; the key packet has every work
block filled.
\end{teachernote}

skills to be chosen when the set is built with the teacher. The last 10 minutes are for
\emph{starting} it --- launch problem~1 aloud, then circulate, checking factors' signs first. Due
the first class after two study halls.
\end{teachernote}

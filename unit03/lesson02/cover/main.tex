\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item \ldots{} factor out the \textbf{greatest common factor (GCF)} --- the largest number and
        the \emph{lowest} power of each shared variable --- including a \emph{negative} GCF, and
        factor a four-term polynomial by \textbf{grouping}.
  \item \ldots{} factor a \textbf{sum of cubes} and a \textbf{difference of cubes} with the
        identities $a^3\pm b^3=(a\pm b)(a^2\mp ab+b^2)$, getting the signs right with
        \textbf{SOAP}.
  \item \ldots{} \textbf{factor completely}: GCF first, then a pattern (squares, cubes, a
        trinomial, or \textbf{quadratic form}), then check every factor again --- and recognize a
        \textbf{prime} factor such as $x^2+4$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\boxguard[8]
\begin{remindbox}
\small
The \textbf{GCF} uses the \emph{lowest} power of each shared variable, and a negative GCF flips
the sign of every term inside. \textbf{Grouping} works on four terms: GCF each pair, then factor
out the shared binomial. Cubes follow \textbf{SOAP}: $a^3-b^3=(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)$ --- the middle
term is $ab$, never $2ab$. \textbf{Factored completely} means no factor can be factored again:
after every step, check every factor --- $x^4-16=(x^2+4)(x^2-4)$ is \emph{not} finished, because
$x^2-4=(x+2)(x-2)$; but $x^2+4$, a sum of squares, is \textbf{prime} and stays.
\end{remindbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=forestbg, colframe=forest, boxrule=0.9pt, arc=2mm,
    left=2mm, right=2mm, top=1.5mm, bottom=1.5mm]
    \textbf{Primary Objective:} Students factor polynomials completely over the integers, in one
    and two variables: they take out the greatest common factor (including a negative one) and
    factor four terms by grouping; factor a sum or difference of cubes with the identities
    $a^3\pm b^3=(a\pm b)(a^2\mp ab+b^2)$; and chain the moves --- GCF, then a pattern (squares,
    cubes, quadratic form), then every factor checked again --- recognizing a prime factor such as
    $x^2+4$. Unit 3's merged-rounds exception: three skills, climbing.
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Standards (2023 VA SOL):} \textbf{A2.EO.3b} (factor polynomials completely in one or
    two variables, no more than four terms, over the integers); \textbf{A2.EO.3d} (verify
    polynomial identities --- the sum and difference of cubes, checked by multiplying back, and
    the difference of squares).
    \\[2pt]
    \textbf{Lesson model:} \emph{Packet first} (§0a). Students work on paper the whole hour: the
    warm-up page as they walk in; three rounds in the Guided Notes, each a short
    \textbf{definition} and \textbf{example} ($\le$3 min), a \textbf{ladder} (\rung1\ \rung2\
    \rung3), and a \textbf{Show me} or \textbf{Find the mistake}; the self-scored
    \textbf{Closing Practice}; and the homework started in class. The deck projects the notes
    frame for frame. Graded, optional \textbf{Extra Credit} tears off the back.
    \textbf{Homework source:} DeltaMath, online --- set \textbf{A2: Lesson 3.2: Factoring
    Polynomials} (not in the packet).
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Priority Ideas \& Skills]{goldbox}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X|X@{}}
\textbf{Priority Ideas \& Skills} & \textbf{Key Understandings (the ``why'')} \\[2pt]
\hline
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Factor out the GCF --- largest number, \emph{lowest} power of each shared variable ---
        and a negative GCF when the lead is negative.
  \item Factor four terms by grouping, taking a negative out of the second pair when the
        binomials must match.
  \item Factor $a^3\pm b^3$: name $a$ and $b$ first, then SOAP.
  \item Factor completely: GCF, count the terms, then check every factor; factor
        $x^4+bx^2+c$ in quadratic form; apply the difference of squares twice.
\end{itemize}
&
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em, nosep, after=\vspace{2pt}]
  \item Factoring is multiplying run backward --- every answer checks by multiplying out
        (A2.EO.3d).
  \item The GCF can only use the lowest power, because a higher power does not divide the
        smaller term.
  \item In a cube identity four of the six products cancel, so the middle term is $ab$, not
        $2ab$. \textbf{Secondary misconception:} writing the trinomial factor as $a^2+2ab+b^2$.
  \item \textbf{Target misconception: stopping early.} Students write
        $x^4-16=(x^2+4)(x^2-4)$ and call it done --- $x^2-4$ still factors --- or ``finish'' by
        breaking the prime $x^2+4$ into $(x+2)^2$ or $(x+2)(x-2)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[{Vocabulary, Concepts, \& Theorems --- one definition frame each}]{sky}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\textbf{Greatest common factor (GCF)} & The largest number and the \emph{lowest} power of each
                          shared variable that divide every term; a negative GCF changes the sign
                          of every term left inside. \\
\textbf{Factoring by grouping} & Split four terms into two pairs, take the GCF out of each pair,
                          then factor out the binomial the pairs share. \\
\textbf{Sum / difference of cubes} & \TallMath{a^3+b^3=(a+b)(a^2-ab+b^2),\quad a^3-b^3=(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)} \\
\textbf{SOAP} & \textbf{S}ame sign in the binomial, \textbf{O}pposite sign on the middle term,
                \textbf{A}lways \textbf{P}ositive last term. \\
\textbf{Factored completely} & A product in which no factor can be factored again over the
                          integers. \\
\textbf{Prime} & A factor that cannot be factored; a sum of two squares such as $x^2+4$ is
                 always prime. \\
\textbf{Quadratic form} & $x^4+bx^2+c$, factored as though $x^2$ were the variable. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Engagement --- how this hour keeps everyone solving]{goldbox}
\textbf{Nobody waits:} every ladder runs \rung1\ everyone (a quick win), \rung2\ everyone,
\rung3\ early finishers. \textbf{Climbing skills:} Round 1 is mostly review (the GCF), so the
first ten minutes are wins; each round leans on the last --- Round 3 uses the GCF and the cube
identities again. \textbf{Everyone commits, safely:} Rounds 1 and 2 end on \emph{Show me}, 1--4
fingers on the count of three, all at once. \textbf{Critique a stranger:} Round 3's \emph{Find
the mistake} puts the crux error on an anonymous Student A. \textbf{Their world:} the art
studio's clay cube (Round 2 \rung3) and the garden club's raised bed (the Closing Practice
bonus); the Extra Credit transfers to the riding program's hay bin. \textbf{Short clock:} every
practice frame carries its minutes. Praise the climb more than the answer.
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Warm-Up (5 min, on paper as they walk in)]{forestbg}
Three quick wins on the warm-up page; the deck reveals the answers in one click.
\textbf{(1)} $x^2+7x+10=(x+2)(x+5)$, $x^2-x-12=(x-4)(x+3)$ --- Unit 2 trinomials, needed again
in Round 3's quadratic form. \textbf{(2)} $x^2-49=(x+7)(x-7)$, $4x^2-25=(2x+5)(2x-5)$, and
$x^2+49$ does \emph{not} factor --- the seed of the crux (a sum of squares is prime).
\textbf{(3)} The cubes $1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 8x^3, 27y^3$ (Round 2 is unusable without them) and the
largest monomial dividing $12x^3$ and $18x^5$, $6x^3$ --- the \emph{lowest} power. The reveal
ends on \emph{Hold on to this: why the lowest?} Round 1's definition answers it. Then the
targets frame (30 seconds).
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[The Lesson --- three rounds and Closing Practice (45 min)]{forestbg}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\textbf{Round 1 --- GCF and grouping (13 min).} Definition in 1 minute: point at the side
display --- $18x^5=6x^3\cdot3x^2$, and $x^5$ cannot divide $x^3$. Example 1: (a)
$-8x^5+12x^4-20x^3=-4x^3(2x^2-3x+5)$ --- ask what each sign inside becomes before writing it;
(b) $2x^3-6x^2-5x+15=2x^2(x-3)-5(x-3)=(x-3)(2x^2-5)$ --- stop on why it is $-5$, not $5$.
Ladder (7 min): \rung1\ $6x^2y^2(2x^2-3y)$ (watch $6x^4y^3$: the highest powers);
\rung2\ $-5x(x^2-2x+3)$ (watch $-5x(x^2+2x-3)$: copied signs); \rung3\
$(2x+3)(x^2-5)$ (watch $x^2(2x+3)+5(2x+3)$ with mismatched signs). Show me $-4x^3+8x^2-12x$:
answer \textbf{1}, $-4x(x^2-2x+3)$; \textbf{2} = signs not flipped; \textbf{3} = missed the
$x$; \textbf{4} = not the greatest number.

\textbf{Round 2 --- sum and difference of cubes (13 min).} Definition in 1 minute: read SOAP
off the two identities, and say ``$ab$, never $2ab$.'' Example 2: (a) $x^3+8=(x+2)(x^2-2x+4)$,
then multiply back on the frame --- four of six terms cancel (A2.EO.3d); (b)
$27x^3-64y^3=(3x-4y)(9x^2+12xy+16y^2)$ --- name $a=3x$, $b=4y$ out loud first. Ladder (7 min):
\rung1\ $(x-5)(x^2+5x+25)$; \rung2\ $(2x+3)(4x^2-6x+9)$ (watch $4x^2-12x+9$: the $2ab$ slip);
\rung3\ clay cube, $(4x-5)(16x^2+20x+25)$ (watch $a=64x$: the cube root of the coefficient is
$4$). Show me $x^3-27$: answer \textbf{3}, $(x-3)(x^2+3x+9)$; \textbf{1} = middle sign not
opposite; \textbf{2} = $2ab$ (the perfect-square pattern --- the secondary misconception);
\textbf{4} = cubed the binomial.

\columnbreak
\textbf{Round 3 --- factoring completely: the crux (13 min).} Definition in 1 minute: the
order --- GCF, count the terms, check every factor --- and ``a sum of two squares is prime.''
Example 3: (a) $2x^4-162=2(x^2+9)(x+3)(x-3)$ --- pause after $2(x^2+9)(x^2-9)$ and ask ``done?'';
(b) $x^4-13x^2+36=(x^2-4)(x^2-9)=(x+2)(x-2)(x+3)(x-3)$ --- treat $x^2$ as the variable, then
check each factor. Ladder (7 min): \rung1\ $5(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)$ (watch $5(x^3-8)$, stopped);
\rung2\ $(x+1)(x-1)(x+2)(x-2)$ (watch $(x^2-1)(x^2-4)$, stopped); \rung3\
$(x+2)(x^2-2x+4)(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)$. \textbf{Find the mistake --- the frame not to rush:} Student
A wrote $x^4-16=(x^2+4)(x^2-4)$, ``two factors, so I'm done.'' Give 2 minutes on paper; ask
``what would you tell Student A?'' before revealing. Fix: $x^2-4=(x+2)(x-2)$, so
$(x^2+4)(x+2)(x-2)$ --- and $x^2+4$ stays: it is prime, not $(x+2)^2$.

\textbf{Closing Practice (6 min).} Alone, one point each: $(x-6)(x^2+2)$;
$(5x+2)(25x^2-10x+4)$; $(x^2+9)(x+3)(x-3)$ (the crux's twin --- $(x^2+9)(x^2-9)$ scores 0);
$3x(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)$. \textbf{Bonus:} the raised bed, $(x+3)(x+2)(x-2)$, and at $x=5$ the
dimensions $8$, $7$, $3$ ft. Collect scores on fingers --- the exit read. Say the \emph{Watch
out} aloud: after every step, check every factor; $x^2-4$ keeps going, $x^2+4$ is prime.
\end{multicols}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Extra Credit --- optional, graded (alg2-u03-l02-ec, 16 points)]{goldbox}
Two pages at the back of the packet; they tear off and are turned in with the homework.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{1. Combine Rounds 1 and 3} (2 pts): $x^5+2x^4-16x-32=(x+2)(x^4-16)
        =(x+2)^2(x-2)(x^2+4)$ --- grouping, then the difference of squares twice.
  \item \textbf{2. Combine Rounds 2 and 3} (2 pts): $x^6-7x^3-8=(x^3-8)(x^3+1)
        =(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)(x+1)(x^2-x+1)$ --- quadratic form in $x^3$, then both cube identities.
  \item \textbf{3. Find the mistake} (rubric, 3 pts): Student A wrote
        $8x^3-27=(2x-3)(4x^2+12x+9)$ using $2ab$; correct $(2x-3)(4x^2+6x+9)$ --- the secondary
        misconception, written out.
  \item \textbf{4. Reverse} (2 + 1 pts): $-2x(x-3)(x^2+3x+9)=-2x^4+54x$ (recognize the
        cube identity in reverse); $k=125$ in $8x^3+k=(2x+5)(4x^2-10x+25)$.
  \item \textbf{5. Model} (2 + 1 + 2 pts): the riding program's hay bin,
        $V=x^3-2x^2-25x+50=(x-2)(x-5)(x+5)$; at $x=8$ the bin is $6$, $3$, $13$ ft; it exists
        only for $x>5$, because $x-5$ is a length (rubric).
  \item \textbf{6. SOL-style multiple choice} (1 pt): the complete factorization of $3x^4-48$
        is \textbf{C}, $3(x^2+4)(x+2)(x-2)$; A stops at the GCF, B is the crux (stopped early),
        D breaks the prime $x^2+4$.
\end{itemize}
\end{skillbox}

\begin{skillbox}[Active Monitoring --- Watch For]{redbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{Wrong GCF} (warm-up 3, Round 1 \rung1, Show me \textbf{3}--\textbf{4}): $6x^4y^3$ (highest powers) or a GCF that is not the greatest ---
        ask ``does $x^4$ divide $x^2$?''
  \item \textbf{Signs left unflipped under a negative GCF} (Round 1 \rung2, Show me
        \textbf{2}): have her distribute back.
  \item \textbf{Mismatched binomials in grouping} (Round 1 \rung3): $(2x+3)$ and $(-2x-3)$ ---
        take the negative out of the second pair.
  \item \textbf{$2ab$ in the cube trinomial} (Round 2 \rung2, Show me \textbf{2}, Extra Credit
        3): $x^2+6x+9$ is a perfect square; the cube identity's middle is $ab$.
  \item \textbf{Stopping early --- the crux} (Round 3 \rung1--\rung2, Find the mistake,
        Closing Practice 3, Extra Credit 6\,B): $(x^2+4)(x^2-4)$ left as is. Point at
        $x^2-4$ and ask ``is this a pattern you know?''
  \item \textbf{Breaking a prime} (Round 3, Extra Credit 6\,D): $x^2+4=(x+2)^2$. Multiply
        $(x+2)^2$ back: $x^2+4x+4$.
  \item \textbf{Attitude:} a student parked at \rung1\ with nothing written --- crouch, point at
        the example in her notes, ask for just the first step (``what do all the terms
        share?'').
\end{itemize}
Cold-call prompts (after the fingers, never before): ``Why does the GCF use the lowest power?''
``What does SOAP stand for, and which letter did option 1 break?'' ``How do you know you are
finished factoring?''
\end{skillbox}

\begin{teachernote}[Warm-Up]
On the desks as they walk in; the deck shows the same three items. Reveal at about 4 minutes,
one click. Item~2's last question ($x^2+49$, no) is the seed of the crux --- say ``remember
this'' and move on. Item~3's cubes take ninety seconds but Round~2 is unusable without them; if
the room is slow on them, leave the reveal up while Round~2 starts. End on the hanging question
--- why the \emph{lowest} power? --- and go straight to Round~1.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Guided Notes \& Practice]
Three rounds of 13 minutes, then 6 for the Closing Practice. Definition + example \textbf{at
most 3 minutes} a round --- students copy the example's steps into its work space as you click
through; explain further at desks, not at the board. Reveal a ladder when most have \rung2.
Round~1 is the cushion: if behind, cut Round~1's \rung3\ (grouping recurs in the Closing
Practice and the bonus) --- never Round~3's Find the mistake. In Round~2, say $a$ and $b$ aloud
before every cube problem. In Round~3, ask ``done?'' after every line of the example; that one
word is the lesson. The Closing Practice is alone and silent; scores on fingers are the exit
read --- a room of 2s and 3s usually lost item~3 (the crux's twin).
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Homework]
DeltaMath set \textbf{A2: Lesson 3.2: Factoring Polynomials}, online; its skills are chosen with
the teacher when the set is built. The last 10 minutes are for \emph{starting} it in class ---
launch problem~1 aloud, then circulate, looking first at whoever scored 2 or below on the

announced in class and on TurtleNet.
\end{teachernote}

\begin{teachernote}[Extra Credit]
Optional and graded (\texttt{alg2-u03-l02-ec}, 16 points, two rubric items with YAMLs in
\texttt{rubrics/}). Every item is harder than every \rung3: items 1--2 chain two rounds, item~3
is the $2ab$ error in prose, item~4 runs the identities backward, item~5 transfers to a new
context with an interpretation, item~6 is the crux as an SOL-style item. Only what is inside a
box is graded; the pages carry no name --- the cover does. Mention it once, at the homework
frame; do not teach to it in class.
\end{teachernote}

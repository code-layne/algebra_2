\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Skills We'll Use Today}
Three things you already know. Last lesson you multiplied polynomials; today you run the machine
\emph{backward} and factor them --- work quickly, then check with a neighbour.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{1. Factor the trinomial (Unit 2).} Find two numbers with the right product and sum.

\vspace{4pt}
\quad $x^2+7x+10=$ \ans{$(x+2)(x+5)$} \qquad $x^2-x-12=$ \ans{$(x-4)(x+3)$}

\vspace{12pt}
\textbf{2. Difference of squares (Unit 2).} $a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$.

\vspace{4pt}
\quad $x^2-49=$ \ans{$(x+7)(x-7)$} \qquad $4x^2-25=$ \ans{$(2x+5)(2x-5)$}

\vspace{4pt}
\quad Does $x^2+49$ factor the same way? \ans{no} (yes / no)

\vspace{12pt}
\textbf{3. Perfect cubes and common factors.} Fill in the cubes --- you will need them on sight
today.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$n$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ & $5$ & $2x$ & $3y$ \\ \hline
$n^3$ & \ans{1} & \ans{8} & \ans{27} & \ans{64} & \ans{125} &
        \ans{$8x^3$} & \ans{$27y^3$} \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both $12x^3$ and $18x^5$ are divisible by $6$. What is the \emph{largest} power of $x$ that
divides \emph{both} of them? \ans{$x^3$}

\vspace{4pt}
So the largest monomial that divides both is \ans{$6x^3$}. \; Is the power you used the
\emph{highest} or the \emph{lowest} power in sight? \ans{lowest}
\end{notesbox}

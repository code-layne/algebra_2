\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Skills We'll Use Today}
\small Lesson 3.2 ended at a wall: $x^3 - 3x + 2$ factors, but none of our tools could crack it.
Today's tool is \emph{division}. These four skills are what it takes.

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{1. Long division with numbers.} Divide $247$ by $5$.

\vspace{2pt}
Quotient: \ans{49} \qquad Remainder: \ans{2}

\vspace{3pt}
Now write the answer two ways: \; $247 = 5 \cdot$ \ans{49} $+$ \ans{2}
\qquad and \qquad $\dfrac{247}{5} = $ \ans{49} $+ \dfrac{{\color{keyred}\mathbf{2}}}{5}$

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{2. Dividing monomials} (exponent rules from Algebra 1). Simplify.

\vspace{2pt}
$\dfrac{12x^5}{4x^2} = $ \ans{$3x^3$} \qquad
$\dfrac{-20x^4y^3}{5x^2y^3} = $ \ans{$-4x^2$} \qquad
$\dfrac{6x^2}{6x^2} = $ \ans{$1$}

\vspace{4pt}
And split a sum over its denominator: \; $\dfrac{8x^3 + 12x^2}{4x} = $ \ans{$2x^2 + 3x$}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{3. Evaluating a polynomial.} Let $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 2$. Find each value.

\vspace{2pt}
$f(1) = $ \ans{$0$} \qquad $f(-2) = $ \ans{$0$} \qquad $f(0) = $ \ans{$2$} \qquad
$f(3) = $ \ans{$20$}

\vspace{3pt}
Two of those came out $0$. On the graph of $f$ you read in Lesson 3.0, what are those two inputs
called? \ans{the zeros ($x$-intercepts)}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{4. Multiply back --- and watch closely.} Expand and collect like terms.

\vspace{2pt}
$(x - 1)(x^2 + x - 2) = $ \ans{$x^3 - 3x + 2$}

\vspace{3pt}
Compare your answer with the $f(x)$ in item 3. What did you just show about $(x-1)$?
\ans{that it is a factor of $f$}

\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
This Warm-Up is built so that today's two theorems are already on the page before they are named.
Item~1 is the whole lesson in miniature: $247 = 5\cdot 49 + 2$ is the division statement
$P = D\cdot Q + R$ that governs \emph{every} polynomial division we do today --- keep it on the board.
Item~2's third answer is the one to debrief: $\frac{6x^2}{6x^2}=1$, not $0$, and that dropped
constant term is the most common monomial-division error of the week.

\vspace{3pt}
Items~3 and 4 are the Factor Theorem, discovered rather than announced. Item~3 gives $f(1)=0$ and
$f(-2)=0$; item~4 shows $(x-1)$ multiplies out to exactly that $f$. Ask: \textbf{``$f(1)=0$ and
$(x-1)$ is a factor --- coincidence?''} Hold the question open until the Factor Theorem section of
the notes. Item~4 also hands students the quotient they will \emph{produce} by division ten minutes
later, so when the long division of $x^3-3x+2$ by $x-1$ returns $x^2+x-2$, they will recognize it.
\end{teachernote}
